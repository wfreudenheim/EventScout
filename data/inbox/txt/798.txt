UID:798 | 2026-10-14 | "Screen Slate Presents" <jon@screenslate.com> | Psycho-Fiction: The Surreal Nightmares of Screaming Mad George

The late surrealistic special effects artist is celebrated in a retrospective full of 35mm prints.

SERIES & SCREENING INFO

This year we lost one of the great special effects artists, Screaming Mad George. Once seen, SMG’s work is forever seared in the retina. Taking his name from Screaming Jay Hawkins and MAD Magazine, his creative sensibility is a mashup of the former’s theatrical rock ‘n’ roll histrionics and the latter’s cartoonish satire, heavily infused with the biomorphic distortions of Salvador Dalí. Whether creating a discrete sequence for a blockbuster like the cockroach transformation in A Nightmare on Elm Street 4, or taking center stage in the work of his longtime collaborator Brian Yuzna—especially in their magnum opus Society—Screaming Mad George’s effects represent a singular artistic vision that viewers could always sense, even if they didn’t always know the name behind the nightmares.

Beyond his feature film special effects work, Screaming Mad George’s creative output extended to No Wave, downtown NYC horrorpunk, film directing, sculpture, video games, and work for other musical artists ranging from Slipknot to *NSYNC. For this series, Screen Slate brings together a selection of Screaming Mad George’s iconic feature films and some of our favorite deep cuts, all programmed alongside short films, clip reels, musical performances, rarities, and, of course, oddities. Many of the films will screen in NYC in 35mm for the first time ever, including Yuzna’s personal prints, and several will be accompanied by special guests.

Programmed by Screen Slate’s Jon Dieringer. Flyer by Steak Mtn.

SOCIETY
Brian Yuzna, 1989, 99 min, Director's personal 35mm print!

Friday, October 23 - 4:30PM
Friday, October 30 - 7:00PM

Tickets

Brian Yuzna’s tangle of class warfare, scatalogy and ugly sex is a stupendously unsubtle eat-the-rich satire. The film’s central metaphor, which suits the 21st century even better than it did the Reagan-Bush 80s, presents the wealthy as literally inhuman gluttons. Since time immemorial, this cupidinous race has parasitically feasted on the inferior homo sapiens during “shuntings,” Burroughsian nightmares of viscera, gobs of lube and denatured flesh. The mutations are the greatest showcase of the unnerving surrealistic makeup designs of Screaming Mad George—which went on to be the primary influence on the make-up designs of films like The Substance and Zach Cregger’s Resident Evil.

FREAKED
Tom Stern & Alex Winter, 1993, 80 min, 35mm print

Saturday, October 24 - 3:00PM
Sunday, November 1 - 3:00PM

Tickets

One of the quintessential early-’90s cult films, Freaked was adapted by Alex Winter and Tom Stern from their psychotronic MTV series The Idiot Box. Winter plays a vain Hollywood actor who takes a gig promoting an evil South African fertilizer company. But an airplane incident lands him in the middle of a grotesque amusement park, where he is transformed into a member of its expansive freak show. A deliriously grotesque live-action cartoon, Freaked is a carnivalesque melange of eye-popping make-up effects and fun-house set design. It also features an all-star cameo casting ranging from Gibby Haynes of The Butthole Surfers to Mr. T, plus masked appearances from Keanu Reeves and future Wicked director Jon M. Chu. Must be seen to be believed.
 

Preceded by a special video musical performance!

THE GUYVER
Screaming Mad George & Steve Wang, 1993, 80 min, DCP

Saturday, October 24 - 5:00PM

Tickets

Screaming Mad George’s sole directorial feature is a tokusatsu film about a guy who finds an alien suit that turns him into a superpowered cyber-bug. This puts him on a collision course against a shadowy mega-corp called Chronos and a slimy, reptilian race known as the Zoanoids, who get mad and kidnap his girlfriend. With the help of rogue CIA agent Mark Hamill, it’s up to The Guyver to rescue the girl, take down the man, and save the planet. Co-directed with adrenaline junkie Steve Wang (1997’s Drive), The Guyver is a fantastic showcase for SMG’s icky creature effects.
 

Preceded by a special short film!

A NIGHTMARE ON ELM STREET 4: THE DREAM MASTER
Renny Harlin, 1988, 93 min, 35mm

Tuesday, October 27 - 9:30PM
Wednesday, October 28 - 9:30PM

Tickets

Freddy Krueger’s popularity was at his peak for A Nightmare on Elm Street‘s fourth installment, which more or less abandons any pretext that it is not treating the wisecracking undead childmolester as the hero. Often referred to as “the MTV entry” in the series, Nightmare 4 represents the absolute zenith of pre-CGI, practical horror effects in a big-budget popcorn spectacle. Screaming Mad George is the mastermind behind the surreal cockroach transformation sequence, which wizened gorehounds often rank among the series’ very best kills.

SILENT NIGHT, DEADLY NIGHT 5: THE TOY MAKER
Martin Kitrosser, 1991, 86 min, Producer Brian Yuzna's personal 35mm print

Curator's choice! First-ever 35mm NYC screening! Insane movie!

Saturday, October 31 - 3:00PM
Sunday, November 1 - 7:15PM

Tickets

The series curator’s dark horse pick for must-see movie in the Screaming Mad George retrospective, the late entry from the direct-to-video years of the long, byzantine Christmas series is shockingly entertaining. The story involves a young boy who is traumatized when bizarre mystery toys kill people including his deadbeat father, his school bully, and a landlord (yes!) in strange and gruesome ways. This puts him in touch with a toy seller named Joe Petto—owner of “Petto’s Toys” (say it out loud)—played by a typically unhinged late-career Mickey Rooney. And from there, the story takes a twist into being a… Terminator knock-off? To the best of our knowledge, Silent Night, Deadly Night 5: The Toy Maker has never screened theatrically in New York City on 35mm.

Preceded by two special music videos!

BIG TROUBLE IN LITTLE CHINA
John Carpenter, 1986, 99 min, 35mm

Tuesday, November 3 - 8:30PM
Wednesday, November 4 - 9:30PM

Tickets

Kurt Russell is Jack Burton, an independent trucker piloting the Pork Chop Express through the streets of San Francisco’s Chinatown. When a new friend’s fiancée is kidnapped, the pair find themselves thrust into the middle of a supernatural gang war that pits them against dueling sorcerers, freakish fiends, and black magic. Big Trouble in Little China combines John Carpenter’s affinity for burly men-on-a-mission narratives with the fantastical tropes of vintage adventure serials—an even more colorful, cartoonish, and muscular take on the Indiana Jones formula. Its jaw-dropping array of practical effects includes several pieces by Screaming Mad George, including a sewer demon and floating creature dotted with dozens of bloated eyeballs.

BRIDE OF RE-ANIMATOR
Brian Yuzna, 1990, 96 min, DCP

Monday, November 2 - 9:30PM

Tickets

The original Re-Animator adapted Frankenstein-by-way-of-Lovecraft into a bloodsoaked med school nightmare. And like any great sequel, Bride of Re-Animator goes bigger, zanier, and gorier, with moments that represent the zenith of practical effects work. Reuniting stars Jeffrey Combs as Dr. Herbert West and Bruce Abbott as former medical student Dr. Dan Cain, Bride sees the duo expand their experiments to re-animating individual severed limbs. Their goal: to resurrect Cain’s fiance by cobbling pieces of other women around her preserved heart. And from there, the creations only get crazier. Bride of Re-Animator is an extraordinary follow-up to Brian Yuzna and Screaming Mad George’s previous collaboration Society with an all-star special effects team also includes John Carl Buechler and the KNB EFX Group.
