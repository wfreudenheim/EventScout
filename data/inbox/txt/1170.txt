We're Piloting Video Hosting for Artists

❓🫵 Are you an artist who uses commercial platforms like Vimeo or YouTube to distribute or host your videos?

❓🫵 Do you work for an arts institution that relies on a third-party platform to host artists' work or documentation from your events? 

❓🫵 Have you ever uploaded a video to a third-party platform for it to be later flagged for removal? 
 
🫵🫵🫵
 
In light of artists’ videos increasingly being removed from commercial platforms like Vimeo and YouTube, Rhizome is piloting artist channels on our self-hosted video platform, video.rhizome.org (VRO).

Rhizome launched VRO in 2023 as a way to reliably and independently host videos from our archive, including commissioned artworks and documentation from past Rhizome events. When Ann Hirsch’s horny lil feminist— commissioned by Rhizome and the New Museum as an online exhibition in 2015—was taken down by Vimeo, the risks involved in platform dependence were illuminated. In order to keep the project online, we initiated our own node in the open-source service PeerTube to host the videos embedded in Hirsch’s project website.

As we determine a sustainable model for this initiative, we invite artists to express interest in being included. If you are interested in hosting your work with us or learning more about ongoing development, please register your interest using this form.

Artist Profile: Tara Kelton

"I’m less interested in prompting AI. With a human, there’s a real tension in not knowing whether they’ll do it, or what you’ll get back. With AI, you’re not prompting; you’re commanding a sycophant—it always returns something plausible. A person fills in what an instruction leaves out with judgment; a model fills it with the average. Boring, except at the edges, where it gets synthetic and disturbing." 

—Tara Kelton is interviewed by Upendra Vaddadi for our Artist Profile series.

There's still time to apply for a Rhizome Microgrant!

If you are seeking funds to help realize a creative project related to digital art and networked culture, there's still time to apply to Rhizome’s annual microgrants open call! This years open call is juried by Herdimas Anggara, Annie Rauwerda, Joygill Moriah, Larva Labs, Rahel Aima, and Rhizome staff.

This year, we invite proposals in the following categories: 

🫆PERMANENCE, in partnership with Larva Labs (digital projects built to endure);
🛠️MISUSE, (projects that misuse the technologies they depend on); 
✋HANDMADE, (non-AI works and handmade digital practices); 
💬CO-OP (cultural artifacts that result from collective online behavior)

This year, Rhizome is also partnering with Joygill Moriah and Liz Mputu (LVLZ Healing Hub) to offer additional annual grants for artists who exemplify the legacy of digital artist and curator Terrell Davis. This fund aims to support work that speaks to Black and Afrodiasporic experiences, and projects in any category may opt-in to receive additional funding through this award.

Apply by November 20!
