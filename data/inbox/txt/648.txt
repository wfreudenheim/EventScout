PROTECT THE FREEDOM TO READ WITH NYPL: GIVEAWAYS, DISCUSSION GUIDES & MORE!

Since 1982, libraries across the United States have observed Banned Books Week as a time to shine a light on titles that have been targeted for removal from schools and libraries. Join us at select locations for giveaways of frequently banned or challenged books, explore book discussion guides and resources—including special editions of our Teen Voices magazine—and register for programs that elevate and amplify the stories, voices, and titles that censorship attempts to repress.

BANNED BOOKS FROM THE COLD WAR TO THE PRESENT

Thurs, Oct 8 | 6:30 PM | Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library (SNFL) & Online
Join Yasha Klots of the Tamizdat Project for a conversation about the smuggled literature that defied Soviet censorship during the Cold War and illuminated the enduring power of the printed word. In the Soviet Union, tamizdat (literally, published "over there," or abroad) was a term for manuscripts that were rejected, censored, or never submitted for publication but smuggled out from behind the Iron Curtain and printed elsewhere.

CURRICULUM GUIDE & EXHIBITION | BANNED: CENSORSHIP & THE FREEDOM TO READ

The Library's online exhibition Banned: Censorship & the Freedom to Read examines the long history of battles over censorship, intellectual freedom, and the freedom to read. Educators can use an accompanying curriculum guide that offers case studies and resources grounded in NYPL's collections to introduce this vital subject to students in grades 6–12.

HOW BUDDY BRADLEY TRANSFORMED BALLET MOVEMENT 

Mon, Oct 5 | 6 PM | Library for the Performing Arts & Online
A panel of dance artists discuss the influence of the innovative jazz dancer and choreographer Buddy Bradley with live demonstrations. Iconic choreographers including George Balanchine and Agnes de Mille were among the many major artists who drew inspiration from his work. Some of Bradley and Balanchine's "creative synergies" will be displayed by the dancers in work such as Agon, Rubies, and Symphony in C.

KERRY KENNEDY WITH DOUGLAS BRINKLEY: THE BOLD AND EXTRAORDINARY ETHEL KENNEDY

Tues, Oct 13 | 7 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building & Online

HOME TO HARLEM: CELEBRATING BLACK BIBLIOGRAPHY AT THE SCHOMBURG CENTER 

Mon, Oct 19 | 1–5 PM | Schomburg Center 
Join the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture for a half-day symposium featuring panel discussions with archivists and scholars to reflect on the importance of Black bibliography and why it matters today. Get a behind-the-scenes look at how we used material evidence—bookplates, accession numbers, inscriptions, library stamps, card catalogs, and archival documents—to identify items in founder Arturo Schomburg's personal library or "seed" collection of work by and about Black people. Some items are now on view in To Uncover and Reveal to the World: Arturo Schomburg's Library, an exhibition that celebrates 100 years of the Schomburg Center. Co-presented by the Schomburg Center and the Bibliographical Society of America.

BLACK ON SCREEN: HONORING MARLON RIGGS

Wed, Oct 21 | 6:30 PM | Schomburg Center
Honor the life and legacy of the late filmmaker, poet, and activist Marlon Riggs, who documented Black gay life and addressed the urgency of the AIDS crisis within Black gay communities throughout the late 20th century. We will screen Riggs's 1993 short, Non, Je Ne Regrette Rien (No Regret), an intimate look at five HIV-positive gay Black men as they discuss their experiences living with AIDS and confront the stigmatization of the illness at the height of the epidemic. The film will be followed by a conversation on Riggs's legacy and the role of experimental art within the history of AIDS resistance and awareness with Dr. Gervais Marsh, associate curator at Creative Time, and writer, curator, and archivist Sur Rodney (Sur). Presented in collaboration with Creative Time.
