UNEASY ARRANGEMENTS: COLLAGE, ANIMATION & THE ARCHIVE

----------------------------------------

A workshop about unsettling, reassembling, and transforming images to uncover new meanings within the frame.

Oct 23 — Oct 25

In this three-day workshop, filmmaker and animator Kelly Sears leads an exploration of the politics and creative possibilities of reappropriating, reassembling, and mutating images. Approaching the image as raw material, participants will consider how collage, remix, and transformation can release images from their intended functions and generate new narratives, temporalities, and critical perspectives.

Across the weekend, participants will work through collage not simply as a visual technique, but as a way of thinking: bringing historical documents, institutional archives, family photographs, fragmented media, and the scraps of everyday visual culture into unexpected collision. Through screenings, discussion, and experimentation, the workshop will ask what becomes possible when images are displaced and recast, and how the visual culture of surveillance and consumption might even be turned into material for its own critique.

 

Kelly Sears
Workshop Lead

Alongside Sears, an all-star cast of artists working across found footage, photography, and appropriation will share their distinctive approaches to reworking existing images: Bill Morrison will delve into his recent work with surveillance materials; Anti-Banality Union will explore how composite imagery can reframe the assumptions of mainstream media; and acclaimed photographer Keisha Scarville will share her iterative approach to reinterpretation and artistic reappropriation.

ZODIAC KILLER PROJECT

----------------------------------------

A mysterious commercial and an abandoned true crime film become exercises in documentary invention.

Friday, October 2 — 7:30PM

Join us for a special screening and conversation with essay filmmaker Charlie Shackleton, kicking off his upcoming workshop Tell Don’t Show: Adventures in First-Person Filmmaking. The evening brings together Camera Test (King Cadbury), a documentary readymade that turns a mysterious Cadbury’s commercial preserved in family mythology into an investigation of memory, evidence, and chocolate biscuits, and Zodiac Killer Project, winner of the NEXT Innovator Award at the 2025 Sundance Film Festival. Beginning with the true crime documentary Shackleton tried and failed to make, the film conjures that abandoned project through empty Bay Area landscapes, stylized re-enactments, and Shackleton’s dryly funny narration, dismantling the familiar tricks and conventions of the genre along the way. Together, the films offer a brilliant introduction to Shackleton’s formally inventive first-person practice. Come through!

TELL DON’T SHOW: ADVENTURES IN FIRST PERSON FILMMAKING

----------------------------------------

A workshop exploring what becomes possible when a nonfiction filmmaker puts their own subjectivity at the center of the work.

Oct 2 — Oct 4

Only two spots left! In this three-day workshop, essay filmmaker Charlie Shackleton turns the familiar documentary mantra “show don’t tell” on its head, leading participants in an excavation of how first-person cinema can both offer a generative framework and be a creative technique; considering its rich proponents, revelatory applications and profound potentials for documentary. 

We’re thrilled to welcome Rebeca Huntt as a visiting artist, fresh from the world premiere of her new documentary Rapinoe, which opened TIFF Docs and was just acquired by HBO.

 

Rebeca Huntt
Visiting Artist

Alongside Shackleton, filmmakers Alan Berliner, Joanna Arnow and Rebeca Huntt will share their distinctive approaches to bringing the personal into their work. Participants will also have the opportunity to share a work-in-progress for constructive group feedback with this stellar crew!

Practice Thing with Adam Cardone

We're back with a new Practice Thing from professional magician and variety entertainer Cardone! Cardone joined us for The Magical Image: Special Effects as Documentary Method, a workshop exploring illusion, special effects, and the slippery space between the real and the manufactured image. An award-winning magician, musician, and escape artist with more than 3,000 performances under his belt, Cardone has built a practice around the mechanics of illusion and our desire to believe what we see.
