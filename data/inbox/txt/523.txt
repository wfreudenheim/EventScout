SEPTEMBER NEWSLETTER: URBAN HEALTH IN TENTH-CENTURY WEST ASIA

In the tenth-century Abbasid Caliphate, physicians explained medical diseases by using environmental terms. Writings described various health risks from air and water and how physicians could protect patients through routine changes and household hygiene. Elaine Van Dalen will explore the relationship between theories of disease, physicians’ recommendations, and historical approaches to urban hygiene and health. 

Join us on September 29 at 12:00 PM ET to consider how medicine intersected with environmental practice. Common Airs, Individual Remedies: Environment and Health in Tenth-Century West Asia is free and open to the public; registration is required. Lunch will be provided.

SCOTT KNOWLES - THE UNITED STATES OF DISASTER: DISASTER SCIENCE IN AMERICA SINCE 2001

September 30 | 6:00 PM - 7:30 PM ET
Online and In-person: Gallatin School (Room 801), New York University

The collapse of the World Trade Center towers in 2001 initiated one of the largest research initiatives in American history. The impact of Hurricane Katrina compounded this effort. THE POLITICAL ECONOMY OF RANGELAND DEGRADATION AND CONSERVATION: EVIDENCE FROM NAMIBIA

October 26 | 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM ET
