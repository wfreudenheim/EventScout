Thank you to everyone who joined us last week for Blank Forms’ ninth annual gala at the Angel Orensanz Foundation and the performances by Keiji Haino and Fushitsusha that followed. We were especially excited to welcome audiences into our new home at 151 Lafayette Street. There are still walls to paint and a few doors to install, but the vision for the space is already becoming clear. The energy throughout the week affirmed our sense that we are creating something with a distinct identity, unlike anything else in New York. As we approach our tenth anniversary, it was particularly meaningful to share this moment with so many people who have supported Blank Forms over the past decade and understood what we have been working toward from the beginning.

As we complete the space, we invite you to support this next chapter of Blank Forms by becoming a member or making a tax-deductible contribution toward furnishings, archival infrastructure, and workspaces.

Tonight, we continue our opening program with a free concert featuring Shouji Hara and Loren Connors, performing together for the first time. Hara and Connors will improvise on shakuhachi and guitar, respectively. Audiences are invited to arrive early, spend time in the new space, and enjoy drinks before the performance. Free with RSVP.

Shouji Hara and Loren Connors
Tuesday, November 17
5:00pm doors, 7:00pm performance
free entry with RSVP

On the heels of Keiji Haino’s bombastic three-night run inaugurating Blank Forms’ new loft, Shouji Hara and Loren Connors offer an intimate performance at 151 Lafayette Street. Audiences are invited to gather at Blank Forms early in the evening to view the space and enjoy drinks; to close the night, Hara and Connors will perform together for the first time, improvising on shakuhachi and guitar, respectively.

 

Blank Forms is a nonprofit organization supporting emerging and historically significant artists who produce work across disciplines, often rooted in traditions of experimental and creative music. We aim to establish new frameworks to preserve, nurture, and present these artists’ work and to build platforms for practices underrepresented in art’s commercial, institutional, and historical fields. Blank Forms collaborates with artists on commissions, exhibitions, publications as well as archival and estate projects within contemporary cultural ecosystems and in perpetuity. In presenting and documenting this work, Blank Forms seeks to foster an artistic community founded upon engaged and equitable conversations across continents, media, and generations.
