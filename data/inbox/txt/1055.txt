MoMI NEWS | November 4, 2026

Dear will,

This week, MoMI welcomes a bounty of special guests: director Lynne Littman with her underseen masterpiece Testament (we're showing her personal 35mm print!); directors Kogonada and Noam Argov appear in person with a Sloan Showcase of After Yang, preceded by the short Verse; artist and ophthalmologist Benjamin Freeman and curator Elisabeth Sherman discuss the 1990s Visible Human Project; and join us for a special live taping of Criterion's The Craft of Acting with Léa Seydoux in person. Coming soon: major retrospective The Minnellis kicks off with 35mm screenings of Cabaret; Some Came Running; The Long, Long Trailer; and The Cobweb; the beloved Peanuts movie A Boy Named Charlie Brown, and more family favorites.

EXHIBITION HIGHLIGHT

nephomancer
On view on the Schlosser Media Wall through December 6

In this new commission for the Schlosser Media Wall, presented in partnership with the Tezos Foundation, artists misha de ridder (b. 1971, Netherlands) and miragenesi (b. 1986, Italy) connect photographic evidence with nephomancy, the practice of reading clouds for signs and predictions. At a station in the Museum lobby and online, the public is invited to collect a process image from the production of nephomancer.

Testament (35mm)
With Lynne Littman in person
Saturday, November 7, 12:30 p.m

The Killing (35mm)
Sunday, November 8, 12:00 p.m

Stanley Kubrick and pulp-fiction legend Jim Thompson co-wrote this darkly comic thriller about a racetrack heist gone awry. Kubrick’s fragmented crime noir is his true narrative breakthrough, laying the groundwork for a filmography of flawed, failed masculinity and fatalistic humor.

Part of Kubrick

The Immigrant
Sunday, November 8, 2:15 p.m

James Gray’s exquisitely mounted drama of New York immigrant lives at the turn of the last century forgoes period piece grandstanding in favor of finely etched authenticity. Starring Marion Cotillard, Joaquin Phoenix, and Jeremy Renner.

Part of James Gray's New York and Reverse Shot Presents, free for MoMI members.

 

Force of Evil
Sunday, November 8, 3:00 p.m.

A jewellike masterpiece of film noir, blacklisted director and writer Abraham Polonsky’s classic is a pure dose of moral fatalism, starring the great John Garfield as an unscrupulous Wall Street lawyer who has no qualms about getting into business with a mobster.

Part of James Gray's New York

 

Raging Bull (35mm)
Sunday, November 8, 4:45 p.m

With Raging Bull, Scorsese turned the boxing movie on its head, forgoing every cliché to create an indelible work of art. In an Oscar-winning role, De Niro is Jake La Motta, whose life story is dramatized with a mix of Shakespearean tragedy and documentary-like realism, while Pesci and Moriarty received nominations for their spectacular work as Jake’s brother and second wife. 

Part of James Gray's New York

COMING SOON

The Minnellis
November 13–January 17, 2027

Of all the Hollywood dynasties, there is perhaps none more dazzling than the Minnellis. This retrospective calls attention to the thrilling range of father and daughter Vincente and Liza and the exacting approach they took to their art, with a lineup of unparalleled classics that often combine the dark and the exuberant in the same breath.

Produced in collaboration with Liza Minnelli, the Vincente Minnelli estate, Carrberry Companies, and V2 Entertainment Group. This series is supported by The Ensler-Rivel Fund for Classic Cinema.

The French Connection
Saturday, November 14, 4:00 p.m

“I felt this was a kind of crude poem to the city,” said director William Friedkin about this emotionally complex action masterpiece about an ethically dubious New York City cop, “Popeye” Doyle, who will do anything to take down a ring of foreign heroin smugglers. Winner of the 1971 Academy Award for Best Picture.

Part of James Gray's New York and New York Stories, free for MoMI Members

REVERSE SHOT AND SLOAN SCIENCE & FILM

Reverse Shot Review: 
Fatherland

The third and final installment of his black-and-white postwar trilogy trilogy, Paweł Pawlikowski's new film Fatherland follows a father and daughter as they traverse Germany, the father's homeland. Reviewing the film, Leonardo Goi writes that "Fatherland concerns a father and a daughter trying—and failing—to repress an unspeakable loss, and its aesthetic testifies to that same failure: it thrives on the dissonance between its immaculate, meticulously studied surfaces and all the pain that percolates into them."

Read the review here.

Sloan Science and Film:
Director Interview—Everest: The Other Side

From filmmaking duo Elizabeth Chai Vasarhelyi and Jimmy Chin, Everest: The Other Side follows an exhilarating, harrowing, and life-threatening attempt to hike up and ski down the little-traversed North Face of Mount Everest. In advance of the film’s release on October 30, we spoke with the filmmakers about how this film became personal, how they planned the shoot, and the techniques they used to film at such an incredible elevation.
