Wednesday, October 21, 6:30 p.m.
The Midnight Special: The Secret Prison History of American Music
Colin Asher in Conversation with Jefferson Cowie
FREE; RESERVE NOW

In The Midnight Special, Colin Asher explores America’s rich tradition of music behind bars, from blues to hip-hop. In popular music, rebellious artists and “outlaws” get glorified, but Asher tells a deeper story about the criminal justice system’s impact on our musicians, through portraits of artists from Lead Belly to Johnny Cash to Tupac Shakur. “The book underlines sheer human resiliency” (The New York Times, Editor’s Choice Pick). Asher, a former fellow at the Leon Levy Center for Biography, is also the author of the acclaimed Never a Lovely So Real: The Life and Work of Nelson Algren. He speaks about the new book with Leon Levy Biography Fellow Jefferson Cowie, a Pulitzer Prize-winning historian and a professor at Vanderbilt University who writes on American politics, labor, and race.
 
Presented with the Leon Levy Center for Biography.

Wednesday, October 14, 6:30 p.m.
AI and the Future of Education
FREE; RESERVE FOR ZOOM

Artificial intelligence has caused seismic disruption in the field of education in just a few years. Students, educators, and parents are confused about how to navigate this game-changing technology. How should schools from K-12 to colleges and universities approach learning in this new era, and how can they prepare students for success in a transformed workplace? A panel of experts weighs in on the salient issues, the points of tension, and possible paths forward. 
 
Featuring Steven Jackson, professor of information science and science and technology studies and vice provost for academic innovation at Cornell University; Aankit Patel, university dean for technology and computer and information sciences, The City University of New York; and Natasha Singer, a technology reporter for the business section of The New York Times, whose new book is Coding Kids: Big Tech's Battle to Remake Public Schools. Luke Waltzer, director of the Teaching and Learning Center at the CUNY Graduate Center, moderates.
 
Presented with the Teaching and Learning Center, CUNY AI Lab, GC Digital Initiatives, the American Social History Project/Center for Media and Learning, and the Mina Rees Library.
