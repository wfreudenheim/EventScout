THE JOANNA JACKSON GOLDMAN MEMORIAL LECTURES: NATIVE NATIONS, BENJAMIN FRANKLIN & THE COMING OF THE AMERICAN REVOLUTION WITH NED BLACKHAWK

Part I: Tues, Nov 17 | 6 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building (Midtown)
Part II: Wed, Nov 18 | 6 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building (Midtown)
This biennial lecture series at The New York Public Library, established by the estate of the historian Eric F. Goldman in honor of his wife, aims to encourage provocative comment and analysis concerning contemporary issues of deep, long-term significance for American democracy. In Part I, Ned Blackhawk discusses the story of Benjamin Franklin's origins and the influence of his family's relationships with Native communities. In Part II, he expands on Franklin's role in the conflicts over Indian affairs and in the Revolution to explore imperial warfare, settler uprisings, and the crisis of Native American diplomacy. This is a two-part lecture; attendees must register for each part of the lecture separately.

THE SET PIECES OF MARTHA GRAHAM'S PERFORMANCES

Mon, Nov 16 | 7 PM | Online

Watch the online rebroadcast of the 2024 Dance Division Symposium to see how she engaged with the Martha Graham Dance Company archives.

CONVERSATIONS IN BIOETHICS | ETHICS & THE FUTURE OF REPRODUCTION

Thurs, Nov 19 | 6:30 PM | Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library (SNFL) (Midtown) & Online
As part of our new series, join faculty from the Johns Hopkins Berman Institute of Bioethics to examine some of the most challenging questions in bioethics today. Scientists are exploring in vitro gametogenesis (IVG), a technology that could expand reproductive options while raising profound ethical questions. Join Jeffrey Kahn, PhD, MPH, and Anna Mastroianni, JD, MPH, as they explore the promises, risks, and societal implications of a technology that could reshape the future of human reproduction.

MADE IN NEW YORK: THE FORCES THAT SHAPED A CITY

Fri, Nov 20 | 2 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building (Midtown)
Drawing on the Library's research collections, including the Robert Moses Papers, professor Rashad Shabazz examines the lasting impact of major urban planning projects, including how the Cross Bronx Expressway disrupted poor Black and Puerto Rican neighborhoods in the South Bronx while unintentionally creating the conditions from which hip-hop emerged.

NOW EXTENDED! MARTHA GRAHAM: THE MOTHER OF PSYCHOLOGICAL DANCE

Through Jan 2, 2027 | Library for the Performing Arts (Lincoln Center)
The Library for the Performing Arts' popular large-scale exhibition exploring Martha Graham and her dance company has been extended by popular demand! Martha Graham: The Mother of Psychological Dance pulls rich materials from the Jerome Robbins Dance Division's Martha Graham archive, which the Library for the Performing Arts acquired from the Martha Graham Dance Company in 2020. Set pieces designed specifically for the dance company by Isamu Noguchi, and costumes designed by Martha Graham are also on display.

CLOSING SOON: SILLY OLD BEAR! WINNIE-THE-POOH AT 100

Through Nov 30, 2026 | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building (Midtown)
Winnie-the-Pooh was first published in October 1926. To celebrate the book's 100th birthday, The New York Public Library invites you to explore an anniversary display featuring original mockups of A.A. Milne's text alongside E.H. Shepard's famous illustrations, some notable translations of the Pooh stories from around the world, and, for the first time on public display, the new Roo toy gifted to the Library in 2026 by Her Majesty Queen Camilla. Plus, visit the stuffed toys that inspired the books and more.

CLOSING SOON! TO UNCOVER AND REVEAL TO THE WORLD: ARTURO SCHOMBURG'S LIBRARY

Through Dec 5, 2026 | Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture (Harlem)
Discover the story of Arturo Schomburg, whose collection of books, manuscripts, and art turned what was originally the 135th Street Branch Library into a global destination for the study of the African diaspora. To Uncover and Reveal to the World showcases over 100 objects from the remarkable collection The New York Public Library acquired from Schomburg in 1926, many on display for the first time.

LEARN MORE ᐳ

DECLARING AMERICA: 1776 AND BEYOND

Through Jan 10, 2027 | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building (Midtown)
Spanning multiple galleries at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, Declaring America: 1776 and Beyond unpacks the complex, diverse, and powerful stories of the American Revolution—from 1776 to today. Featuring broadsides and posters, iconic founding documents, protest ephemera, powerful artworks, and artifacts from the Library's collections at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture, and the Library for the Performing Arts, the exhibition presents a diverse range of perspectives and invites visitors of all ages to explore our nation's remarkable beginnings, complex legacies, and promises fulfilled and unfulfilled.
