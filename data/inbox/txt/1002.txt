FAILED EXORCISMS AND WRITING HORROR FOR PLEASURE

BATTLING DEMONS, FAMILY, AND GOODREADS WITH GRACE BYRON AND KATIE KITAMURA

Pioneer Works

Oct 30

What happens when your ex has transformed into an actual demon? Does it matter if the sex is good? Sometimes, the exorcism fails and people don’t change at all. This week on Broadcast, we’re battling demons, Goodreads warriors, and the delusions of family with new conversations featuring Grace Byron, Alexandra Schwartz, and Katie Kitamura.

Grace and Alex celebrate the paperback launch of Herculine with discussions of the Babadook, literary pans, and the sociopathy of Steinbeck’s Cathy Ames—while Katie Kitamura speaks with Broadcast Senior Editor Lucy McKeon about psychological horror and writing for pleasure. Read on for more on trans villains, love, and the Benadryl Hat Man.

----------------------------------------

Grace Byron’s Demons
Grace Byron in conversation with Alexandra Schwartz

Alexandra Schwartz first came to know Grace Byron as an incisive and prolific critic, a thrilling new voice on the literature scene. In early October, the two sat down at McNally Jackson Seaport to mark the paperback release of Herculine with a conversation spanning sleep paralysis, trauma in art, and the responses the novel has provoked in the last year. In Alexandra’s words, “Herculine is a horror novel, but it is also wry and witty; just because you suddenly find yourself in the midst of a Rosemary’s Baby situation doesn’t mean that you can’t quip about it.”

Katie Kitamura: Writing Pleasure
Katie Kitamura in conversation with Lucy McKeon

Katie Kitamura’s Audition, a magic eye of a book, is a novel preoccupied with middles: a woman’s experience of middle age, the midpoint of a romantic relationship, midlife crisis, mid-career creativity, and a surreal shift, mid-book, that has thrilled and perplexed readers trying to make sense of the novel’s multiple realities. The question of how incompatible truths can coexist, even form a cohesive story, seems less important than what the bifurcated narrative itself can do.

Broadcast Senior Editor Lucy McKeon spoke with Kitamura at the New York Public Library while she was on a fellowship at the Cullman Center. They sat in a wood-paneled room and drank black tea. Lucy writes: “She spoke about her work openly and generously, even when I asked slightly embarrassing questions, like what if anything did she wish she’d known earlier about love.”
