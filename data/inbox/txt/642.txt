WE’RE SICK TO DEATH OF ALL THIS NONSENSE: DIVAS DE MAS MUERTAS

----------------------------------------

Readings, moving images, amulets, spells, and an invocation of the divas who never really leave us.

Thursday, October 8 — 7:30PM

A true diva never really dies, does she? We’re Sick to Death of All This Nonsense, our multimedia series selected by poet Christine Larusso and writer and curator Rachael Rakes, returns for round four: Divas de Mas Muertas. Bringing together live performance, reading, sound, and moving images, this edition features readings from poets Dorothea Lasky and Hafizah Geter alongside Jesse McLean’s short film Somewhere Only We Know and Possibly in Michigan by Cecelia Condit. It’s October, and we’re invoking those merely departed from this plane — the baddies in our bones, keeping us keeping the flame. We, the nearly departed, will lay the marigolds on the altar together. Literally. Bring your objects, amulets, and spells along, and join us for an evening of fragments, feelings, artworks, and imaginaries conjured in the moment. Come through!

UNDER THE SKY OF FETISHES

----------------------------------------

Moving between archive and fabulation, Déodat follows the traces of histories that refuse to disappear.

Sunday, October 4 — 7:30PM

We’re delighted to welcome Caroline Déodat for a special evening of films and conversation with writer, researcher, and film programmer Yasmina Price! Working at the intersection of ethnography and fabulation, archival research and critical theory, Déodat’s films explore the spectral lives of images and the histories of violence that continue to haunt them. We’ll bring together three of her films, Landslides (2020), Under the Sky of the Fetishes (2023), and her newest work, Nostra Memoria (2026), tracing enduring imaginaries of slavery, colonialism, fascism, and empire while experimenting with forms that unsettle the authority of the archive and the hegemony of the visible. Following the screening, Déodat will join Yasmina Price for a conversation about their shared work around African and Afro-diasporic expressive cultures, aesthetic remembrance, and the politics of camera-based technologies. Don’t miss it, come through!

ZODIAC KILLER PROJECT

----------------------------------------

A mysterious commercial and an abandoned true crime film become exercises in documentary invention.

Friday, October 2 — 7:30PM

Join us for a special screening and conversation with essay filmmaker Charlie Shackleton, kicking off his upcoming sold out workshop Tell Don’t Show: Adventures in First-Person Filmmaking. The evening brings together Camera Test (King Cadbury), a documentary readymade that turns a mysterious Cadbury’s commercial preserved in family mythology into an investigation of memory, evidence, and chocolate biscuits, and Zodiac Killer Project, winner of the NEXT Innovator Award at the 2025 Sundance Film Festival. Beginning with the true crime documentary Shackleton tried and failed to make, the film conjures that abandoned project through empty Bay Area landscapes, stylized re-enactments, and Shackleton’s dryly funny narration, dismantling the familiar tricks and conventions of the genre along the way. Together, the films offer a brilliant introduction to Shackleton’s formally inventive first-person practice, where subjectivity, humor, and creative frustration become tools for thinking through what documentary can and can’t show. Following the screening, Charlie Shackleton will join us for a conversation. Come through!

FALL OF FREEDOM — UNDER PRESSURE: HOW TO DOCUMENT “UNPRECEDENTED TIMES”

----------------------------------------

How can we continue to work critically, responsibly, and safely in a political landscape increasingly threatening to those organizing and documenting on the ground?

Thursday, October 1 — 7:30PM

Tonight! We're sold out, with a handful of walk-up tickets at the door, come through!

Presented as part of Fall of Freedom, this conversation brings together filmmakers and collaborators Rachel Lauren Mueller and Laura Poitras with radio producer and Radiolab creator Jad Abumrad to consider the practical and ethical questions facing political documentaries in the United States today. We’ll be taking Poitras and Mueller’s recent collaboration on They’re Here, a documentary about ICE’s occupation of Minneapolis as a starting point. They’ll draw from their experiences across on-the-ground organizing, investigative film, journalism, data, sound, and nonfiction storytelling to ask how makers navigate access and risk, protect sources and collaborators, and safeguard their teams and materials.
