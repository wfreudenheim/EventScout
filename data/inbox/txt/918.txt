MEET THE PORTAL’S KEEPERS

A GLIMPSE INTO THE FALL EXHIBITIONS SEASON AT PIONEER WORKS

Pioneer Works

Oct 23

What version of reality do we need to leave behind to return to nature, to each other, and to ourselves? So asks LaJuné McMillian, whose exhibition Child/ren of the Bloom is on view at Pioneer Works through December 20. With interactive video, beaded embroidery, holographic displays, and 3-D models, the space becomes a portal into the ocean where the wisdom of sovereignty and liberation is found in the movement of phytoplankton.

In the gallery directly below it, an exhibition from Finnegan Shannon is similarly committed to transformation at every scale. There’s no me without you features a conveyor belt that circles the room, bringing the artworks to the viewer to hold and touch from the vantage of a comfortable chair. Inverting the typically white-walled gallery space that prioritizes protection of objects above all else, Shannon foregrounds people and their needs, allowing the objects to change, regenerate, and be replaced to support the human’s experience. And to further exemplify our interdependence, the art itself comes from a rotating cast of emerging and established figures of New York City’s disability art scene: Joselia Rebekah Hughes, Ariana Martinez, and Alex Dolores Salerno. Shannon explains: “My work is so dependent on other artists, and is often an expression of collective or cultural knowledge. That’s not how the art world typically operates. It wants to pluck you out and say, ‘You are an individual genius with all these amazing thoughts.’ That’s just not how I experience being creative.”

The Portal’s Keeper
LaJuné McMillian in conversation with ruth gebreyesus

In LaJuné McMillian’s work, portals are not fixed; they’re ever-changing spaces where one state gives way to another. They’ve long sought to render liberation as something that’s seen and felt, rather than merely imagined—and their latest show, Child/ren of the Bloom, takes that pursuit underwater, where the deep becomes a site of spiritual communion. Ahead of the show’s opening, we hosted the artist in conversation with writer and curator ruth gebreyesus to discuss algal blooms, the power of spirit, and sovereign selves of the past and future.

Touch Me
Finnegan Shannon in conversation with Ksenia M. Soboleva

Where museums demand we stand and move at their pace, Finnegan Shannon’s works flip the expectations by centering disabled audiences while also opening new possibilities for how any body might inhabit these spaces. Shannon’s practice challenges the hierarchies embedded in institutional spaces, where artwork preservation typically takes precedence over human experience. Their installation, There’s no me without you, inverts these priorities—featuring comfortable chairs that visitors can sit on and a conveyor belt that brings art to seated viewers. Back in 2025, the artist sat down with writer and art historian Ksenia Soboleva to talk queerness, creativity, and collective knowledge. We’re sharing their conversation now, in celebration of the exhibition’s debut.
