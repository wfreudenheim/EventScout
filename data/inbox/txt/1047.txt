Dear readers,

This morning I went back and reread the note I sent to n+1’s esteemed newsletter recipients two years ago, the day after Donald Trump was reelected. My takeaway is that it feels much better writing under conditions other than despair.

This isn’t a place for hot takes and election analysis—there are many high-quality memes for that—but as pundits proclaim the end of the Trump era and the Long 2016, I thought I’d reflect on how the past couple of years have looked for n+1, about which I can speak with more confidence than the state of the American electorate.

The most striking fact about n+1 over the past two years is how many people have shown up. In the midst of an alleged right-wing realignment, when culture was supposed to be patriotic or produced by AI (which was also supposed to be patriotic), you subscribed to the magazine by the thousands, broke all kinds of readership records, and regularly packed the office for events. 

The fact of this engagement was incredibly buoying, in part because it wasn’t the gamified engagement of sponsored tweets by idiot AI influencers. It was the real, sustained, in-person thing: people reading together, thinking together, giving a shit together. (I promised no election analysis, but I’ll allow myself this much: one way to interpret last night’s results is as a victory for people organizing themselves against a shallow, foreordained consensus.) I’ve spent a lot of the past two years worrying about threats to independent media from the government and from big tech, and despite the blue tsunami, there are plenty of reasons to remain concerned. But even in the bleakest moments, those worries were always mitigated by your attention to what we do, and your solidarity with the people who do it. 

When I think about what n+1 means to me—and, I hope, to many of you—I increasingly think about it like this: as a sustainable alternative to corrosive habits of thought and enforced alienation, and as a community that can model how intellectual life should really work. Building the alternative and maintaining the community isn’t easy, and it isn’t cheap, which is why I hope you’ll contribute as much as you can to our Bookmatch fundraiser, which launched this week. But no matter how you can give, know that I’m profoundly grateful for your support over the past two years and beyond. Doing this work under conditions other than despair—sounds fun!

Yours warmly, 
Mark Krotov 
Publisher and coeditor, n+1
