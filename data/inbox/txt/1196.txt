MATTER OF INTELLIGENCE SEMINAR

SEMINAR 8: COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE, COLLECTIVE AGENCY

TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 17, 2026, 6:30–8 PM EST 
WOLLMAN HALL, THE NEW SCHOOL
65 WEST 11TH STREET, 5TH FLOOR

The eighth installment in our Matter of Intelligence Seminar Series is convened with and features artist Agnieszka Kurant in conversation with neuroscientist and AI researcher Ida Momennejad to extend the question of intelligence beyond the individual to the central concern of Kurant’s work: collective intelligence. Collective intelligence describes how novel forms emerge from complex systems in nonlinear and unpredictable ways. Considering intelligence as an emergent property of entire ecosystems, the speakers ask what new forms of agency collective intelligence makes possible, and what its political implications are. The conversation is introduced and moderated by the VLC's Eriola Pira.

WAR TRASH

SATURDAY, DECEMBER 12, 2026, 3:30–5 PM EST 
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER
THE NEW SCHOOL, 63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL
NEW YORK CITY

For the exhibition Aziz Hazara: Coming Home, now on view at SculptureCenter, artist Aziz Hazara offers an archaeology of what remains after war ends and a straightforward gesture of return. In a moment when the 2001 intervention in Afghanistan is being reassessed in light of new conflicts, this symposium expands upon the proposition to reckon with war waste. Beginning with an artist talk by Hazara (who joins remotely), the program features conversations and presentations with Adam HajYahia, Tom Keenan, Saad Khan and Madiha Tahir, Jasbir K. Puar and Sophia Stamatopoulou-Robbins. Presented by SculptureCenter in collaboration with the Vera List Center for Art and Politics at The New School and the Center for Human Rights and the Arts at Bard College.

MATTER OF INTELLIGENCE SEMINAR

SEMINAR 9: INTELLIGENCEMAXXING

JANUARY 25, 2027
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER
THE NEW SCHOOL, 63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL
NEW YORK CITY

Our ninth Matter of Intelligence Seminar considers “intelligencemaxxing” and its corollaries: IQ-maxxing or brain-maxxing. At a time of otherwise widespread anti-intellectualism, intelligencemaxxing reveals a response to the same as well as to the growing literacy crisis, distrust in higher education, the need to combat brainrot and doomscrolling, the desire to distinguish oneself from AI slop, and a search for cultural capital in the absence of physical capital. Convened with Rosaline Dou and featuring designer, artist, and cyberethnographer Ruby Justice Thelot, and artist, writer, and founding member of the trend forecasting group K-HOLE Dena Yago.

VLC NETWORKS

EXCITING NEWS AND ANNOUNCEMENTS FROM OUR GLOBAL NETWORK OF ARTISTS, SCHOLARS, ACTIVISTS, AND ORGANIZATIONS WITHIN THE VLC COMMUNITY.

First up, this Sunday, November 22, join our 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Fellow Stephanie Dinkins at 601Artspace for The Stories We Tell Our Machines, a gathering exploring how shared stories, experiences, and knowledge can help shape technologies that better understand and support our communities.

On Friday, November 27 at London's V&A South Kensington, current VLC Fellow Kira Xonorika presents her interactive performance Agent, exploring liminal encounters with otherness, robotics, animacy, cognition, and movement. The performance stems from an ongoing intention to decouple technology from domination, featuring Xonorika and her robot dog DIVA.

Here in New York, our 2013–2015 VLC Fellow Jill Magid and spring 2023 Post/doc contributor Cato Ouyang are featured in the group exhibition Trinket at Parent Company. The 100-artist group show, curated by Anne-Laure Lemaitre, is designed around single small-scale works, probing the intent, ideas, or impulses of a broader practice embedded within a tiny gesture (on view through January 9, 2027).

And at Philadelphia's Calder Gardens, don't miss Magid's four-day-only exhibition With Full Consent (November 20–23). Developed in conjunction with the launch of her new monograph, Magid works with the Centre Pompidou in Paris, Calder Gardens, and the Barnes Foundation to use an existing State Department procedure to pursue questions central to her work: Who determines cultural significance? What can the State protect? And can an individual use the State’s own procedures to enter her body of work into the public record? Read more about the project in her recent New York Times feature.
