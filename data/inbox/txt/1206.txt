On September 15, 2026, e-flux Screening Room presented a conversation with Andrei Ujică moderated by Film Comment editor Devika Girish and e-flux Film curator Lukas Brasiskis. Organized with the Romanian Cultural Institute in New York, the evening featured excerpts from Videograms of a Revolution (1992, co-directed with Harun Farocki), The Autobiography of Nicolae Ceaușescu (2010), and TWST / Things We Said Today (2024). It also marked the New York launch of Meridian (2026), Ujică’s book of conversations with Claudia Nedelcu Duca, published in English by Sticking Place Books. The conversation below has been edited for clarity and length.

Selected excerpts from the conversation:

Andrei Ujică: The fascination of cinema lies partly in its limits. First of all, though, there is the materiality of images and film. It’s a promise of materiality, and this materiality is there. Literature is more abstract. It creates a film and images in your head. There is a direct, secret dialogue with a writer who might have been dead for centuries, speaking directly into your brain. This creates images, feelings, abstraction, reflection.

Film does the same thing. It always tells a story, a poem, or a play. But it’s more material. You start with images, and afterward those images become a story in your head. It works in the opposite direction. This materiality is fascinating.

[…]

AU: The best thing to do is to try to reconstruct the film of history from itself. This became possible only in the twentieth century, with moving images. It’s the great advantage the twentieth century offers us in reading history. It also ended with the twentieth century. Soon afterward, we entered an inflation of images, with phones and social media. The status of images as serious, credible historical documents vanished.

[…]

AU: From one film to the next, I’ve searched for a new level of understanding and formal accomplishment. Videograms, with Harun, was a more explicit essay, an analysis of images. In Autobiography, the challenge was to achieve something like pure cinema. In TWST / Things We Said Today, it was to turn historical documents and images back into fiction and let fiction enter historical reality. I played with this. That’s why the characters are ghostly drawings rather than filmed figures.
