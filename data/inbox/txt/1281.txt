BERENICE OLMEDO: EPITHUMIA

Jan 28–Apr 19, 2027

Berenice Olmedo, 2026, research image. Histology Laboratory of the Department of Cell and Tissue Biology, Faculty of Medicine, UNAM: Digestive System, oral cavity. Photograph by Dr. Enrique A. Sampedro Carillo. Courtesy the artist 

Opening Reception

Wed, Jan 27, 2027, 6–8pm
 

Epithumia, often translated as a certain spiritedness or breath of life, is understood by Berenice Olmedo as a vital flow that “runs through machines and organisms, enabling a reorganization of life, organs, and technologies.”
 

Olmedo’s first institutional solo exhibition in New York advances her work with machinic bodies. In Epithumia, she shifts from examining external extremities to the interior, working with artificial organs that she proposes are not simply substitutes for normative organ functions but a means to redefine support systems. Her research-based sculptural process asks how the bodies that might host these artificial organs are dependent on and inscribed within our technical networks of care and social fabric.

 

At SculptureCenter, Olmedo will produce a group of large stomach sculptures, rigged with a motorized and pneumatic kineticism. The exhibition advances considerations visible in her recent work such as Pnoê, Pneuma (both 2025), and Sinécdoque (2026), which are inspired by the method of ex vivo perfusion, through which organs are kept alive outside the body. Often making sculpture in mutual exchange with scientists and medical technicians, Olmedo’s practice is informed, in part, by time spent around medical equipment, precise instruments, and imaging apparatuses that visually isolate parts of the body from their composite whole. 

 

At a moment when emphasis on the brain and intelligence dominates our technological imagination, Olmedo insists we look elsewhere: to the stomach, the diaphragm, and the visceral rhythms of the body. Altering the long and canonical history of figurative sculpture, Olmedo casts the artificial organ as her central subject, and, borrowing the aesthetic vernacular of medical imaging technology, troubles the limitations of what viewers can understand as a subject. Implicit in this is the assertion that life cannot be reduced to discrete measures but depends, as well, on continuous interdependence, disintegrating binary classifications used to distinguish between the “able” and “disabled,” the “healthy” and “unhealthy”. 

 

Berenice Olmedo: Epithumia is curated by Jovanna Venegas, Curator. 

 

Berenice Olmedo (b. 1987, Oaxaca) lives and works in Mexico City. Known for her sculptures and kinetic objects, her work questions the idea of bodily wholeness and addresses political dimensions of disability, illness, and care. Olmedo has exhibited at institutions such as Berkeley Art Museum and Pacific Film Archive; Kunsthalle Basel; Simian, Copenhagen; Jan Kaps, Cologne, Tate Modern, London; New Museum, New York; Museo de la Ciudad de México; Museo Jumex, Mexico City; Musée d’Art Contemporain de Bordeaux; ICA Boston; Boros Collection, Berlin; Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Monterrey; the Museum für Moderne Kunst, Frankfurt; Museo Tamayo, Mexico City, amongst others. She has participated in the 36th Bienal de São Paulo, the 14th Bienal do Mercosul, and the 2nd Riga International Biennial of Contemporary Art.
