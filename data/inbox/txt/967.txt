EXHIBITION HIGHLIGHT

It’s a Dream (2007)
On view in the Jane Henson Amphitheater through November 29

In 2007, the Cannes Film Festival invited Tsai Ming-liang and more than 30 other directors to make three-minute films about the experience of going to the movies. Tsai responded with It’s a Dream, drawing on memories of the cinemas he attended as a child in Malaysia. This Gallery Screening Program is presented in conjunction with the exhibition Seeing the Cinema: Photographic Visions of Movie Theaters.

NOW SHOWING

Goodbye, Dragon Inn
Friday, October 30, 6:00 p.m

Astonishingly beautiful, and filled with expertly timed sight gags, Goodbye, Dragon Inn is Taiwanese master Tsai Ming-liang's rueful backwards glance at the disappearance of the filmgoing culture of his youth, set entirely in one decaying movie theater. One of the seminal films of the 21st century. With your ticket, request a same-day admission sticker to see Tsai Ming-liang's It's a Dream in the Jane Henson Amphitheater.

Sneak Preview: The Meltdown (El deshielo)
With director Manuela Martelli
Friday, October 30, 7:00 p.m.

Set in 1992 against the backdrop of Chile’s post-Pinochet “economic miracle,” The Meltdown follows nine-year-old Inés during a stay at an Andean ski resort, where she befriends Hanna, a rebellious German teenage athlete. Chile's 2027 Oscar submission for Best International Feature.

With live piano accompaniment by Makia Matsumura
Friday, October 30, 8:00 p.m.

Groundbreaking German director F.W. Murnau all but invented the modern horror film with this unnerving—and unofficial—adaptation of Bram Stoker’s novel Dracula.

The Vourdalak
Saturday, October 31, 1:00 p.m.

A genuinely eccentric spin on the vampire story, integrating uncanny puppet effects with live-action storytelling, this unsettling, lovingly crafted—and often funny—period creep-show adapts “The Family of the Vourdalak,” a novella by Aleksey Konstantinovich Tolstoy written in 1839—that’s nearly 60 years before Bram Stoker’s Dracula.

The Shining: International Version (U.S. Theatrical Premiere)
With Garrett Brown and Michael Chaiken in person
Sunday, November 1, 12:00 p.m

When The Shining was first released in 1980, most critics were generally baffled or negative, unable to understand Stanley Kubrick’s visionary approach to the horror genre. Kubrick responded to the critiques by editing a shorter version with less slack for the European market. Preceded by a conversation with Steadicam inventor Garrett Brown and Kubrick archivist Michael Chaiken. 

Part of Kubrick

The Shining
Sunday, November 1, 3:30 p.m.

With remarkable performances by Jack Nicholson and Shelley Duvall, iconic set design, and groundbreaking Steadicam photography, Kubrick’s masterpiece of transcendent horror adapts Stephen King’s novel about Jack Torrance, an alcoholic writer driven mad while working as the caretaker of the cavernous Colorado hotel the Overlook over the course of one isolated winter.

The Killing (35mm)
Sunday, November 8, 12:00 p.m

Stanley Kubrick and pulp-fiction legend Jim Thompson co-wrote this darkly comic thriller about a racetrack heist gone awry. Kubrick’s fragmented crime noir is his true narrative breakthrough, laying the groundwork for a filmography of flawed, failed masculinity and fatalistic humor.
