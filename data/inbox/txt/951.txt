Open Call: Microgrants 2026

If you are seeking funds to help realize a creative project related to digital art and networked culture, consider applying to Rhizome’s annual microgrants open call.

Since 2014, Rhizome has awarded small grants ($500-$1500) to support the creation of new web-based works. In recent years, awarded projects have included a multilingual archive of printed “Internet directory” books, a browser-based adventure game where you care for “small but alive” creatures, a free pop-up school in Portland, hacking inactive Twitter accounts to acquire the username “everyoneisugly,” and many more. 

This year, we invite proposals in the following categories: 

🫆PERMANENCE, in partnership with Larva Labs (digital projects built to endure);
🛠️MISUSE, (projects that misuse the technologies they depend on); 
✋HANDMADE, (non-AI works and handmade digital practices); 
💬CO-OP (cultural artifacts that result from collective online behavior)

This year, Rhizome is also partnering with Joygill Moriah and Liz Mputu (LVLZ Healing Hub) to offer additional annual grants for artists who exemplify the legacy of digital artist and curator Terrell Davis. This fund aims to support work that speaks to Black and Afrodiasporic experiences, and projects in any category may opt-in to receive additional funding through this award.

Agentic Aesthetics, Featuring Intersubjectivity IV book launch
October 28, 2026, 6:30-9pm ET
The New Museum and online
235 Bowery, New York, NY, 10002 

On October 28, Rhizome will explore Agentic Aesthetics as the next edition in our long-running Net Aesthetics series. Since 2005, each of these panels has attempted to capture a current conversation in artistic practice as it relates to changing internet culture and technology. The most recent, Neural Net Aesthetics, took place in 2023, exploring machine learning and its implications for composing and processing sounds and images. 

This event will also mark the book launch of “Intersubjectivity IV: Network Affect” published by Sternberg Press, gathering contributors from the book and artists from Rhizome's community. Books will be available for purchase at the event.

Internet Walk with Ingrid Burrington
November 1, 2026, 1pm ET
Elizabeth Street Garden
New York, NY, 10012

Join Rhizome and Ingrid Burrington, author of "Networks of New York: An Illustrated Field Guide to Urban Internet Infrastructure," on November 1st for a guided tour of the physical internet as seen in Manhattan’s Lower East Side.

Using the vernacular of a nature field guide, Burrington will identify often overlooked network objects, utility markings, electronic equipment and sensors, and buildings we pass by everyday that are instrumental to global internet activity.  

We will meet at 1pm outside of Elizabeth Street Garden and the tour will last approximately an hour.

Trust & Safety
November 10, 2026, 7-9pm ET
Electronic Arts Intermix, 264 Canal Street 3W
New York, NY, 10013 

EAI and Rhizome present Trust & Safety, a screening and conversation addressing the challenges faced by organizations entrusted with preserving artworks that test cultural, technological, and institutional limits.

The program features a selection of works from EAI’s collection by Peggy Ahwesh, Alex Bag, Ellen Cantor, Barbara Hammer, and Muntadas. The works were selected in relation to Vimeo’s content moderation policies and the limitations those policies pose for an archive of challenging media. After EAI encountered repeated problems hosting works on Vimeo, Rhizome assisted with the migration of EAI’s video holdings to PeerTube, an open-source platform that allows organizations to maintain greater control over their digital infrastructure.
