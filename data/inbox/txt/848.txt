THURSDAY LECTURE SERIES: IBERIAN MODERNISMS AND THE RIF WAR: SPAIN, MOROCCO, AND THE TRANSCOLONIAL 

IN PERSON AT THE HEYMAN CENTER @ 12:15PM EDT

Modernism developed in Spain amid the collapse of its overseas empire at the turn of the century, the rise of peripheral nationalisms within the Peninsula, and a chronic colonial war in Morocco. In this lecture, Arnau Sala Sallent examines Spanish Modernism as an effect of the mutual implication of colonial warfare and Spain’s internal national fragmentation. 

Open to Columbia-affiliated faculty, students, and invited guests only.

WHEN WOR(L)DS COLLIDE: CHINESE CHARACTERS, EAST ASIAN WRITING, AND IDEOGRAPHIC UNIVERSALISM

IN PERSON AT KENT HALL AND VIA ZOOM @ 6:10PM EDT

By examining the typology of the Chinese script, the role of Classical Chinese in pre-modern East Asia, and the mechanisms of adaptation of Chinese characters as they spread across Asia, Zev Handel shows that the explanation for the apparent paradox is both mundane and profound.
