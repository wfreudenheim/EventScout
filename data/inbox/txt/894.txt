CAMERA MINISTRY: LENS-BASED ENCOUNTERS

----------------------------------------

A workshop exploring lens-based storytelling as a practice of presence, connection and encounter.

Nov 13 — Nov 15

In this three-day intensive workshop, photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah invites participants into the methods and philosophy behind his singular lens-based practice and what he calls the “Camera Ministry.” Moving between street photography, portraiture, and poetic documentary, we’ll consider image-making as a practice of presence and encounter: what does it mean to truly see another person, and how can the camera become a means for connection rather than simply a tool for observation? Through screenings, discussion, and hands-on exploration, participants will think through how intuition, repetition, proximity, conversation, trust, and vulnerability enter into making an image, alongside the technical knowledge that allows the camera itself to become increasingly unobtrusive.

 

Jamil GS
Legendary Hip-hop Photographer & Cultural Archivist

Alongside Khalik, filmmaker, cinematographer, and teacher Shevaun Mizrahi will discuss her transition from cinematographer to director and the ways magical realism and vérité interact in her work, while photographer and archivist Jamil GS will reflect on photography as a form of cultural memory. Participants will also have the opportunity to share their own lens-based work with Khalik in a creative feedback session, bringing questions of attention, intimacy, responsibility, and representation back into their own practices.

THE LOST SEASON

----------------------------------------

Explore the uncanny terrain between documentary and speculative fiction.

Thursday, October 22 — 7:30PM

Tonight! We’re kicking off our upcoming workshop Uneasy Arrangements: Collage, Animation & the Archive with a special screening and conversation with filmmaker and animator Kelly Sears! Seasons vanish mid-frame, a telephoto lens reveals a spirit dimension, and a cross-species rebellion takes shape in Sears’ uncanny worlds, where documentary meets speculative fiction. Spanning nearly two decades of work, the program brings together films that reconfigure archival and original imagery through collage, layering, and experimental animation, transforming familiar materials into strange new narratives. Through thousands of filmic and photographic fragments, Sears opens up possibilities for reexamining histories and imagining alternate futures. Following the screening, Kelly Sears will join writer, educator, and editor Catherine Taylor for a conversation about her singular approach to moving images. Come through!

A RADICAL THREAD

----------------------------------------

An 83-foot tapestry, seventeen years in the making, traces a community’s history of environmental resistance.

Thursday, November 5 — 7:30PM

We’re delighted to welcome Jeanne C. Finley for a special screening of her film A Radical Thread, followed by a conversation with filmmaker Lynne Sachs! In the late 1960s, hundreds of families seeking an alternative to American consumer culture settled on California’s San Juan Ridge, building a community rooted in collective living and environmental stewardship. Over the decades, they organized against clear-cutting, river damming, and open-pit gold mining, building a remarkable legacy of resistance. The film traces this history through an extraordinary tapestry by Marsha Stone and a collective of artists and community members. Weaving together the tapestry’s twelve embroidered panels, archival footage, and the voices of Ridge residents, Finley explores how histories of struggle, survival, and collective action can be preserved and passed on, even as climate-driven wildfires threaten the community’s future. THIS WEEK ON THE UNDO MEMBERSHIP

----------------------------------------

Practice Thing with Alan Berliner

Find some words of wisdom from acclaimed experimental documentary filmmaker Alan Berliner, who recently joined us as a visiting artist for our workshop Tell Don’t Show: Adventures in First-Person Filmmaking! Known for his inventive and deeply personal film essays, Berliner’s work combines experimental cinema, humor, and intimate explorations of family, memory, and identity. His films have screened at major festivals around the world, earned numerous awards and retrospectives, and helped shape the language of personal documentary filmmaking. Listen in as Alan reflects on the challenges of filmmaking and shares a deceptively simple way of thinking about what makes the whole endeavor worthwhile.
