The eighth installment of our Matter of Intelligence Seminar Series is convened with and features artist Agnieszka Kurant in conversation with neuroscientist and AI researcher Ida Momennejad. This seminar extends the question of intelligence beyond the individual to the central concern of Kurant’s work: collective intelligence. 

Collective intelligence describes how novel forms emerge from complex systems in nonlinear and unpredictable ways. Drawing on living systems, the evolution of culture and technology, artificial life, simulated societies, and AI agent swarms, the program also explores the processes of networked value creation in the digital economy and the global extraction of labor of the multitude underlying artificial intelligence. Considering intelligence as an emergent property of entire ecosystems, the speakers ask what new forms of agency collective intelligence makes possible, and what its political implications are. The conversation is introduced and moderated by the VLC's Eriola Pira.

The Vera List Center for Art and Politics is excited to participate in Critical Distance Centre for Curators’ 3rd edition of PROTOTYPE: An Arts Publishing Assembly. Free and open to the public, PROTOTYPE encompasses an art book fair/market, special exhibitions, and symposium/public program featuring talks, workshops, performances, and more. Visit the fair and pick up copies of VLC publications including As for Protocols, Carmen Amengual: A Non-Coincidental Mirror, Studies into Darkness: The Perils and Promise of Freedom of Speech, An Incomplete* Listing of NYC Libraries, Reading Rooms, and Archives, and more.

VLC NETWORKS

EXCITING NEWS AND ANNOUNCEMENTS FROM OUR GLOBAL NETWORK OF ARTISTS, SCHOLARS, ACTIVISTS, AND ORGANIZATIONS WITHIN THE VLC COMMUNITY.

First up, this Thursday, November 12, join our 2022–2024 VLC Fellow Anna Martine Whitehead at the Graham Foundation for the Chicago launch of FORCE! an opera in three acts (Sming Sming Books 2026). Whitehead developed and performed FORCE!, an abolitionist opera set in a prison waiting room, as part of their VLC Fellowship project. To mark the launch of the publication—which features a foreword by the VLC’s Re'al Christian—Whitehead shares highlights from the opera and its footnotes, alongside performances by Teiana Davis, Kai Black, Carmani Edwards, Daniella Hope, Nexus J, Zachary Nicol, and Eva Supreme. 

Current VLC Fellow Kira Xonorika is featured in the recently opened group exhibition I Follow Rivers at the Denver Art Museum. The show, curated by Raphael Fonseca, is the largest ever exhibition of modern and contemporary Indigenous Latin American artists in the US, with more than 100 works exploring interconnected themes of history, land, community, and resilience (on view through July 11, 2027).

For the Harun Farocki Institut, 2011–2013 VLC Fellow Joshua Simon's "Images after Seeing Everything. A Visual Essay” examines a trove of photographs, social media posts, and AI-generated images depicting the realities of ongoing settler violence in Gaza.

This Saturday, November 14, Interspecies Library (featured in An Incomplete* Listing) presents Sensoria Sessions I, a program rooted in multi-sensory responses to more-than-human ways of being. Hosted at Field Meridians in Brooklyn, the first session brings together zines, sounds, and moving images, including film shorts by Jacob Lindgren, who designed our 2022–2024 VLC Fellow Carmen Amengual's monograph A Non-Coincidental Mirror. 

And tomorrow, November 10, at Bowdoin College, Theodore Kerr, who participated in New School New Books 2025, will deliver the lecture THIS COULD BE AN AIDS MEMORIAL, exploring the limits and expansive possibilities of AIDS memorials within a new era of crisis.
