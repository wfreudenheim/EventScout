FILM HIGHLIGHT | October 16, 2026

Fantasia + Faust

This Saturday, we’re pleased to present two visually astonishing masterpieces of cinema back-to-back as part of our month-long Handcrafted Horrors series: Disney’s animated classic Fantasia and F. W. Murnau’s German expressionist fable Faust, which was released 100 years ago this month! We’re particularly excited to screen them together, as Murnau’s film was a direct visual inspiration on the animators of Fantasia’s “Night on Bald Mountain” sequence. The resemblances, as you’ll see, are uncanny.

Fantasia, screening at 1:00, has become one of the most beloved, influential films of all time. But when it was released in 1940, audiences were baffled. Set to works by Bach, Tchaikovsky, Beethoven, Stravinsky, and more, the wordless, exquisitely hand-drawn segments that make up this ambitious masterpiece constitute a high point in animation. Among the most marvelous sequences is its finale, a diptych of the profane and the sacred juxtaposing Modest Mussorgsky’s frenetic and terrifying “Night on Bald Mountain” and Franz Schubert’s ecstatic hymn “Ave Maria.” The former, set on the diabolical Walpurgis Night, is perhaps cinema’s greatest Halloween party. 

Faust, screening at 3:30, was released 14 years earlier, before Murnau emigrated to the U.S. from Germany. Here he adapts the classic German legend of an alchemist who makes a pact with the devil for eternal youth. This magnificent production—Murnau’s final German film before coming to Hollywood and directing Sunrise—is one of the great accomplishments of the legendary Ufa Studios in Berlin, anchored by a diabolical Emil Jannings as Mephisto. The film’s grand designs and special effects include such unforgettable sights as flying banshees and the devil spreading his wings (and the plague) over a small town (lifted for “Night on Bald Mountain”). This new restoration of the original German version of the film (with hand-painted intertitles) features a score compiled from historic photoplay music by the Mont Alto Motion Picture Orchestra.

See both films this Saturday, October 18! Fantasia also screens Sunday.
