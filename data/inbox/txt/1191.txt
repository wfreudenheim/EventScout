Dear readers, 

During their undergraduate studies, n+1 founding editors Chad Harbach and Keith Gessen took a class together on Herman Melville in which they were introduced to the concept of “unwriting”: not just revising, but unraveling a text to reveal what took shape beneath its initial structure. Gessen invoked the concept when he was interviewed for a new profile of Harbach in the Los Angeles Times, explaining the relationship between Harbach’s beloved first novel The Art of Fielding and his newly published followup, The Brightness: the second novel unwrites the first, reckoning with some of its more idealistic themes. “The characters in the book are sort of trapped and beguiled, and want to stay at Westish,” says Gessen. “And the book is like, you can’t.”

In The Brightness, Harbach revisits familiar territory with fresh eyes, focusing on the college’s young women in 2015, on the precipice of the first Trump presidency and #MeToo. Characters from The Art of Fielding return, but we see them from different angles. Even more satisfying is the way Harbach unwrites the cozy world of Westish, which reveals itself to be as divided as any real-life campus.

Whether you’ve spent the last fifteen years longing to return to Westish or you’re discovering Harbach’s work for the first time, The Brightness is the perfect late-fall reading. To quote from the profile: it’s a “big, sprawling, unapologetically old-fashioned social-realist novel, one that was worth the wait.” You can order it from our shop now. 

Yours,
The Editors

Praise for The Brightness

“Beautiful and perceptive… Passages like [his] are not only a joy to read―they convey something fundamental about people, who are always in conflict with themselves, managing a jumble of desires that pull in wildly different and sometimes mutually exclusive directions.”― The New Yorker

“[Harbach’s] stuff has a candor, simplicity and seriousness that puts you in mind of the work of Sherwood Anderson (born in Ohio), Willa Cather (raised in Nebraska) and Jonathan Franzen (born in Illinois).” — The New York Times

“You might think of this novel as a rebuttal or counterpoint to its forerunner, a book that replaces roseate campus fantasy with spikier societal conflicts . . . [Harbach’s] characterizations are generous and insightful.”― The Wall Street Journal

“Masterfully captures the dazzling ennui of being in your 20s.”― Marie Claire

“What may surprise readers this time out is the depth of feeling and insight Harbach brings to The Brightness’s female characters, especially Pella and Irma. The ache that sheltered, studious Irma feels to be closer to the wilder and more charismatic Dahlia rings with authenticity, as does the mix of pain, jealousy, and pride Pella experiences watching Irma come into her own.”― Publishers Weekly

“Brilliant, poignant, and absorbing.”―Kirkus Reviews (starred)
