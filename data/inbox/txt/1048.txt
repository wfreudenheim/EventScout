CAMERA MINISTRY: LENS-BASED ENCOUNTERS

----------------------------------------

Spend three days with photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah exploring the art of seeing, connecting, and making images.

Nov 13 — Nov 15 

Only a couple of spots remain! What happens when the camera becomes a way of getting closer to the world, rather than simply recording it? Join acclaimed photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah for a three-day intensive exploring the methods, instincts, and encounters that shape his extraordinary lens-based practice. From street photography and portraiture to poetic documentary, Khalik will share his approach to working with people, building trust, and following intuition. Through screenings, conversations, and hands-on exploration, participants will discover new possibilities for their own image-making, with the opportunity to share their work and receive direct creative feedback from Khalik.

 

Shevaun Mizrahi
Visiting Artist

Joining the workshop are two exceptional guest artists: filmmaker and cinematographer Shevaun Mizrahi, who will explore the interplay of magical realism and vérité in her filmmaking, and legendary photographer and archivist Jamil GS, whose portraits of Jay-Z, Nas, D’Angelo, Mary J. Blige, and A Tribe Called Quest helped define the visual culture of hip-hop in the 1990s. Jamil will reflect on photography as a form of cultural memory.

A RADICAL THREAD

----------------------------------------

An 83-foot tapestry, seventeen years in the making, traces a community’s history of environmental resistance.

Thursday, November 5 — 7:30PM

Jeanne C. Finley joins us for a screening of A Radical Thread, followed by a conversation with filmmaker Lynne Sachs! In the late 1960s, hundreds of families seeking an alternative to American consumer culture settled on California’s San Juan Ridge, building a community rooted in collective living and environmental stewardship. Over the decades, they organized against clear-cutting, river damming, and open-pit gold mining, building a remarkable legacy of resistance. The film traces this history through a tapestry by Marsha Stone and a collective of artists and community members. Weaving together the tapestry’s twelve embroidered panels, archival footage, and the voices of Ridge residents, Finley explores how histories of struggle, survival, and collective action can be preserved and passed on, even as climate-driven wildfires threaten the community’s future. UNDO SPOTLIGHT: A SHOWCASE OF SUPPORTED PROJECTS

----------------------------------------

Two nights celebrating bold new work from artists supported by UnionDocs’ Fiscal Sponsorship program.

Thursday, November 19 & Saturday, November 21 — 7:30PM

Come through for two special evenings celebrating the artists supported by our Fiscal Sponsorship program! Across both nights, this talented group of filmmakers and artists will be sharing their works in progress, spanning documentary, audio storytelling, experimental cinema, and beyond. These projects traverse remarkable terrain, from the fragile myths of American masculinity and the lives of queer men exiled under Italian Fascism to the hidden histories of labor, migration, and political resistance. They explore the politics of surveillance and technology, the shifting landscapes of cities and communities, and intimate questions of memory, identity, family, and belonging. Both evenings are FREE and feature the artists in attendance, so come through, discover some exciting new projects, and raise a glass to the brilliant minds dreaming them up!

All presenting artists are recipients of a 2026 Support for Artists grant, administered through UnionDocs’ Fiscal Sponsorship program and made possible by the New York State Council on the Arts with support from the Office of the Governor and the New York State Legislature.
