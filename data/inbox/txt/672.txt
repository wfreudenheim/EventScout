HOW BUDDY BRADLEY TRANSFORMED BALLET MOVEMENT 

Mon, Oct 5 | 6 PM | Library for the Performing Arts & Online
A panel of dance artists discuss the influence of the innovative jazz dancer and choreographer Buddy Bradley with live demonstrations. Iconic choreographers from George Balanchine to Agnes de Mille were among the many major artists who drew inspiration from his work. Some of Bradley and Balanchine's "creative synergies" will be displayed by the dancers in work such as Agon, Rubies, and Symphony in C.

BANNED BOOKS FROM THE COLD WAR TO THE PRESENT 

 
Thurs, Oct 8 | 6:30 PM | Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library (SNFL) & Online
Join Yasha Klots, Mark Lipovetsky, and Valeriia Korotkova of the Tamizdat Project for a conversation about smuggled literature that defied Soviet censorship, illuminating the enduring power of the printed word. In the Soviet Union, tamizdat (literally, "published 'over there,'" or abroad) was a term for manuscripts that were rejected, censored, or never submitted for publication but smuggled out from behind the Iron Curtain and printed elsewhere.
ATTEND ᐳ

AMERICAN METROPOLIS: THE WORKERS WHO BUILT MEXICO CITY

Fri, Oct 9 | 2 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
In her new book, American Metropolis: The Making of Mexico City, Rutgers University's Tatiana Seijas reimagines the city's history through the lives of the people who built and sustained it. Drawing on archival research and written during her time at the Library as a Cullman Center Fellow, Seijas brings together the stories of the countless workers who sold food, provided transportation and care, crafted goods, and supported the silver economy that fueled the region.

HOME TO HARLEM: CELEBRATING BLACK BIBLIOGRAPHY AT THE SCHOMBURG CENTER

Mon, Oct 19 | 1–5 PM | Schomburg Center 
Join the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture for a half-day symposium featuring panel discussions with archivists and scholars to reflect on the importance of Black bibliography and why it matters today. Get a behind-the-scenes look at how the Home to Harlem team used material evidence—bookplates, accession numbers, inscriptions, library stamps, card catalogs, and archival documents—to identify items in founder Arturo Schomburg's personal library or "seed" collection of work by and about Black people. Some items are now on view in To Uncover and Reveal to the World: Arturo Schomburg's Library, an exhibition that celebrates 100 years of the Schomburg Center. Co-presented by the Schomburg Center and the Bibliographical Society of America.

FORGING AHEAD: RECENT ACQUISITIONS OF BLACK WOMEN ARTISTS

Through Mar 27, 2027 | Schomburg Center 
Working across various media, the 21 artists featured in this exhibition engage wide-ranging themes including iconography of the Black woman’s body, storytelling, futurism, spirituality, and the mapping of Black history. These recent acquisitions acknowledge the importance of Black women artists and advance the Schomburg Center’s ability to represent, preserve, and share more dimensions of our expansive Black culture. Plus, register to join us for an opening reception at 6 PM—space is limited.

DECLARING AMERICA: 1776 AND BEYOND

Through Jan 10, 2027 | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
Spanning multiple galleries at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, Declaring America: 1776 and Beyond unpacks the complex, diverse, and powerful stories of the American Revolution—from 1776 to today. Featuring broadsides and posters, iconic founding documents, protest ephemera, powerful artworks, and artifacts from the Library's collections at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture, and the Library for the Performing Arts, the exhibition presents a diverse range of perspectives and invites visitors of all ages to explore our nation's remarkable beginnings, complex legacies, and promises fulfilled and unfulfilled.

UNLEASHED: PETS OF THE PERFORMING ARTS

Through May 8, 2027 | Library for the Performing Arts
Drawing from the rich archives of the three Library for the Performing Arts' divisions, Unleashed: Pets of the Performing Arts gives center stage to the animals behind the artists. From Billie Holiday's boxer Mister and Anna Pavlova's pet swan Jack to Larry Kramer's beloved hound Molly, animals have quietly shaped the lives and work of the world's most iconic performers. Beyond the stage, their quiet loyalty revealed a human tenderness in artists whose public personas often kept audiences at a distance.
