WHAT’S ON AT ASIA SOCIETY

October at Asia Society brings together a colorful mix of arts and culture programming from around the world. In the Museum, curator Xiaohan Du reflects on why art matters and the impetus for creating the inaugural Asia Society Museum Salon Series, launching this month. Additional programming features music by Chinese and Indian artists; films from Iran, Armenia, and Japan; a Pakistani literary festival; a night of standup comedy, and our monthly Leo Bar Happy Hour. 

IN THE MUSEUM

Asia Society Curator of Pre-modern Art Xiaohan Du. Photograph by Mengxing Wang

Curiosity towards the past constitutes a profound (and one of the most satisfying) aspects of being human. In satisfying that curiosity, one can read books, listen to podcasts, and watch documentaries; yet all of these pursuits constitute a heavily mediated access to the past. For example, very few people can read The Odyssey in ancient Greek and thus have to rely on modern translations. Not relying on layers of interpretations, art offers a singular access to the past that is simply unparalleled. As a physical object, it contains within itself visual and material clues to the passage of time and the terrains that it traversed to be in the here and the now. Great works of art are great because they reveal insights about creativity, about the civilizations that produced and treasured them, and, ultimately, about the human condition itself. Spending quality time with them has never disappointed anyone who knows how to look. 

This fall, Asia Society Museum is launching an inaugural museum salon series, in which we will get together with art historians and artists for intimate conversations about the stories that objects tell, and about how the obscure past continues to illuminate the contemporary moment. 

All who are curious are welcome!

— Xiaohan Du, John H. Foster Assistant Curator of Pre-Modern Asian Art

ART HISTORIES, ACROSS AND WITHIN: THE ROCKEFELLER COLLECTION AT ASIA SOCIETY

October 8 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Join Asia Society Curator Xiaohan Du and Arathi Menon, Associate Professor of Art History at Hamilton College and a specialist in the histories of South Asian art, for a conversation exploring the museum’s collection as a record of artistic exchange across Asia. Looking closely at selected objects, the program traces how ideas, techniques, and visual traditions traveled across regions while developing within their local contexts.

LAHORE LITERARY FESTIVAL IN NEW YORK

October 10 from 11:00 AM—6:00 PM (ET)

The Lahore Literary Festival (LLF), one of South Asia’s premier cultural events, returns to Asia Society New York, exploring contemporary Pakistan through artists, writers, and other experts. The festival will present American audiences with a more nuanced view of Pakistan, with discussions on history and politics, cities, cuisine, television and even tarot. This year's LLF speakers include Hazel Kahan, Sadia Shepard, Salima Hashmi, Cassim Shepard, Suketu Mehta, Ayeda Husain, Mehreen Jabbar, Sunita Kohli, Farwa Aamer, Felicia Kainat Norton and more!

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA: A CONVERSATION WITH T.M. KRISHNA

October 17 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Acclaimed Karnatik musician, author and public intellectual T.M. Krishna explores the historical, cultural, and philosophical contexts of India’s national symbols—the flag, the anthem, the emblem, the motto and the Preamble in his latest book We, the People of India: Decoding a Nation’s Symbols. In conversation with author and activist Ruchira Gupta, they will reflect on how these symbols have shaped the nation’s idea of citizenship, freedom and democracy.

OUR CENTURY: SPIRALING PATHS THROUGH ARMENIAN CINEMA

October 23—25, see website for film descriptions and showtimes

This series brings together five films by Armenian filmmakers working across different periods and diasporic contexts. Informed by a history of displacement, migration, and the search for homeland, the films approach travel as both a physical journey and a means of examining memory, identity, and belonging. Together, the films reveal how journeys that appear to lead away from home can also become a search for connection and return. 

Guest curated by Sona Karapoghosyan.
