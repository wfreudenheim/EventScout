Dear readers,

One of the hundreds of ads that aired during the 2026 World Cup depicted a DoorDash delivery driver interrupting a watch party before he’s ushered in to join the celebration. Liv Veazey spent this past summer with members of New York City’s Senegalese diaspora, who watched the games “just like the worker in the beginning of the ad, stitching it together in ten-minute intervals stolen between each delivery.” In the ad, the driver is greeted like a friend or a neighbor. In reality, other Senegal fans listen to the cheers from MetLife stadium through the windows of an ICE detainment center.

Also new online: Lauren O’Neill-Butler chronicles the MAGA takeover of her alma mater, the New College of Florida. In 2023, Florida governor Ron DeSantis “declared he was making NCF his pet project.” The results have been devastating for the school’s historically progressive culture of teaching and learning. “In May I spoke to a professor who described two schools at NCF,” writes O’Neill-Butler. “One is loud, issues nonstop press releases and op-eds, and requires a constant political audience; the other is quietly keeping the spirit of the college alive.” 

Last month, we published an excerpt from Adrian Daub’s What Tech Calls Governing, in which Daub details the opacity and ubiquity of AI billboards in the Bay Area. Daub is especially bewildered by one that reads “Your AI Agent Needs A Workflow Engine.” “These are the least solicitous billboards imaginable,” Daub writes, “solitaires throning over the highway, haughty and without any desire to be understood.” 

Finally, on the other side of the country: Dennis M. Hogan reflects on the primary win of David Morales, the Democratic nominee for mayor of Providence, a source of tenuous hope. Morales opens his campaign communications with the phrase “Hi neighbors!” a greeting that, Hogan writes, “invokes a kind of universality. We are all neighbors here, and who’s to say where the neighborhood stops?” 

Yours,
The Editors
