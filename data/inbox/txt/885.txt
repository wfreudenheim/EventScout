Trust & Safety
Artistic Expression vs. Platform Censorship

Tuesday, November 10, 2026
7:00pm
Electronic Arts Intermix (EAI)
264 Canal St #3W
New York, NY, 10013

RSVP

Electronic Arts Intermix (EAI) and Rhizome present Trust & Safety, a screening and conversation addressing the challenges faced by organizations entrusted with preserving artworks that test cultural, technological, and institutional limits.

The program features a selection of works from EAI’s collection by Peggy Ahwesh, Alex Bag, Ellen Cantor, Barbara Hammer, and Muntadas. The works were selected in relation to Vimeo’s content moderation policies and the limitations those policies pose for an archive of challenging media. After EAI encountered repeated problems hosting works on Vimeo, Rhizome assisted with the migration of EAI’s video holdings to PeerTube, an open-source platform that allows organizations to maintain greater control over their digital infrastructure.

Following the screening, Rebecca Cleman of EAI and Michael Connor of Rhizome will discuss the formation of alternative systems for distribution and access.

Artists and independent media organizations have long developed their own channels of circulation in response to cultural and institutional barriers. Histories of queer experimental media are especially bound up with these challenges. Artists created alternative networks for engaging sexuality, identity, and desire at moments of stringent cultural suppression. Today, commercial platforms apply standardized moderation systems to material whose meaning requires crucial historic context.

Trust & Safety considers how arts organizations build and maintain their own technological infrastructure, and how federated systems such as PeerTube might support new networks for the circulation of challenging work.

About EAI
Electronic Arts Intermix is a nonprofit resource that fosters the creation, exhibition, distribution and preservation of media art. Founded in 1971 by gallerist Howard Wise, EAI offered an alternative paradigm to support the burgeoning medium of video. EAI's founding principles of access, community, and experimentation remain cornerstones of the work we do.

Accessibility
EAI’s venue is located at 264 Canal Street, 3W, near several Canal Street subway stations. Our floor is accessible by elevator (63" × 60" car, 31" door) and stairway. Due to the age and other characteristics of the building, our bathrooms are not ADA-accessible, though several such bathrooms are located nearby.
