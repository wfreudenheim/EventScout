Did you know that the Japanese word for painting, え (e), is one of the shortest vocabulary words you’ll learn? 🎨

When written in kanji (絵), it combines the radical for thread (糸) with a character meaning joining (会)—evoking the image of threads woven together to create a masterpiece!

Want to learn more about foundational Japanese? Check out our virtual Japanese for Teens classes this fall! 🍁

🗣️ Home & Habits (Oct. 8–Dec. 17): Learn to talk about daily routines and household items.
🗣️ Family, Favorite Things & Me (Oct. 6–Dec. 15): Practice describing objects, family members, and personal spaces.
✒️ Basic Kanji (Oct. 7–Dec. 16): Master basic kanji characters through interactive writing practice.

Remembering the legendary visionary, Yayoi Kusama (b. 1929).

After moving to New York in 1958, Kusama channeled her anxiety into her iconic "Infinity Net" paintings—a practice of infinitely repeating patterns she called "obsessive art".

During those foundational years in the city, Japan Society was honored to support her journey. In the early 1960s, she gifted us her 1961 painting "Net S.P." in gratitude for our assistance with her U.S. visa. We were further privileged to support her through a 1965–1966 Fellowship, which helped her exhibit and travel across the country.

"Net S.P." remains a treasured piece of our institutional history and a testament to her enduring genius.

🧑🎨 Yayoi Kusama, "Net S.P.", 1961. Oil on canvas, 29 1/2 x 35 1/2 in. (74.9 x 90.2 cm). Japan Society JS 12.200; gift of the artist.
📸 Reiko Tomii. Kusama’s "Net S.P." installed in Japan Society’s lobby, April 2007.
