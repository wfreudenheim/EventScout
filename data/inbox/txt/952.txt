Support The Kitchen

 

 

Dear Friend,

This spring, I brought Swerve Fatigue to The Kitchen’s loft at Westbeth. I brought people I love and trust. We came to work. Dance, sound, film. Each of us carrying a history, each of us implicated in what happened in that room. 

I am still thinking about what it takes to get us there. 

The schedules. The money. The hours spent attending to something nobody can see yet. The particular exhaustion of trying to make a work while also making the conditions for it to exist. And then rehearsal. Someone does something that changes everything. You have to be there to catch it. You have to have the time. 

The Kitchen gave us time.

Donate

I care about the floor. Who gets to work on it. What it costs them to be there. Whether they can return tomorrow and take the thing further. I care about the people who stay with a work as it becomes more demanding, who listen closely enough to recognize that the question has changed.

This is how support enters a dance. It enters the bodies. It changes what we can ask of one another.

The Kitchen carries a history I feel in my own work. Artists have spent years here making demands on form, on perception, on the institution hosting them. I have learned from those demands. I am still learning. There is permission in that history, and there is pressure. What will you do with the room now that you have it?

With Swerve Fatigue, we worked on that question together. My collaborators brought their attention, their intelligence, their lives. The work belongs to that encounter. The Kitchen helped us sustain it long enough to bring other people in.

I want a New York where this remains possible. Where an artist can pursue something difficult and find people willing to get involved. Where we can afford to gather, rehearse, argue, change our minds. Where the work can get stranger as we get closer to it.

For me, downtown has always carried that promise between people. I want to be accountable to it. Especially now, when every hour and every square foot is being priced beyond our reach.

Historic ground needs people working on it. It needs the next artist, with a question that puts the whole place to work again. Keeping that possibility alive takes wages, equipment, rehearsal hours, and a community willing to contribute.

Please give what you can to The Kitchen. I want us to keep meeting here. I want to see what happens next.

With love,

Jonathan González
