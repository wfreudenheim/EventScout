Join us this Saturday, November 7, at 3pm, for a screening of With an Acre, presented in collaboration with the Canadian Centre for Architecture. Director Joshua Frank will introduce the film, followed by a conversation with Francesco Garutti, Ella den Elzen, Lukas Brasiskis, and Nick Axel.

The arabica coffee plant was introduced to Brazil three hundred years ago and, through the exploitation of enslaved then immigrant labor, grew into an industry that takes up two million hectares of land today. Centuries of extractive culture have not only devastated the environment but entrenched social inequities. Unpredictable and extreme weather events are further threatening livelihoods in the countryside. Despite a brutal history and ever-precarious present, there is a movement in the country towards regenerative agriculture and reforestation.

With an Acre follows architect Carla Juaçaba as she develops a museum and community space in solidarity with Flor de Café, a collective of smallholder farmers in Minas Gerais. Inspired by the temporality of Indigenous collective structures and the form of highway billboards that mark the extended rural landscape in the region, Juaçaba proposes a landmark on an elevated plantation overlooking the town of Nepomuceno. Light and tactical construction will provide a space for sharing knowledge, offering a symbolic stage of resistance while leaving a minimal trace on the earth. The pavilions will frame views of the region, but more important will set a vision for the future of the territory.

With an Acre — An Afternoon with the CCA
Screening and conversation
Saturday, November 07, 2026, 3pm

Free admission

Join the Canadian Centre for Architecture (CCA) as it returns to e-flux with an afternoon of film and conversation featuring a screening of With an Acre, the third chapter in the Groundwork series conceived and produced by the CCA. The film follows architect Carla Juaçaba as she develops a museum and community space in solidarity with a collective of smallholder farmers in Minas Gerais, Brazil, resisting extractive industrial agriculture. The film will be introduced by director Joshua Frank, and followed by a conversation with e-flux Curator of Film and Video Lukas Brasiskis, e-flux Architecture Deputy Editor Nick Axel, CCA Associate Director, Programs Francesco Garutti, and curator and writer Ella den Elzen, exploring the use of film as a tool for curatorial research.
