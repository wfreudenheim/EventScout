FILMMAKER IN PERSON!
VACANCES
October 9 – 13
A strange and unusual mixture of warts-and-all family portraiture, subtle exposé of class and privilege, and minimalist depiction of a particular setting that’s rendered with an almost miraculously tactile sense of presence, photographer and filmmaker Victoria Hely-Hutchinson’s VACANCES is a deceptively unassuming film. Filmed over the course of a decade at the property in Provence inhabited by Hely-Hutchinson’s outrageously uninhibited grandmother, a bohemian Austro-Hungarian countess and family matriarch, VACANCES grew out of Hely-Hutchinson’s 2016 short film of the same name, and is something of a filmic complement to her autobiographical 2023 book, “I Left My Grandmother’s House”. The documentary delves, with a grace and open-endedness that belies its analytical bite, into both the bigger-than-life personality of “granny” as well as the sometimes strained dynamics among the rest of the family, including Hely-Hutchinson’s grandfather, mother, and siblings. Unique in suggesting by turns cinema vérité, reality TV, chamber theater, and the minimalism of landscape cinema, VACANCES is a profoundly singular work – a film whose technique is nearly invisible, but which sneaks up on you with its trenchant perceptions, layered meanings, and emotional and filmic complexity.

“This time-lapse film, shot over a period of ten years, follows members of several generations of a wealthy aristocratic family with Austro-Hungarian roots. The entire film is set in a Provençal villa, whose social heart is the swimming pool, around which the women gather to exchange various observations and, surreptitiously, gossip. The center of the family is the aging matriarch, who likes to talk about her extravagant, promiscuous life, but at the same time commands all the attention and authority of those around her. However, her influence gradually weakens with each passing generation.” –JI.HLAVA INTERNATIONAL DOCUMENTARY FILM FESTIVAL

THE CINEMA OF GENDER TRANSGRESSION
October 8 – 11
An extensive, ongoing series exploring the ways in which cinema has intersected with the experiences, struggles, and ideas of transgender, non-binary, and gender-nonconforming lives and communities, “The Cinema of Gender Transgression” showcases contemporary films and videos that explore concepts related to gender transgression as well as films that have had historical resonance within and beyond these communities.

Transgender and gender-nonconforming issues hold a more prominent place in the national conversation today than ever before, though gender transgression and the experiences of these individuals have always existed. This series aims to call attention to those films that dared to question gender norms in periods when even to broach the topic was considered controversial, and to provide a platform for the contemporary socio-political concerns of trans filmmakers and artists challenging the gender binary today.

The October chapter features a retrospective of Berlin-based multi-disciplinary artist Vika Kirchenbauer, in two short film programs organized by Angelo Madsen. Vika’s expansive work exploring the politics of labor, performance, language, and art history over the past two decades has distinguished her practice as rigorous, perplexing, and thoroughly singular. Kirchenbauer will be in attendance to speak with Angelo Madsen following both programs. Later in the weekend, “The Cinema of Gender Transgression” will continue with an in-person appearance from Annie Sprinkle & Beth Stephens, who will present the NYC premiere screenings of the third film in their climate crisis trilogy: PLAYING WITH FIRE: AN ECOSEXUAL EMERGENCY (2025).

“The Cinema of Gender Transgression” is guest-curated by Joey Carducci and Angelo Madsen.

VIKA KIRCHENBAUER: PROGRAM 1
We enter this program with a salacious direct address-cum-invitation in PLEASE RELAX NOW, as Kirchenbauer seduces the viewer from a black box void, stretching the edges of imagination. In LIKE RATS LEAVING A SINKING SHIP, Kirchenbauer layers psychiatric assessments to consider the coherency of a biography, and assess the ways in which individual memory is puppeted by state apparatuses. Both SHE WHOSE BLOOD IS CLOTTING IN MY UNDERWEAR and MOOD MANAGEMENT again push the viewer’s capacity to render a coherent picture of actions, generating a half-constructed image which lends itself only then to a projection. Both works are guided by Kirchenbauer’s music project, COOL FOR YOU. Through essay and material vignettes, UNTITLED SEQUENCE OF GAPS explores light outside of the visible spectrum to consider violence as it attaches to different kinds of visibility and invisibility. To tickle is a form of touch, an intimate act carried out to delight, to excite, or to torment. In THE ISLAND OF PERPETUAL TICKLING (III), Kirchenbauer surrenders to this metaphoric and literal tickle. Noting that it is impossible to tickle oneself, her task requires assistance.

COMPASSION AND INCONVENIENCE

VIKA KIRCHENBAUER: PROGRAM 2
In the second program of our 2-part retrospective devoted to Vika Kirchenbauer, we dive deeper into the politics of labor in contemporary art, as the artist offers us a WELCOME ADDRESS, directly calling into question the art economy’s coded-progressive positioning of difference and diversity as symbolic cultural currency. Considering the foul, late-stage capitalist “Experience Economy,” YOU ARE BORING troubles the nature of looking. THE CAPACITY FOR ADEQUATE ANGER positions still images from the artist’s presumable childhood archive, gaps of nothingness, and the essay form to offer a critique of the production of contemporary art in the face of upward mobility, mapping subjectivity and political agency. To close this program with a thorough and considered look at the complicit role of artists and art institutions, COMPASSION AND INCONVENIENCE takes us back to 18th century London to trace the first public exhibitions of contemporary art, when materiality intersects with ideology via Liberalism and Enlightenment, taking to task the economic and colonial interests that define/d the “pursuit of refinement.”

Annie Sprinkle & Beth Stephens
PLAYING WITH FIRE: AN ECOSEXUAL EMERGENCY
2025, 71 min, DCP. Co-sponsored by Climate Film Festival and Queer New York International Arts Festival.
Following their two previous films – GOODBYE GAULEY MOUNTAIN: AN ECOSEXUAL LOVE STORY (2014) and WATER MAKES US WET: AN ECOSEXUAL ADVENTURE (2019) – Annie Sprinkle and Beth Stephens bring us the third chapter in their queer environmental doc film trilogy: PLAYING WITH FIRE: AN ECOSEXUAL EMERGENCY. With the climate crisis worsening with each passing season, the artist/activist duo sound the alarm by enlisting a collective of artists, elders, witches, neighbors, sex workers, and friends, to examine the ways we can support the health of our dear Earth, cope with catastrophic events, and blend loss and grief into ecosexual art and transformation. Partially narrated by Boulder Creek’s mythical white peacock Albert and culminating in a ceremonial Wedding to Fire in the Redwoods, PLAYING WITH FIRE is a powerful portrait of queer resilience but also a reckoning with the power of fire, with its capacity to heal and its potential to destroy.

WOMEN IN ANIMATION
October 11 – 15
By no means an exhaustive history, this series attempts to chronicle the many talents of women working in animation. Lost to time until recently, FLOWER FAIRIES by Bessie Mae Kelley is the earliest-known hand-drawn animated film directed by a woman. Other women artists who worked within the animation studio system were largely uncredited for their contributions, while still others collaborated with male colleagues to create their own studios, such as Joy Batchelor & John Halas (represented here by THE OWL AND THE PUSSYCAT) and Morton & Millie Goldsholl (INTERGALACTIC ZOO).

Program 1 of the series focuses on family-friendly animations depicting the animal kingdom. Program 2 features animations that reflect on a diversity of experiences of womanhood, from being idolized (THE FIGUREHEAD) to being a mother (BIRTH and PETIT BONHEUR) to being a woman of the world (W.O.W). Program 3, co-presented with the animation collective Sunflower Lane, encompasses personal stories of reproductive health (CYCLES), meditations on existence (MY UNIVERSE INSIDE AND OUT), and an animated interview about female friendship (INTERVIEW).

Guest-programmed by Rachael Guma. The series is co-presented by WIA.

MY NAME AIN'T SUZIE

AVATARS OF THE WHORE
October 2 – 13
In her book “Puta Life”, Juana María Rodríguez writes that the whore embodies the prurient fascination of society’s anxieties around money, sex, and power, which often reveal themselves in filmic representations of people working sex. These representations contribute to the common and tired tropes which inevitably depict people who sell sex – specifically women – as “trafficked” and as possessing loose “morals.” These representations are informed by a global oppression of women that positions sex along a private/public binary, leaving accounts of those selling sex to act as the confessional truth of what sex is like for women. These hollow depictions position whores as vessels for male desire rather than as artisans using the craft of sex to achieve different economic and spiritual goals for themselves.

Taking this ambiguity of the sex trade as its focus, the second iteration of “Avatars of the Whore” (previously titled “Women, Workers, and Whores on Film”) examines a wide range of women working sex across the club, dungeon, street, and bayou to meet multifaceted ends that include breaking a curse, negotiating childcare, seeking erotic enlightenment, and so on. The films gathered here reflect a diversity of experiences of working sex – through the various avatars of the profession – that combat the stigmatization of being a “whore” in society.

Guest-programmed by Ayanna Dozier, who wrote the introduction and individual film descriptions.

Angie Chen
MY NAME AIN’T SUZIE / FA GAI SI DOI
1985, 96 min, 35mm-to-DCP. In Cantonese and Mandarin with English subtitles.
Positioned as a rebuke to the colonial fantasy of THE WORLD OF SUZIE WONG (1960), MY NAME AIN’T SUZIE spans three decades of the life of a girl from a poor fishing village who makes a name for herself as a courtesan in the Wan Chai red light district in Hong Kong. Over the course of the film, MY NAME AIN’T SUZIE confronts and redresses the occidental fantasies of the East through the protagonists’ interactions with her clients.

SEDUCTION: THE CRUEL WOMAN / VERFÜHRUNG – DIE GRAUSAME FRAU
1985, 84 min, 16mm-to-DCP. In German with English subtitles.
In SEDUCTION: THE CRUEL WOMAN, Wanda is a dominatrix and gallerist who invites patrons to witness her scenes with her submissives. Inspired by the Marquis de Sade’s “Venus in Furs”, writer-directors Monika Treut and Elfi Mikesch spin Sade’s tale of power, apathy, and pleasure to foreground the role the spectator (both imagined and real) plays in enabling these violating acts to occur.

THE DOMINATRIX SLEEPS TONIGHT
Taking the avatar of the dominatrix as its starting point, this short film program gathers together various films made – across a 40-year period – by or in collaboration with those who have experience in the leathersex scene. As an avatar of working sex, the figure of the dominatrix is elusive in so far as she uses the theater of the scene of “violation” to both pursue and obfuscate her desires. Though cinema longs to unmask the dominatrix to witness her “true” self, this program instead finds pleasure in the artifice of the scene wherein various characters find themselves at the mercy of the seductive and controlling gaze of the dominatrix. The shorts here range from documentary exercises of sadism, like Ione Wang’s WHEN I LOOK AT YOU, I SEE ME, to the electric pop-music video work of Beth B’s THE DOMINATRIX SLEEPS TONIGHT, made for the band Dominatrix. Shorts by Azian Nurudin and Michelle Handelman from the late 1980s and early 1990s invite a reconsideration of race, power, and aesthetics through their respectively overlooked and censored films, while M.M. Serra and Maria Beatty’s work demonstrates the aesthetic bliss that emerges from submitting to the fantasies of a powerful woman.

ODDS AND ENDS

COMBAT BREATHING
October 1 – 8
“Combat Breathing Vol. II: A Symposium on Aesthetics and Politics” is a multisite platform, spanning film screenings, critical discussions, and focused workshops that unfolds throughout October 2026 at Anthology Film Archives, New York University, and Wendy’s Subway. Responding to the current geopolitical juncture, it asks what roles artistic and intellectual production, circulation, and collective study might assume amid intensifying imperial violence and renewed global mobilizations. In an era when audiovisual technologies are increasingly deployed to discipline collective imagination and stabilize statist and capitalist power, the symposium responds by foregrounding insurgent poetics, militant pedagogy, and autonomous infrastructures of media production, distribution, and reception.

Drawing on the histories of Third Cinema and other anti-colonial and anti-imperial cultural movements, the film program at Anthology Film Archives considers how these traditions persist, mutate, and reemerge in the present. Organized into six chapters comprising twenty-seven films, “Combat Breathing” explores the convergences of aesthetics and politics: how they take form on screen; where the militant image resides today; how images index and reveal infrastructures of labor; the pervasiveness of and resistance to carceral logics; the strategic reappropriations of imaging technologies; and film’s capacity to institute and reorganize desire.

Curated by KJ Abudu and Adam HajYahia.

For more information, visit the Combat Breathing website.

READ MY DESIRE
Social orders are resisted and overturned not merely because the dominant classes create unlivable conditions for the dominated classes, but also because the dominated classes actively and transgenerationally imagine, prefigure, and desire the realization of alternative social relations that cannot be accommodated by the present order. Such disruptive, exorbitant, and irrepressible desires – often structurally designated as queer – are the focus of this program. “Read My Desire” invokes the work of philosopher Joan Copjec and echoes her formulations that desire precisely emerges from the divisions within the subject which cannot be reduced to social identity or captured by historical regimes.

Bringing together historical and contemporary works to explore cinema’s desire-producing capacity as well as the often-elided place of desire, love, eroticism, and sensuality in social movements and associated streams of cultural production, the program traverses multiple geographies and genres, including sci-fi, music video, mythical fable, narrative drama, parodic documentary, and experimental film. The selected works by Marlon Riggs, Valentin Noujaïm, Michelle Parkerson, Peng Zuqiang, Jumana Manna, and Naomi Rincon Gallardo theorize the function of desire in reorienting the horizons of liberation struggles and affirming subjectivities at continual risk of erasure.

COMING SOON:
• NARROW ROOMS: LES FANTÔMES DU HARD + FISTULE
October 15
Most kinky queers in NYC know all about the Mineshaft, the legendary, long-shuttered sex club in the Meatpacking District that was in operation from 1976 to 1985. But across the Atlantic, France’s first “hard” gay BDSM sex club was also called Mineshaft, and it has remained a sacred space for fetish sex in Marseille since its opening in 1967. In the two-part LES FANTÔMES DU HARD, director Lazare Lazarus and friends excavate the archive left by Mineshaft founder Jean-Pierre Fouque. Chapter 1 tells the story of the squatters who took over Fouque’s apartment after his death in 2022, while chapter 2, co-directed with Lupa Charon Gateff, uses the club's archive of gay porn and BDSM videotapes to form a found-footage experimental porno mixtape. These two shorts will be followed by Emre Busse’s FISTULE, which invites the audience into a realm where an anal fissure serves as a dramatic springboard, exposing the absurdity of societal norms with a mix of tragedy, camp, and sexuality.

• SEXUAL CINÉMA VÉRITÉ: THE MULTI-MEDIA RESOURCE CENTER IN CONTEXT
October 16 – 20
In 1968, a coalition of sex-positive, politically progressive San Francisco religious leaders and homophile activists founded the National Sex Forum. Led by the Methodist Rev. Dr. Ted McIlvenna, the NSF began producing films, and was soon supplemented by a distribution arm called the Multi-Media Resource Center. The MMRC distributed both NSF films – most of them directed by McIlvenna’s friend Laird Sutton – and erotic avant-garde cinema by filmmakers from the Bay Area and beyond. The MMRC was radical not only in its ethos of sexual freedom and openness as compatible with Christianity, but also in its use of experimental film as a form of sex education. This series, guest-programmed by Lucy Talbot Allen, will introduce a modern audience to a host of works that have rarely (if ever) been shown theatrically. In these programs, films distributed by the MMRC are paired with those that could have been: avant-garde, educational, and agit-prop films from the 1960s and ’70s that share a sensibility of free sexual expression.

• ROSALYN DREXLER: WHO DOES SHE THINK SHE IS?
October 22
Rosalyn Drexler (1926-2025) was a category-defying creative force. Over the course of her multidecade career, she worked as a pioneering Pop art painter, a novelist, an Obie-award-winning playwright, a film critic, an occasional nightclub singer, and briefly, a professional wrestler. The documentary WHO DOES SHE THINK SHE IS? (1975) provides a window into Drexler’s extraordinary creative universe. Set against the backdrop of the lost artistic demimonde of 20th-century New York, the film – directed by Patricia Lewis Jaffe and Gaby Rodgers – shows the artist rehearsing her madcap cabaret performances, cooking for her family in her art-strewn Manhattan apartment, and speaking candidly about her art, writing, and life. The documentary will be accompanied by never-before-seen home movies shot by Drexler and her husband Sherman in the 1940-50s, as well as a panel discussion. The screening coincides with a major exhibition of her work from the 1960s at Garth Greenan Gallery.
