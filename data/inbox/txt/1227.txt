Dedicated to the 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice, this year's VLC Forum brought together artists, scholars, curators, and technologists from around the globe. Together, we explored the topic of intelligence in all its relational, generative, political, and creative dimensions, while celebrating artist Rosana Paulino, recipient of the Jane Lombard Prize, alongside the Jane Lombard Fellows: ArTree Nepal, Stephanie Dinkins, Jason Edward Lewis, and Jenna Sutela.

As always, the VLC Forum is free and open to all. It is not, however, free to produce. Help sustain our work into the future by purchasing the newly commissioned VLC tote bag, with original artwork by Kite, or consider joining our membership circle Vera’s List.

The Forum kicked off on October 9 with the opening of Paulino's prize exhibition Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?, anchored by her critical examinations of the entanglements between science, race, knowledge, and power in Brazil and throughout the African diaspora. The exhibition hosted the four-part Black Feminist Study Group, with participants OlaRonke Akinmowo, Zoë Hopkins, Jessica Lynne, and Cresa Pugh. 

For the Jane Lombard Prize convocation, Paulino was joined in conversation by Denise Ferreira da Silva, Diane Lima, and exhibition co-curator Carin Kuoni. Focusing on Paulino’s artist book ¿História Natural?, they explored processes of undoing colonial violence while subverting human and non-human hierarchies.

The annual VLC Community Dinner (AKA the "alt gala"), Dream Futures, featured Stephanie Dinkins's Campfire Cards—playful provocations on AI. Oglála Lakȟóta artist Kite’s performance Tȟokátakiya Owótȟaŋla na Awíyuškiŋphiča [A Just and Joyful Future] (For the Vera List Center), with Robbie Wing and an ensemble of New School students, reflected on collective dreaming and shared futurity. 

The dinner also honored Carin Kuoni, our recently departed director, for her 22 years of visionary leadership at the VLC, with remarks by board chair JK Brown.

Prize jury chair Chus Martínez opened day two with a keynote lecture on exhibition making as relational intelligence. Conversations with the Jane Lombard Fellows on their prize-nominated projects rounded out the Forum: Jenna Sutela and Elvia Wilk discussed Sutela’s nimiia cétiï, considering new forms of communication within biological organisms and neural networks. Stephanie Dinkins and Neta Bomani reflected on technological future histories in Dinkins’s ongoing Conversations with Bina48.

Jason Edward Lewis and Mashinka Hakopian spoke about the Indigenous-led, anticolonial AI research program Abundant Intelligences. And ArTree Nepal’s Lavkant Chaudhary, Mekh Limbu, and Subas Tamang joined Prabal Gurung in a conversation on Tika Chhedna Angana (“A Garden of Body Markings”)—a project on Indigenous tattooing practices as embodied knowledge.

If you missed out on the program, read the newly published catalogue that accompanies the VLC Forum, featuring artworks from Rosana Paulino's ¿História Natural? with writing on the project by Diane Lima and Lorraine Leu; a conversation between the artist and curators Carin Kuoni and Eriola Pira; and a prelude to the Black Feminist Study Group and reading list by Re’al Christian, along with essays on the Jane Lombard Fellows by Neta Bomani, Mashinka Hakopian, Suzanne Livingston, and Vivian Ziherl. Pick up a free copy or find the digital version on our website. And read the conversation with Paulino on the exhibition and ¿História Natural? in Hyperallergic. 

The VLC Forum 2026 would not have been possible without the generous support of Jane Lombard, with additional support from Critical Minded, the Finnish Cultural Institute in New York, the Barbara Jordan Lectures: The State of Democracy fund, the David Randolph Distinguished Artist-in-Residence endowment, and the Helen Shapiro Lectureship fund. 

Thank you again for joining us for this year's VLC Forum, and stay tuned for more Matter of Intelligence programming in the coming months!
