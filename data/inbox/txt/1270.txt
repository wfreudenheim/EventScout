Bridging the Gap Between Scientific Discovery & Venture Capital

How do scientific breakthroughs become investable ventures?

The New York Academy of Sciences and RoundZero present PhD to VC: Translating Scientific Breakthroughs into Investable Ventures, a four-part course introducing researchers to the fundamentals of venture capital, startup evaluation, and science commercialization.

Through expert conversations and practical lessons, participants will learn how investors assess early-stage science startups, explore funding pathways, and discover opportunities to engage with the venture ecosystem.

Designed for graduate students, postdocs, early-career researchers, and aspiring scientific founders, no prior experience in finance or entrepreneurship is required. The course runs January 25–March 8, 2027.

2026 Tata Transformation Prize Recognizes Three Scientists Advancing Solutions to Global Challenges

The New York Academy of Sciences and Tata Sons have announced the winners of the 2026 Tata Transformation Prize, recognizing three exceptional scientists in India whose research addresses critical challenges in food security, sustainability, and healthcare.

Congratulations to Lakshminarayana Rao, PhD, of the Indian Institute of Science, whose work on plasma-activated water aims to provide a more sustainable alternative to conventional fertilizers; Amol Kulkarni, PhD, of CSIR-National Chemical Laboratory, who is developing solvent-free chemical manufacturing technologies to reduce industrial waste and pollution; and Colin Jamora, PhD, of Shiv Nadar Institution of Eminence, who is advancing a regenerative treatment for diabetic foot ulcers.

Each winner will receive INR 2 crores to advance their research and accelerate the development of real-world applications that could benefit communities in India and beyond. The winners will be honored at an awards ceremony in Mumbai in December 2026.

What Does Human Inquiry Mean in the Age of AI?

In a new opinion piece for the Financial Times, Nicholas B. Dirks, President and CEO of The New York Academy of Sciences, examines how artificial intelligence is reshaping our understanding of human knowledge and the future of learning.

As AI reshapes how knowledge is produced and understood, Dirks considers what this means for universities, research institutions, and the future of learning. He argues that knowledge-based institutions must rethink their roles in an AI-driven world, emphasizing the importance of human judgment in determining how these technologies are developed and used.

#31 David Krakauer on Complexity Theory, Skepticism about Machine Intelligence, and Reimagining Institutions in the Age of AI

David Krakauer is an evolutionary theorist and President of the Santa Fe Institute, the pioneering research center devoted to the science of complex systems.

Krakauer, who also serves as William H. Miller Professor of Complex Systems at the Santa Fe Institute, studies some of the biggest questions in science: how intelligence evolved, how ideas themselves evolve, and what it even means for something – a cell, a brain, a society, a machine – to be a “problem-solving” system.
