EAI Celebrates 55 Years!

Dear friends of EAI,

This year, EAI turns 55. Longevity like ours requires introspection to meet a moving moment.

Video has never been more abundant. Social media places an extraordinary volume before us, but much of it circulates without an archive or history. Its purpose rarely extends beyond immediate engagement. These images appear separated from whoever made them and the conditions in which they were produced.

EAI was founded amid a different kind of abundance: the daily television broadcast and the arrival of portable video equipment. Over time, that abundance became an onslaught. Just as it did at its founding, EAI provides a context-rich haven for artists’ video and media art. We have preserved and distributed artists’ moving-image and media work, giving it a durable cultural life. In an art world shaped by market pressures, EAI remains independent and artist-centered, committed to experimentation and long-term access.

Support EAI

Contemporary digital tools offer an unprecedented ability to generate and circulate images, but their underlying systems are increasingly difficult to examine. Technical knowledge and computing resources are concentrated within industries driven by commercial growth. Artists are encouraged to use powerful technologies without the access or understanding needed to question how they operate.

EAI sustains a space between technological development and cultural understanding. We preserve the histories of artists who challenged earlier media systems while supporting those confronting the present. Moving images should remain available for critical study, beyond the short life cycles of the attention economy. Our ongoing digitization service preserves hundreds of hours of physical media. The collection holds over 5,000 media works, distributed to tens of thousands of viewers all over the world, while thousands of students access it through our Educational Streaming Service.

As our fiscal year closes on September 30th, we ask you to support our work with a tax-deductible donation. A contribution of any size helps EAI provide the continuity and technical care that moving-image art needs to endure, historically grounded and publicly accessible.

Thank you for being part of EAI’s community and for helping to sustain a space where artists can continue to experiment and move the medium forward.

Rebecca Cleman
Executive Director

About EAI
Electronic Arts Intermix is a nonprofit resource that fosters the creation, exhibition, distribution and preservation of media art. Founded in 1971 by gallerist Howard Wise, EAI offered an alternative paradigm to support the burgeoning medium of video. EAI's founding principles of access, community, and experimentation remain cornerstones of the work we do.
