Pélagie Gbaguidi: Iteration

Pélagie Gbaguidi, Entanglement, 2026. Pigment on canvas, 154 x 107 cm.

PÉLAGIE GBAGUIDI ITERATION

November 25, 2026–January 23, 2027

Tim Van Laere Gallery
Jos Smolderenstraat 50
Antwerp, 2000
Belgium

www.timvanlaeregallery.com
Instagram / Facebook

Tim Van Laere Gallery presents Pélagie Gbaguidi’s first solo exhibition since their collaboration began earlier this year. Born in Dakar, the artist has lived and worked in Brussels for 25 years. She describes herself as a contemporary griot or storyteller: “I see it this way: all our bodies are living archives, and my creative process involves articulating our relationship with the world.” Through archives, documents, conversations and lieux de mémoire, her body becomes a seismograph, transforming emotions and experiences of injustice into a multifaceted poetic language.

Central to the exhibition is The Architecture of Courage (2026), commissioned by HKW—Haus der Kulturen der Welt in Berlin for Tirailleurs: Trials and Tribulations, an exhibition exploring the role of African soldiers during the First and Second World Wars. Gbaguidi’s research focused on the contributions of women and mothers in military camps and on the front lines. Across seven panels, she pays tribute to the strength, courage and solidarity of women in wartime, past and present. By painting and naming them, she restores their voices: the woman sounding the alarm with a drum, the woman who deserts, the woman whose skin forms protective cactus spines, and the Agojie warriors of the Kingdom of Dahomey. On the reverse of the canvases, she created collages using handwoven Senegalese textiles whose patterns also carry social, cultural and spiritual significance.

Gbaguidi’s imagery often draws on archival images, photographs and texts. Her drawing series Naked Writing emerged from months of visits to the AfricaMuseum archives. Some images she records on site; others settle in her memory, resurfacing years later. The new painting Marches brings together several events: the 1949 anticolonial women’s uprising in Grand-Bassam, Côte d’Ivoire, Martin Luther King’s marches and recent uprisings in Europe. It also relates to La Marche, approximately 400 drawings made on Réunion Island in 1999. The drawn feet and legs form a collective march, emphasising the importance of togetherness.

Gueules Bleues depicts women working as lamp attendants in coal mines. This industry was crucial to Belgium and continues to underpin precarious working and living conditions across several continents. The work also addresses the informal or underground economy, human exploitation and invisible labour.

In Home, Gbaguidi connects the nineteenth-century botanical garden with the ubiquitous Monstera houseplant, a domesticated version of a colonial concept. The painting questions acclimatisation and our response to global warming: do we adapt, as plants were forced to, or opt for a paradigm shift? At the same time, the title suggests that nature can offer refuge. Holes in the canvas emphasise the need to look beyond the surface.

The drawing series Iteration (of Violence), which gives the exhibition its title, was prompted by protests and strikes in Belgium and France met with repression by police and authorities, including in Mantes-la-Jolie and Brussels’ Bois de la Cambre. Gbaguidi examines how this violence affects bodies and how they can reclaim their presence in public space. The title refers to the recurrence of violence and the ways colonial violence continues to reverberate physically and psychologically.

For Wave, Gbaguidi applied fluid motifs in walnut stain to a textile with a rigid checkerboard pattern. Taking the recurring form of the human pelvis as her starting point, she explores rhythm, movement and interpersonal relationships. Throughout the exhibition, Iteration thus manifests itself both through recurring themes and formal repetition.

Tim Van Laere Gallery
Antwerp–Rome
