We are excited to present the Vera List Center Forum 2026: Matter of Intelligence, held October 9 & 10 at The New School and online. This year's VLC Forum is organized within the framework of our two-year focus theme Matter of Intelligence, exploring the origins and implications of “intelligence” across species, systems, and societies through art and critical inquiry. Over two days, we convene with artists, curators, writers, scholars, and technologists to explore the many forms intelligence can take. The VLC Forum is free and open to the public with advanced registration. See the full lineup below!

ROSANA PAULINO: ¿HISTÓRIA NATURAL?

OCTOBER 9–NOVEMBER 1, 2026
EXHIBITION OPENING: OCTOBER 9, 5:30–6:15 PM
OPEN DAILY 12–6 PM EDT, THURSDAYS LATE UNTIL 8 PM
ARNOLD AND SHEILA ARONSON GALLERIES
SHEILA C. JOHNSON DESIGN CENTER, PARSONS SCHOOL OF DESIGN
THE NEW SCHOOL, 66 FIFTH AVENUE

The Vera List Center for Art and Politics presents Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?, an exhibition featuring the work of Brazilian artist and Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice recipient Rosana Paulino. Anchored by her landmark artist book of the same name, the exhibition traces Paulino’s critical examinations of the entanglements between science, race, gender, knowledge, and power that played a central role in shaping the national project envisioned by Brazilian elites in the early 19th century. Featuring a range of new, recent, and rarely seen works, the exhibition showcases the poetic and conceptual breadth of her practice and its significance across Brazil, the African diaspora, and other international contexts.

STUDY GROUP

BLACK FEMINIST STUDY GROUP

FRIDAY, OCTOBER 9, 4–5:30 PM EDT
ARNOLD AND SHEILA ARONSON GALLERIES
SHEILA C. JOHNSON DESIGN CENTER, PARSONS SCHOOL OF DESIGN
THE NEW SCHOOL, 66 FIFTH AVENUE

In-person only

The Black Feminist Study Group is a four-part program shaped by the poetics and practice of Rosana Paulino and assembled within her prize exhibition Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?. Anchored by seminal texts that touch on interconnected struggles for liberation and resistance throughout the African diaspora, the weekly series commences on Friday, October 9, with the opening of Paulino’s exhibition. Writer and editor Jessica Lynne begins the series with a reading from Beatriz Nascimento's The Dialectic Is in the Sea, hosted by the VLC's Re'al Christian. Visit our website for the full series lineup.

CEREMONY & CONVERSATION

PRIZE PRESENTATION AND CONVERSATION

FRIDAY, OCTOBER 9, 6:30–8 PM EDT
TISHMAN AUDITORIUM, UNIVERSITY CENTER
THE NEW SCHOOL, 63 FIFTH AVENUE, 1ST FLOOR

We celebrate and honor Rosana Paulino at the VLC Forum with the 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice for her artist book ¿História Natural?. The celebrations begin with an award ceremony, opening with a welcome by university president Joel Towers and an introduction by the VLC's Carin Kuoni. The ceremony is followed by a conversation featuring Paulino, philosopher Denise Ferreira da Silva, and curator Diane Lima. Together, they discuss their ongoing collaboration, Black feminist approaches to art, knowledge, and race in Brazil, situating Paulino’s practice within broader inquiries into epistemic violence and the role of art in challenging its effects.

KEYNOTE LECTURE

CHUS MARTÍNEZ: WHAT SHOULD WE DO WITH INTELLIGENCE? WHAT FORMS OF INTELLIGENCE ARE STILL PRODUCING THE SOCIAL?

SATURDAY, OCTOBER 10, 11 AM–12 PM EDT
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER
THE NEW SCHOOL, 63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL

Chus Martínez, curator and chair of the 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice jury, delivers the keynote lecture for the VLC Forum 2026. Her lecture examines intelligence not as a thing we possess, but as a capacity we activate. The question “What should we do with intelligence?” is inseparable from another: What forms of intelligence are still producing the social? Or—in other words—what forms of intelligence are still capable of generating cultural trust? Martínez considers how trust in culture and trust in each other’s cultures break the sense of isolation, polarization, and defensiveness that marks today’s social debates. Welcome remarks and conversation with the VLC's Eriola Pira.

CONVERSATIONS

CONVERSATIONS WITH THE 2025–2027 JANE LOMBARD FELLOWS

SATURDAY, OCTOBER 10, 12–5 PM EDT
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER
THE NEW SCHOOL, 63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL

In conversation with their nominators or invited guests, the Jane Lombard Fellows—Nepalese art collective ArTree Nepal, New York-based artist Stephanie Dinkins, Hawaiian and Samoan digital media theorist and poet Jason Edward Lewis from Canada, and Finnish artist Jenna Sutela share their nominated projects and their engagement with the material and intangible dimensions of intelligence. 

12–1 pm EDT | Jenna Sutela joins writer and collaborator Elvia Wilk to discuss Sutela’s prize-nominated project, nimiia cétiï—a work that brings together biological organisms and neural networks to imagine new forms of language and relation while unsettling distinctions between the natural and the artificial, the biological and the computational.

1 pm EDT | Lunch 

2–3 pm | Stephanie Dinkins revisits her landmark project Conversations with Bina48 in a dialogue with abolitionist and educator Neta Bomani. The two discuss Dinkins’ ongoing exchange with the social robot Bina48, along with technological future histories, Afro-now-ism, and more-than-human knowledge.

3–4 pm | Jason Edward Lewis speaks with scholar, artist, and 2025–2027 Vera List Center Fellow Mashinka Hakopian about Abundant Intelligences, an Indigenous-led research program and network. Drawing on anticolonial approaches to technology, their conversation asks what becomes possible when AI is designed for human and non-human kin alike. 

4–5 pm | ArTree Nepal members Lavkant Chaudhary, Mekh Limbu, and Subas Tamang are joined by Nepali American fashion designer Prabal Gurung for a conversation on ArTree’s Tika Chhedna Angana (“A Garden of Body Markings”)—a project centering on Indigenous tattooing practices and embodied knowledge in Nepal.

ABOUT THE VLC FORUM

 

The Vera List Center Forum is an annual international convening of leading voices in the field of art and politics and a signature program of the Vera List Center for Art and Politics. A platform for in-depth research and shared learning into the VLC’s biennial Focus Themes, the VLC Forum showcases exemplary politically engaged art practices from around the world, including those of the Vera List Center Fellows, the Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice recipient, and the Jane Lombard Fellows.

From keynote lectures and conversations to exhibitions, large-scale musical productions, and publications, the VLC Forum unfolds over several days at The New School and online, gathering local and international artists, performers, scholars, activists, and policymakers, all working at the forefront of art, political imagination, and social justice. Completely free and open to all, the VLC Forum presents a unique opportunity for collective learning and community-making. Hosted by generous supporters, a free Community Dinner for everyone anchors each VLC Forum, bringing together the politically engaged art community, near and far. 

The Vera List Center Forum 2026 is presented as part of the VLC’s 2025–2027 Focus Theme Matter of Intelligence. It is curated by Carin Kuoni and Eriola Pira and convened with the support of Tabor Banquer, Re’al Christian, and Adrienne Umeh.
