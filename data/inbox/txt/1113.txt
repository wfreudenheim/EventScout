Wednesday, November 18, 6:30 p.m.
Unworlding, Unbuilding, and Trans Anarchitectures
Jack Halberstam and Nicole Eisenman
in Conversation with Claire Bishop
FREE; RESERVE NOW

What does it mean to unbuild the world? In his new book, leading queer theorist Jack Halberstam, professor at Columbia University, revives the 1970s idea of “anarchitecture” and uses it to connect the physical alteration of bodies to an unmaking of our built environment and social systems. Demolition and dismantling are also crucial terms for MacArthur-winning artist Nicole Eisenman, who in 2025 showed a toppled industrial crane in nearby Madison Square Park. They will be joined in conversation by Claire Bishop, presidential professor of Art History at the CUNY Graduate Center.

Presented with the Ph.D. Program in Art History, CLAGS: The Center for LGBTQ Studies, and the Center for the Humanities.

Thursday, November 12, 6:30 p.m.
Deep Dive: Listening to Solange
FREE; RESERVE NOW

Stay for a reception with food and drink after the event!

The next event in the series Deep Dive, which explores the work of popular artists who have shaped our musical imagination, focuses on the multidisciplinary Solange Knowles. On the 10th anniversary of Solange’s A Seat at the Table — an album that redefined contemporary R&B through its intimate reflections on Black womanhood, healing, family, memory, and self-determination — we present a discussion and reflection on key tracks from her catalog.

Gloria Kaba, an acclaimed audio engineer and producer who has worked with Solange, joins with Emily Lordi and Salamishah Tillet, two of the most insightful voices writing and thinking about Black culture today, to help us understand Solange’s artistic and cultural impact. Kaba’s work spans recording, mixing, and songwriting with artists including Beyoncé, Madonna, Yebba, Q-Tip, and A Tribe Called Quest; she teaches at Berklee NYC. Lordi is a professor at Vanderbilt University and author of Holding Lightening, a new biography of Whitney Houston; Tillet is a professor at Rutgers University and a Pulitzer Prize-winning critic-at-large for The New York Times.

Presented with H. Wiley Hitchcock Institute for Studies in American Music. Made possible with support from the Elebash Global Voices Fund.

Wednesday, November 11, 6:30 p.m.
Paul Krugman and Heather Cox Richardson in Conversation
Proshansky Auditorium
FREE; RSVP FOR LIVESTREAM
 
One week after the midterm elections, Paul Krugman and Heather Cox Richardson join in a timely conversation. From their unique vantage points as an economist and a historian, who are both now independent commentators with large followings on Substack, Krugman and Richardson weigh in on the state of democracy, politics, the economy, and the free press. With personal insight, they look at how the media landscape in American has shifted away from legacy outlets to alternative forms of commentary, and how these new forms influence the national discussion. Krugman is a Nobel laureate, former New York Times columnist, and research professor of Economics at the CUNY Graduate Center. Richardson is a professor of history at Boston College and author of, most recently, Democracy Awakening.
 
Presented with the Stone Center on Socio-Economic Inequality.
