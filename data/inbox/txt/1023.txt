The Quick and the Dead is an interdisciplinary program that mobilizes the creative and pedagogical potential of focused engagement with artistic lineages. Through collective research, reading and discussion groups, and public seminars, the program draws connections between these lineages and the work of contemporary practitioners. 

In its fourth edition, Q&D turns to histories of independent and artist publishing practices. Eight artists, writers, and researchers take part in a tuition-free research residency to pursue self-guided study on publishing as an infrastructure of discourse, collaboration, and solidarity. The program unfolds through sustained dialogue with four Q&D advisors—Jenna Hamed, Gerardo Ismael Madera, Kameelah Janan Rasheed, and Sukhdev Sandhu—whose distinct methodologies offer insight and guidance for developing research.

Wendy’s Subway ascribes to the idea that publishing is a practice of social assembly with the capacity to advance significant change. From artists’ books and zines to radical periodicals and even mass-market paperbacks, publishing indexes political and artistic movements while materializing the central role of aesthetics in cultural production. Publications articulate, translate, and disseminate knowledge across contexts and temporalities; they circulate dissent across contested borders and build the shared means by which struggles recognize one another. 

Amid a global landscape marked by shrinking resources, conglomerate publishing, consolidated distribution, and escalating pressures on free speech, we want to contend, together, with the stakes of independent publishing. Its histories demonstrate how people have built durable cultural infrastructures for making, producing, and circulating art and ideas. A study of publishing histories is therefore inseparable from a study of the various forms of collective life and labor that make a given publication possible. We ask: What forms of collectivity have existed in and through publishing, and what can be learned from them to meet the current political juncture? This season of Q&D brings attention to the lasting imprint of these historical gestures as resources for the present.

OPEN CALL FOR PARTICIPANTS

Apply by December 7

Q&D is a tuition-free program, guided by Wendy’s Subway’s commitment to independent, collaborative, and counter-hegemonic pedagogy. Participants bring their own research and methodologies to the group to develop a structure built on curiosity, exchange, political education, and interdisciplinary practice. 

Q&D runs from February through June 2027. The program involves: 
 * Independent research, carried out in dialogue with the Wendy’s Subway team, the cohort, the advisors, and invited guests 
 * Monthly site visits to archives, artists’ studios, or publishing organizations
 * Monthly public programs
 * Monthly cohort reading and discussion sessions to share research in progress 
 * 1:1 mentorship tailored to each participant’s research
 * Dedicated workspace access to the Wendy’s Subway reading room, with library and archive research support 

This program is designed to complement the rhythm of everyday life by offering a rigorous, collective context and intentional, focused time for research that is not contingent on envisioning concrete outcomes or presentations of the work. Instead, Q&D is an opportunity to allow ideas and questions to emerge, to learn from one another, and to foster meaningful relationships through shared critical inquiry alongside a dedicated group of interlocutors.

Wendy’s Subway is a non-profit reading room, writing space, and independent publisher located in Bushwick, Brooklyn. We support emerging artists and writers in making experimental, urgent work and create alternative modes for learning and thinking in community. Wendy’s Subway is dedicated to encouraging creative, critical, and discursive engagement with arts and literature.

Our interdisciplinary programs offer free readings, talks, performances, and reading groups, as well as sliding-scale workshops and courses. Our multiseries publishing initiative includes works in translation, artists' books, poetic texts, and hybrid-genre works by time-based artists. Our non-circulating library holds a collection of over 5,000 titles, with a focus on international independent publishing, art and artists’ books, poetry, theory, and criticism.
