What happens when a Japanese painter lands in 1950s New York and starts trading ideas with Jasper Johns, Mark Rothko and Robert Rauschenberg?

Gen’ichirō Inokuma spent 20 years here finding out, and the results, bold, playful and unmistakably shaped by the city, are now home in the first New York solo museum exhibition of his work. Plan your visit to Gen’ichirō Inokuma’s NYC Salon before it closes January 10, 2027 and remember that students save $5 at the door!
