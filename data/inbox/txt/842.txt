CONVERSATIONS IN BIOETHICS | WHEN BODIES BECOME MARKETS: THE ETHICS OF ORGAN SALES

Mon, Oct 19 | 6:30 PM | Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library (SNFL) & Online
As part of our new series, join faculty from the Johns Hopkins Berman Institute of Bioethics to examine some of the most challenging questions in bioethics today. As thousands of patients await life-saving transplants, some argue that financial incentives could increase organ donation, while others warn that organ markets could lead to exploitation and inequity. Jeffrey Kahn,  PhD, MPH, and Mario Macis, PhD, will examine the values, assumptions, and ethical tensions underlying this debate. EXHIBITIONS AT NYPL

----------------------------------------

SILLY OLD BEAR! WINNIE-THE-POOH AT 100

Through Nov 30, 2026 | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
Winnie-the-Pooh was first published in October 1926. To celebrate the book's 100th birthday, The New York Public Library invites you to explore an anniversary display featuring original mockups of A.A. Milne's text alongside E.H. Shepard's famous illustrations, some notable translations of the Pooh stories from around the world, and, for the first time on public display, the new Roo toy gifted to the Library in 2026 by Her Majesty Queen Camilla. Plus, visit the stuffed toys that inspired the books and more.
LEARN MORE ᐳ

DECLARING AMERICA: 1776 AND BEYOND

Through Jan 10, 2027 | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
Spanning multiple galleries at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, Declaring America: 1776 and Beyond unpacks the complex, diverse, and powerful stories of the American Revolution—from 1776 to today. Featuring broadsides and posters, iconic founding documents, protest ephemera, powerful artworks, and artifacts from the Library's collections at the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture, and the Library for the Performing Arts, the exhibition presents a diverse range of perspectives and invites visitors of all ages to explore our nation's remarkable beginnings, complex legacies, and promises fulfilled and unfulfilled.
LEARN MORE ᐳ

FORGING AHEAD: RECENT ACQUISITIONS OF BLACK WOMEN ARTISTS

Through Mar 27, 2027 | Schomburg Center 
Working across various media, the 21 artists featured in this exhibition engage wide-ranging themes including iconography of the Black woman’s body, storytelling, futurism, spirituality, and the mapping of Black history. These recent acquisitions acknowledge the importance of Black women artists and advance the Schomburg Center’s ability to represent, preserve, and share more dimensions of our expansive Black culture.
LEARN MORE ᐳ

George Balanchine with his cat Mourka, 1964. Photo © Martha Swope. Jerome Robbins Dance Division, The New York Public Library for the Performing Arts.

UNLEASHED: PETS OF THE PERFORMING ARTS

Through May 8, 2027 | Library for the Performing Arts
Drawing from the rich archives of the three Library for the Performing Arts' divisions, Unleashed: Pets of the Performing Arts gives center stage to the animals behind the artists. From Billie Holiday's boxer Mister and Anna Pavlova's pet swan Jack to Larry Kramer's beloved hound Molly, animals have quietly shaped the lives and work of the world's most iconic performers. Beyond the stage, their quiet loyalty revealed a human tenderness in artists whose public personas often kept audiences at a distance.
