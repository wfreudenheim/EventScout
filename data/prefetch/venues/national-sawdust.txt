The Green Room Ensemble is thrilled to present the U.S. Premiere performance cycle of Eleanor Alberga’s Three String Quartets, along with the World Premiere of her Second Piano Quintet with the composer herself at the piano. Eleanor Alberga, OBE, is one of today’s most esteemed and influential composers and pianists. Originally inspired by a local music teacher in Kingston, Jamaica, her compositional journey began with her work for the London Contemporary Dance Theatre, where she honed her evocative and riveting musical style. Her Three String Quartets stand as monumental achievements, each heavily varied in style and tone, yet all uniquely of her voice. They are a profound contribution to the genre.

The album is the invitation. The live performance is the encounter. Grammy-nominated Argentine-American mezzo-soprano Daniela Mack and Grammy-nominated Argentine bandoneonist JP Jofre bring a distinctive voice to Argentine music today. For Daniela, SUR began with a memory: hearing Astor Piazzolla from the back seat of her father’s car. That sound, with its intensity, melancholy, and freedom, and its unmistakable connection to Buenos Aires, stayed with her. Years later, as an artist with an international career in opera, she returned to that music from a different place. SUR became a way to reconnect with a part of her identity, and to ask what Piazzolla can mean now, in the voice of a contemporary classical singer.

Two radical innovators. Three centuries apart. One evening. Electric guitar quartet Dither open the evening with newly commissioned works that channel Hendrix's experimental language. Director R.B. For the past several years Emily Wells has been knocking on the door of an opera. Each year the door cracks open a little wider, the story gets louder, and the collaborators become clearer. This opera is a story about remaking the past in order to understand, and perhaps change, the future. It’s a conversation between a “mirror father” living the life he might have lived had he come out in the early 80s instead of living a closeted life, and his “phantom daughter” who he begins dreaming about. The two discover overlays: their queerness, their art, their relation to capital and its effects on community. At the center is each one's desire to have a child but choosing not to due to their fears of the end of the world, his AIDS, hers climate catastrophe.

Peni Candra Rini, PhD, is a renowned Javanese singer, educator, and composer, known for blending Javanese Sufi poetry with contemporary music for both Indonesian and Western instruments. The Kronos Quartet calls her "one of the world’s greatest singers." A master of traditional forms like gamelan, she stands out as one of Indonesia’s innovative young composers and one of its few female composers. In 2023, she became the first woman commissioned to compose for the Mangkunegaran court of Central Java, and in 2024, she was honored with the court name Raden Nganten Tumenggung. For this performance, the artist will be joined on stage by musician, ethnomusicologist, and professor Andy McGraw and multi-instrumentalist, composer, and producer Shahzad Ismaily.
