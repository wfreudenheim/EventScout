What happens to poetry when we share our language with machines? They Make You Write Your Poems brings together artists, poets, and designers who explore the relationship between the human body and machine cognition.

The title comes from Adrienne Rich’s 1961 poem Artificial Intelligence, addressed to one of the first AI programs, the General Problem Solver. Decades before large language models, Rich imagined a machine force-fed on the archive of human thought. Today, as AI agents become part of the environments in which we speak, think, and relate, the exhibition asks who is the “you” being made to write?

The exhibition reflects on artificial intelligence as a model of cognition that artists have long engaged in conversation by imagining it, borrowing its procedures and turning them toward unexpected uses. Along the way, it traces surprising lineages, from avant-garde noise machines and transrational poetry to spiritualist séances, tide-predicting machines, Cold War computer poems, and the textile legacy of computing.

Expect solar-powered poem generators, knitted computers with felt buttons, an AI that narrates mushroom trips, a dance pad that writes revolutionary poetry, and a simulation that lets you hear the weather through the ears of a machine.

They Make You Write Your Poems is curated by Giulia Colletti for the American Academy in Rome’s second Italian Fellowship for Curatorial Research, in collaboration with and with the support of the Directorate-General for Contemporary Creativity of the Italian Ministry of Culture. Hosted by Onassis ONX, the Onassis Foundation's platform for art and advanced technologies.

How do we touch the digital? How do we reclaim the analog? How do we preserve the human?

As human-made books and images become the exception and algorithmic production becomes the norm, the digital is no longer confined to or emanating from the screen. It permeates the body, clothing, objects, sound, the street, the room, the community, while also returning to the screen as a space for storytelling, connection, and imagination. ONX Seeds is looking for projects that give these signals new forms: images, objects, environments, and rituals that can be seen, touched, worn, inhabited, played, and shared.

Through a four-month artistic development program, ONX Seeds will support up to four prototypes with seed funding of up to €5,000, production, artistic, curatorial, and technical guidance, and access to the new ONX studio at Onassis Ready in Athens. Selected projects will evolve from initial concepts into artistically compelling, technologically functional prototypes and be presented at the ONX Showcase in Athens in March 2027. 

Applications close this coming Monday, October 26, 2026!

PLAZA by Andrew Schneider

Lincoln Center Performances

Through Oct 25 @ 5PM Daily

In the rush of the city, it's easy to miss what—and who—is right in front of us. Step into PLAZA, an interactive experience that turns people-watching into the art itself. Over two weeks, once a day, ONX Member Andrew Schneider—director, writer, and multimedia artist—transforms Josie Robertson Plaza into a living artwork built from what's already there: the crowd, the noise, the strangers beside you, and the coincidences that happen when people share a moment. You won't just be present; you'll be part of what everyone else is watching, too.
