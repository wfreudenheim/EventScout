Game Design Days is a NYU Game Center sponsored event series that highlights the underground independent game development communities of NYC. The goal of the event is to serve NYC game design students and anyone curious about game design, by introducing them to the broader NYC game development scene and giving them opportunities to connect with independent studios, collectives, and spaces. By doing so we also hope to strengthen the network of independent NYC game development professionals. Check out the events list, find something that speaks to you, and dive into learning about the world of local game design!

Patrick Trinh

NYU GAME CENTER LECTURE SERIES PRESENTS PATRICK TRINH
THURSDAY, DECEMBER 3RD, 7:00PM - 8:30PM
370 JAY STREET, LECTURE HALL 202
(ALSO LIVE AT TWITCH.TV/NYUGAMECENTER)
RSVP HERE
 

Patrick Trinh, despite being a professor at the NYU Game Center, has not made a game in years, and has instead fully immersed himself in the NYC club/rave scene. In this experimental performance/talk, he will explore why that happened via various perspectives, including McKenzie Wark’s work including Gamer Theory and Raving, the Situationist Internationale, the origins of techno, and David Kanaga. Through these perspectives, Patrick will explore the similarities between games and music spaces and practice. Could it be that he has been making games disguised as music this entire time?

We are thrilled to announce the details for the 15th iteration of No Quarter!

No Quarter is an annual playable exhibition where four newly commissioned works from leading artists working across games are debuted. This year, No Quarter moves off NYU's campus for the first time since 2019, taking place this year at Life World (563 Johnson Ave, Brooklyn, NY) on Friday, December 4th. The event is free to attend, but spaces are limited.

NO QUARTER 2026 COHORT

Geumhyung Jeong

Geumhyung Jeong is an artist whose work ranges from performance, dance, choreography, theatre, video and installation. Since the beginning of her career, Jeong’s work has investigated the relationship between the human body and the objects that surround it, by way of productions that combine languages and techniques from the fields of contemporary dance, puppet theatre and the visual arts. In the course of the physical interaction between her body and the objects, it becomes ambiguous who controls whom, blurring the line between inanimate and animate.

geumhyungjeong.com

Chia Amisola

Chia Amisola is a tech worker from the Philippines. They're devoted to the internet's loss, love, labor, and liberation, particularly of the third world experience. They make work about intimacy, infrastructure, and infinity across text, websites, and performances. Entangling Philippine specificity and technological histories, their body of work mends the encyclopedic, archival, and personal, to look towards the intimacies of infrastructure and the labor of tools. Today, they look to the future of agency, affect, faith, and labor as changed by technological progress.

GURN GROUP

Gurn Group is a software developer with interests in memory, software affect, and small-scale interactive texture. Assemblage-based game development integrating incidental video footage, free 3D assets, and emulated software (plunderludics). Gurn Group’s work has been exhibited at Babycastles (NYC), LIKELIKE (Pittsburgh, PA), Boshi’s Place (NYC). Previously involved in curation at Death by Audio Arcade and Boshi’s Place. Currently working on an RTS game called Lives of the Saints, an FMV point-and-click game called My Summer Vacation, and a plunderludics development tool called UnityHawk.

gurnburial.itch.io

Nolen Royalty

Nolen Royalty is a game designer and engineer living in Brooklyn, New York. His work is centered around experimental massively multiplayer games and pushing computers to do surprising and delightful things.

eieio.games

Additional info on the NYC Game Jam from the official website:

> The inaugural NYC Game Jam and Career Fair kicked off on Saturday, November 14, 2026, bringing together two essential parts of New York City's video game ecosystem: the next generation of game creators and the industry professionals who can help them build their careers.
>
> The Game Jam welcomed 150 students representing 12 schools and 63 ZIP codes from across the city for a day of game development, collaboration, and friendly competition. Alongside the competition, a career fair connected students with game studios and industry professionals, giving aspiring developers the opportunity to learn about career pathways, discover the skills employers are looking for, and make valuable industry connections.

> One theme. 36 hours. A whole city to explore.
> The challenge: "Get There Already!"

> This is New York City. Everybody has somewhere to be, and the mission was to get across the city before the clock ran out. Teams chose their starting points, destinations, and routes. They could take the train, bus, ferry, bike, or invent an entirely new way to get around. They put their imagination, knowledge of the city, and maybe a few shortcuts only a New Yorker would know to the test. With submissions due by 11:59pm on Sunday, November 15, 2026, teams brought their best ideas, took risks, collaborated, and had fun.  It was exciting to see what they created in such a short time. Congratulations to all the participants.

WHY FANS GO FERAL FOR FICTIONAL PEOPLE
PRESENTED BY NYU CENTER FOR THE HUMANITIES
TUESDAY, DECEMBER 8TH, 5:00PM - 6:30PM
LOCATION TBD
MORE INFO AND RSVP HERE
 

What makes fans go feral for a character from a fictional work? The most recent research from Game Center faculty Dr. Ashley Guajardo and her co-authors says it takes a cocktail of empathy, objectification, and community engagement in their latest book, Play Like a Fangirl. Viral marketing on social media platforms, like TikTok, has become one of the few ways independent writers, filmmakers, artists, and game developers can market their art. Beyond social media strategies, this book launch talk invites the audience to think through thirst traps, queerness, kink, and community connection as a way to inspire desire amongst media consumers.

Presented in partnership with the Tisch Initiative for Creative Research and the Institute of Critical Studies in the Arts.
