WHAT’S ON AT ASIA SOCIETY

November at Asia Society brings together conversations about theater, art, migration, and identity, alongside dance performances, film screenings, and concerts. Highlights include Maya Lin on art, nature, and memory; the latest scholarship on migrants and the arts along the Silk Road; a restored screening of Spring in a Small Town; and a performance by the Aga Khan Master Musicians.

IN THE MUSEUM: GANESHA AND THE SPIRIT OF DIWALI

Front and back views of Ganesha, 1979.13

A beloved work from the Mr. and Mrs. John D. Rockefeller 3rd Collection and on view in the lobby, Ganesha is celebrated as the Remover of Obstacles in Hindu holidays, including Diwali (or Deepavali), which falls this year on Sunday, November 8. 

Given his role as the Remover of Obstacles, Ganesha would have been mounted at a temple doorway, or inside a wall niche on the sanctum exterior. In this sculpture, he swings dramatically to his left, as he heralds the triumph of light over darkness, good over evil. Come celebrate Diwali at Asia Society and see if you can find the bowl of sweet dumplings (modak ) held by one of the ten arms on Ganesha! 

— Xiaohan Du, John H. Foster Assistant Curator of Pre-Modern Asian Art

UPCOMING EVENTS

From left to right: David Henry Hwang, Lauren Yee, and Francis Jue

KING OF THE YEES: LAUREN YEE, FRANCIS JUE, AND DAVID HENRY HWANG

November 2 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Join acclaimed playwrights David Henry Hwang and Lauren Yee and Tony Award-winning actor Francis Jue for a conversation about family, cultural identity, and the power of personal storytelling in theater. Drawing on their experiences and relationships with their fathers, the artists reflect on how family histories, generational differences, and deeply personal memories inspire their work and shape the stories they bring to the stage.

GENDER IN MOTION: BIJAYINI SATPATHY AND SOPHILINE CHEAM-SHAPIRO IN CONVERSATION

November 4 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Acclaimed Odissi dancer Bijayini Satpathy and Cambodian classical dancer Sophiline Cheam-Shapiro examine how movement, expression, and cultural traditions shape representations of gender in performance. Drawing on their respective artistic practices, the conversation explores the possibilities of reinterpreting inherited dance vocabularies and expressing identities beyond conventional boundaries.

TRIKĀYĀ BY BIJAYINI SATPATHY: SOLO ODISSI DANCE PERFORMANCE WITH LIVE MUSIC

November 5 from 7:30—9:00 PM (ET)

Acclaimed Odissi dancer Bijayini Satpathy presents Trikāyā (Three Bodies), a solo performance with live music inspired by the ancient Indian epic Mahabharata. Through the story of Arjuna’s transformation into Brihannala, Satpathy explores gender, identity, and vulnerability, using classical movement to challenge traditional boundaries and express the complexities of human experience.

MUSEUM SALON SERIES: MIGRATION, CULTURAL EXCHANGE, AND THE ART OF THE SILK ROAD

November 5 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Join Columbia University art historian Jin Xu and Asia Society Museum curator Xiaohan Du for a discussion of Xu’s new book, The Silk Road with Many Faces. Drawing on sixth-century stone sarcophagi, the conversation explores the lives of Sogdian immigrants in China and the role of migration, trade, and cultural exchange in shaping early medieval art.

FILMS TO SEE BEFORE YOU DIE: SPRING IN A SMALL TOWN

November 11 from 6:30—8:15 PM (ET)

Discover a landmark of Chinese cinema with the New York premiere of the China Film Archive’s restoration of Fei Mu’s acclaimed film Spring in a Small Town. Set in the aftermath of the Sino-Japanese War, this intimate drama explores love, longing, and emotional conflict as its characters navigate personal desires and the weight of the past. Celebrated for its subtle storytelling and poetic visual language, the film offers a poignant reflection on human relationships amid a changing society.

MAYA LIN ON ART, NATURE, AND MEMORY

November 12 from 6:30—7:30 PM (ET)

Artist and architectural designer Maya Lin takes part in a special conversation celebrating Asia Society’s 70th anniversary. Known for her groundbreaking work across art, architecture, and environmental design, Lin reflects on the intersections of nature, memory, and the built environment that have shaped her artistic vision. The discussion offers an opportunity to explore her creative practice and the enduring relationship between art, history, and our connection to the natural world.

Dr. Yeon Shim Chung, Andrew Russeth 

DANSAEKHWA: KOREAN ABSTRACT ART AND ITS CULTURAL IMPACT

November 17 from 6:30—7:30 PM (ET)

Asia Society hosts the launch of Dansaekhwa: Korean Abstract Art and Its Cultural Impact, with author and art historian Dr. Yeon Shim Chung and Andrew Russeth, art critic and Editor-in-Chief of Artnet News. Emerging in the 1970s, Dansaekhwa was a major abstract art movement in postwar South Korea that pushed the boundaries of painting and color outside the canvas into fields that included drawing, craft, sculpture, installation, and conceptual and experimental art.

CONCERT: AGA KHAN MASTER MUSICIANS WITH VINCENT PEIRANI

November 18 from 7:30—9:00 PM (ET)

Experience an evening of musical collaboration as the Aga Khan Master Musicians perform alongside French accordionist Vincent Peirani. Drawing on musical traditions from Central Asia, the Middle East, and the Mediterranean, the concert brings together diverse instruments, styles, and cultural influences. Inspired by the historic routes that connected Asia and the Mediterranean, the performance celebrates the exchange of musical ideas across borders and generations, blending traditional sounds with contemporary interpretations.

MUSEUM SALON SERIES: LIVING NATIONAL TREASURE ŌSUMI YUKIE AND THE WAY OF METAL

December 2 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

Asia Society presents a conversation with Ōsumi Yukie, the first female metalcraft artist to be designated a Japanese Preserver of Important Intangible Cultural Properties, better known as a Living National Treasure. Moderated by Chinese gold-and-silver specialist Boxi Liu, this event offers a rare glimpse into the tactile world of traditional Japanese metalcraft.
