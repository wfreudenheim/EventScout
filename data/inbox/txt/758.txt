Article Preview: Jean-Jacques Schuhl and Albert Serra on Ingrid Caven

“Ingrid’s acting, Ingrid herself—her talent, sometimes her genius—is based on contradictions. She has this hypnotic side, she sometimes plays a bit like a marionette, the marionettes that [writer Heinrich von] Kleist speaks about, and she also has a very lively side; in the same moment, it can be the opposite of hypnosis: very conscious. In other words, there’s both artifice and a kind of truth to Ingrid that fascinates audiences.”

—A portrait, in our own way by Jean-Jacques Schuhl and Albert Serra

STAFF SPOTLIGHT

BENJAMIN GOFF – FRONT OF HOUSE STAFF

What do you love about NYFF?

What I love most is that, more than at any other festival I attend, people come to NYFF because they genuinely love movies. Everything else (the conversations, events, and festivities) feels like it exists in service of the films themselves.

Do you have a favorite NYFF memory or experience?

After a screening of Lady Bird, I was sitting up on the plaza level and Greta Gerwig came out of the Walter Reade and sat next to me. We chatted for a bit about her movie and she's just as delightful as you'd imagine.
