The submission deadline for the Publisher Work Grant has been extended through October 11 — Last chance to apply!

Now in its third year, the Publisher Work Grant offers funding to independent presses with a history of artists’ book publishing, acknowledging awardees’ accomplishments in past work and providing the capacity-building funds to enable future book projects and publisher sustainability. The Grant aims to strengthen an ecosystem of culturally vital publishing practices that champion experimental book projects falling outside of mainstream distribution channels.

In 2026, three publishers will each receive:
–  An unrestricted grant of $10,000 USD
–  A featured exhibitor presence at a forthcoming Printed Matter NY or LA Art Book Fair in 2027

Applications are open through Sunday, October 11, 2026, 11:59PM EST. Please read the FAQ to learn more about eligibility and requirements.

2025 PUBLISHER WORK GRANT AWARDEES

In 2025, the Publisher Work Grant was awarded to three presses whose work foregrounds a thoughtful artist-driven approach, engages with urgent themes, and has shown a dedication to the output of emerging and underrepresented artists — cahiers manufactoriel (Garges-lès-Gonesse, France), Gato Negro Ediciones (Mexico City, Mexico), and Khajistan Press (Queens, New York). Printed Matter recently spoke with each publisher to learn more about their origins, inspirations, and upcoming projects. Read the interviews here.

“Our work is informed by parapraxis, a methodology we have developed around fragments, omissions, displacements and traces. Rather than understanding knowledge as fixed and complete, we are interested in how it emerges through circulation, transformation and the relations between different forms of transmission.” — cahiers manufactoriel

“The artists’ book begins from a fact often neglected: a book is a sequence, a duration, and a body, not simply a container for words and images. Weight, interval, repetition, scale, friction, and the time imposed by a page are not secondary to meaning; they produce it.” — Gato Negro Ediciones

“Our influences come from what we collect: Lahore footpath digests, Lollywood posters, cheap paperbacks, religious pamphlets, political propaganda and internet culture. Their language, layouts and ways of reaching people inform our books.” — Khajistan Press
