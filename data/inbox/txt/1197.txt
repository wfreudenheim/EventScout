Lin Yi-Chi awarded the Han Nefkens Foundation—LOOP Barcelona Video Art Production Grant 2026

Taiwanese artist Lin Yi-Chi has been selected as the recipient of the Han Nefkens Foundation – LOOP Barcelona Video Art Production Grant 2026, in collaboration with ILHAM Gallery, Kuala Lumpur; Museum of Contemporary Art Taipei (MoCA); Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (MOT); Kunsthaus Zürich; WIELS, Brussels; Almaty Museum of Arts, Kazakhstan; and Museum Ludwig, Cologne. Lin Yi-Chi will receive a 25,000 USD production grant to develop a new moving-image work, to be completed by August 2027 and presented at the seven partner institutions.

Lin Yi-Chi was selected by an international jury from a group of ten finalists following a nomination process involving independent curators from across Asia. The jury was particularly impressed by the sensitivity of her filmmaking, the coherence between her research questions and distinctive visual language, and her ability to transform specific political and historical subjects into broader reflections on power, displacement and belonging.

Her practice explores how postwar histories in Asia continue to shape everyday life across generations. War, militarisation, migration and political orders leave traces in language, family histories and individual identities, often in ways that cross or complicate national boundaries. Through long-term field research and sustained encounters with people and communities, Lin Yi-Chi works with oral histories, sound, moving images, dreams and reenactment to explore the ruptures, silences and conflicting memories through which history is experienced and transmitted.

The jury particularly valued the way Lin Yi-Chi takes specific political and historical subjects, including the history of the U.S. military presence in Taiwan, and transforms them into reflections that resonate beyond their immediate context.

The jury also recognised the importance of oral histories, particularly those of older generations of women, and the way different forms of language and narration become integral to the filmmaking itself. Rather than seeking to fill gaps in history with a definitive narrative, Lin Yi-Chi's work preserves ambiguity and contradiction, creating disjunctions between documentary and fiction, sound and image, and different temporalities.

Lin Yi-Chi: “My practice explores how our daily lives have been shaped by postwar histories in Asia. The imprints left by war, militarisation, migration and political orders have never disappeared. Instead, they are embedded in language, family history and individual identity in everyday life, from one generation to another. I am particularly keen to focus on those histories and memories that sit along the blurred boundaries of what defines a nation, unveiling a non-linear, diasporic experience through a mixed-heritage narrative.”

The new commission will allow Lin Yi-Chi to take this long-term research into a new phase. During the nine-month production period, she will build on her ongoing fieldwork and explore interconnected experiences across different regions and generations within Asia’s postwar histories.

The grant reflects the Han Nefkens Foundation’s commitment to supporting artists not only through the production of new work, but through the creation of sustained international networks around their practices.

The 2026 jury was chaired by Han Nefkens (Founder, Han Nefkens Foundation) alongside jurors Emilio Álvarez (Founder, LOOP Barcelona), Hua-tzu Chan (Deputy Director, MoCA Taipei), Yilmaz Dziewior (Director, Museum Ludwig, Cologne), Keiko Okamura (Curator, MOT), Hendrik Folkerts (Chief Curator, Kunsthaus Zürich), Helena Kritis (Chief Curator, WIELS), and Meruyert Kaliyeva (Artistic Director) and Inga Lace (Chief Curator) from Almaty Museum of Arts. Also present were Hilde Teerlinck and Alessandra Biscaro, Director and Head of Programs of the Han Nefkens Foundation.

The ten finalists were Mengyun Han (1989, China); Lin Yi-Chi (1986, Taiwan); Pallavi Paul (1986, India); Zuqiang Peng (1992, China); Gogularaajan Rajendran (1993, Malaysia); Riar Rizaldi (1990, Indonesia); Miku Sato (1985, Japan); Leyla Stevens (1982, Australia); Santua Tripura (1995, Bangladesh); and Bo Wang (1982, China).

The Foundation and the institutions are grateful to the invaluable support of an international panel of experts, including Ulanda Blair; Diana Campbell Betancourt; Binna Choi; Ulan Djaparov; Natasha Ginwala; Sean Hu; Alvin Li; Gahee Park; Sadia Rahman; Dilda Ramazan; Roopesh Sitharan; Mikala Tai; Sisy, Rui-Xu Wang; and Zoe Yeh.
