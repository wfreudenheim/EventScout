Ali, a self-denigrating Catholic and unruly homosexual who immigrated to Berlin almost a decade ago from southern Lebanon, stands accused of assaulting a police officer during a Palestine solidarity demonstration. Caught in a suspended present as he faces possible deportation from Germany, Ali prepares to bid his goodbyes to a city in ebullition. The many people in his life pierce through the dense fog of days: Francesco, an Italian ex-boyfriend afflicted with the malady of hope; Samira, his tenacious confidante and the bearer of her Syrian community’s burdens; and, somewhere far away, his father Karim, who took arms with the communists against the Israeli occupation in the 1980s. There are also the Germans, and the beast that slumbers in them—who knows what it remains capable of. Carried by tragic humor and political reckoning, Fear Eats Ali is a forthright novel about the time and place of resistance, the debt one carries in its wake, and the helplessness of men in the face of immense historical transformations. 

About the author

Edwin Nasr was born in 1994 in Haret Sakher, Lebanon. He lives and works in Berlin, Germany. His writings on politics and contemporary art have been published in Bidoun, n+1, Afterall Journal, The Funambulist, Mousse Magazine, and Mada Masr. He is the Senior Editor of Palestine is everywhere (TBA21, Silver Press, and the87press, 2025) and translates from French to English for different publications.  

Praise

Edwin Nasr has written a great war novel—sharp, intelligent, probing—that draws us into the exhausting venality of German Staatsräson and into a complex and evocative world of transnational solidarity and anti-colonial struggle centered in and around Lebanon and Palestine. What does it mean to fight and fail and start again? This is the crucial question Nasr’s book engages, a book for our historical moment, a book that needs to be read. 
—Avery F. Gordon, author of Ghostly Matters: Haunting and the Sociological Imagination

Fear Eats Ali is a febrile and slick-witted immersion into the ways evasion is simply impossible in the face of a genocide and its parallel reality as a culture of violent repression and bloodthirst in Berlin. Nasr’s prose is indignant, charming, and unforgiving in its excavation of the paradoxes of our reality in which political agency is wrested from us at every turn. Prescient, razor-sharp, and radiant.
—Juliana Huxtable, author of Mucus in My Pineal Gland

Edwin Nasr gifts us staggeringly beautiful language that refuses to concede this moment and this world to the pragmatists or the fascists. You will not simply want to cite every paragraph of this book: You will leave rambling voice notes about it to comrades; you will gift it; you will read it out loud in bed to whomever, long after midnight.
—Jordy Rosenberg, author of Confessions of a Fox

Wendy’s Subway is a non-profit reading room, writing space, and independent publisher located in Bushwick, Brooklyn. We support emerging artists and writers in making experimental, urgent work and create alternative modes for learning and thinking in community. Wendy’s Subway is dedicated to encouraging creative, critical, and discursive engagement with arts and literature.

Our interdisciplinary programs offer free readings, talks, performances, and reading groups, as well as sliding-scale workshops and courses. Our multiseries publishing initiative includes works in translation, artists' books, poetic texts, and hybrid-genre works by time-based artists.
