ILLUMINATIONS

BY CAROLINE GOLUM
NOVEMBER 13, 2026

READ ON OUR SITE

"We view films in the context of darkness, we sit in darkness and watch an illuminated world.” With this opening incantation, Patrick Shen and Brandon Vedder’s documentary Illuminations (2026) invites us into the alchemists’ lair of filmmakers Nathaniel “Nick” Dorsky and Jerome “Jerry” Hiler, titans of American experimental cinema whose prolific output and 60-year-long romance have produced a body of work that touches the sublime. 

As lovers and co-creators, the pair work in tandem to produce films in constant dialogue with one another and the medium itself. Introduced at the Filmmakers’ Co-op in the mid-1960s by founder Jonas Mekas, Dorsky and Hiler discovered in each other kindred spirits and creative foils. Working predominantly in vivid 16mm color stock, often without sound, Dorksky and Hiler juggle the spheres of science, magic, and divine humanism in front and behind the camera, drawing on their vast knowledge of art history and industrial design to evoke stained glass, figurative painting, and kinetic abstractions. Dorsky aptly describes his Bolex as “a musical instrument,” producing images that transform our eyes into sites of multisensory perception.

Moving seamlessly between close observation, film excerpts, and archival footage, Illuminations’s casual structure befits subjects who resist categorization, letting the work speak for itself. Lifelong devotees will appreciate the “day in the life” portrait of two luminaries who spend their days shooting en plein air, thrifting antique glass, and stuffing their equally aged fridge with Kodak stock and nova lox. To the uninitiated, Dorsky and Hiler’s lived-in artistic practices and charming interviews act as a veritable masterclass in experimental film theory, equal parts process and poeticism. 

More than a dual portrait, Illuminations is at its best while following the pair during last year’s multi-venue retrospective in New York. Locals surely will appreciate familiar faces in the audience, or joining the doting lovebirds on reminiscent walks around their old downtown haunts. Feted wherever they go, from the Museum of Modern Art to the streets of Chinatown, Dorsky and Hiler accept their flowers with grace, knowing all too well that public adoration merely compliments private devotion. Their love story is a story of experimental cinema itself, and the subtle ways filmmaking can carve a path to social, sexual, and metaphysical liberation.

Illuminations screens this evening, November 13, at Village East as part of “DOC NYC.” Co-directors Patrick Shen and Brandon Vedder will be in attendance for a Q&A.
