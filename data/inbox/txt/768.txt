Film at a Distance: On Artavazd Peleshyan
Lecture and Screening
Tuesday, October 13, 2026, 7pm

Admission
General $12
Student $10

 

The Armenian director Artavazd Peleshyan (b. 1938) is the quintessential filmmaker’s filmmaker. Mysterious, elusive, and recalcitrant, his renown is based on a small body of poetic documentaries made primarily between the 1960s and early 1990s. These films showcase Peleshyan’s unique lyrical style evocative of the montage aesthetics of the 1920s Soviet avant-garde. Join us for a lecture and discussion with Daniel Schwartz and Armand Yervant Tufenkian on Artavazd Peleshyan’s theory of “distance montage,” illustrated with film excerpts. Read more here.

Charles Mudede, "Exit Envy: BLM, MAGA, and the Continuing Crisis of Longevity"
Thursday, October 15, 2026, 7pm

Free admission

There is a close connection between longevity and capitalism. The latter would certainly collapse without the former. Why? Because longevity closes the door to the afterworld and forces workers to focus on the here and now, the restless realm of capital accumulation. If life were, as Hobbes put it, “nasty, brutish, and short,” the majority of a population would denounce this fallen world and completely invest in life after death. Such workers would be useless and impossible to manage. They are always not here (the factory, the store, the warehouse) but always there, God knows where. Join us at e-flux for "Exit Envy: BLM, MAGA, and the Continuing Crisis of Longevity," a lecture by cultural critic, filmmaker, and writer Charles Mudede. Read more here.
