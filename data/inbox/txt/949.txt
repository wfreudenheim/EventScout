THE TAKING OF PELHAM ONE TWO THREE

BY JON DIERINGER
ORIGINALLY PUBLISHED SEPTEMBER 2017
OCTOBER 27, 2026

READ ON OUR SITE

In the opening minutes of The Taking of Pelham One Two Three, an MTA conductor-in-training misspeaks the name of a train stop and mumbles an apology over the PA, earning a sharp admonition from his supervisor: “Never say ya sorry, kid.”

It might as well be the mantra of one of the most wantonly, wonderfully cynical thrillers of the 1970s—a film that defines the New York fuck-you-you-fuckin'-fuck mindset and whose verve remains undiminished a half-century later as the city's transportation infrastructure continues to deteriorate. Moments after the conductor’s rookie slipup, four armed men in trench coats and Groucho Marx getups take control of the train and uncouple the lead car. With 17 passengers captive, they demand a $1 million ransom within the hour, after which they'll execute a hostage each minute. With the ball in the air, several players vie for control: the exasperated, politically calculating mayor (Lee Wallace) and his pragmatic deputy (Tony Roberts); an undercover officer hidden among the train's passengers; and transit cop Lt. Garber (Walter Matthau).

Matthau's dramatic performance capped a terrific run of neo-noir roles beginning with Charley Varrick and The Laughing Policeman the previous year. Even amid a murderers' row of supporting players including Robert Shaw, Martin Balsam, Héctor Elizondo, and Earl Hindman as the hijackers, Matthau's weary, deadpan turn outguns them all. But his background in comedy suits Pelham's droll humor, with everyone stomping around in a stubborn crisis-as-usual mode in which a hijacked downtown 6 train is just another irritation on top of political minefields, red tape, and crumbling infrastructure. Pelham doesn't play to modern political sensitivities—as one beleaguered supervisor complains, “If I've got to watch my language just because they let a few broads in, I'm going to quit. How the hell can you run a goddamn railroad without swearing?” But don’t expect an apology.
