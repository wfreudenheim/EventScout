Your guide to November at MoMA

View with images

When Constantin Brancusi first saw the New York skyline in 1926, he exclaimed, “Why, it is my studio.” Brancusi’s actual studio, on impasse Ronsin in Paris, was where he lived, worked, entertained friends and clients, and displayed his sculptures. There, he created some of his most groundbreaking works, redefining sculpture through his radically simple forms.

For the first time in decades, rarely lent sculptures from Brancusi’s studio, along with a rich selection of  photographs, films, hand-carved furniture and pedestals, tools, and archival materials, have traveled to New York and are on view in Brancusi: The Artist and His Studio.

Nov 14, 2026–Feb 15, 2027

In Na Mira’s first solo museum exhibition in New York, a newly commissioned media installation fills the Kravis Studio with shifting projections that explore the edges of perception, drawing on Korean shamanism and structural filmmaking.
