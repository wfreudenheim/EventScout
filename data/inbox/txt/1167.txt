DAY IN THE LIFE: SUNDAY AT THE BENCH WITH DEVON

By Casey Lardner • 13 Nov 2026 View in browser View in browser

 

 

Friend,

I’ve spent the past few weeks telling you about Genspace classes. But what is it like to actually teach them?

Dr. Devon Collins is a neuroscientist, Director of the Dana Frontiers Program at the Dana Foundation, and a longtime Genspace instructor. Recently, he taught Biohacker Boot Camp for our current group of Break into Biotech trainees. Come along for a day in Devon's life while he teaches the foundations of molecular biology to folks just starting on their scientific journeys. 

----------------------------------------

 

9:00 AM

It’s Sunday morning, and I’m waking up to a view of my bedroom plants, with my cats Rose and Ikki sharing my attention for once. I set a reminder to tackle some plant chores later this evening.

9:30 AM

I take my dog Tsuki out for a morning walk in St. Nicholas Park. She tried to do her best impression of the Balto statue in Central Park. I think she did okay. 

11:00 AM 

Back at home, I feed the pets (and myself) and get ready to head out to Genspace. 

11:45 AM

On my way to the lab! A disabled car on the FDR slows me down a bit, but once I pass it, it’s easy going to Sunset Park. (Don't worry, I took these photos while I was at a full stop!)

12:30 PM

At the lab, I start prepping for today’s class. Bryan and Auz have just about everything ready, but I like to make a few extra gels and label materials ahead of time to help things move along.

2:00 PM

Class time! It’s the first day of Biohacker Bootcamp with the latest Break into Biotech cohort. For most of the learners in the room, it’s their first time holding a pipette, so we first take some time to practice transferring tiny amounts of liquid accurately.

3:00 PM

Next up: DNA extraction. After all of the participants collect their own cheek cells (something known as a buccal swab), we use a buffer to break open the cells and separate the DNA from everything else. All of the pipetting skills everyone learned an hour ago are put to direct use here! 

4:00 PM

Now we set up PCR to make millions of copies of one specific stretch of DNA. This is such a fun way to learn molecular biology; ultimately, the participants will be sequencing their own mitochondrial DNA, which is passed down from mother to child and can trace your maternal line back thousands of years (what’s often called ‘genetic ancestry’).

5:00 PM

Gel electrophoresis is where it all comes together. We load our DNA samples, run a current through the gel, and watch as smaller DNA fragments travel faster than larger ones. All in all, the gel looks great, which means the experiment is successful ✅

6:00 – 7:00 PM

Before heading home, I get a few things ready for day two of class, which will take place tomorrow evening. On my way out, I stop to admire the calcium carbonate skulls Sarai has been working on. Love those two at the top, decorated like calaveras de azúcar!

 

7:15 PM

Finding time for lunch is always tricky on teaching days, so I’m starving. I grab some food from Ayat over in Industry City to take home.

8:00 PM

The drive home is quicker than the trip down to the lab. Back home, I wind down and eat dinner with my partner while we watch (way too much!) HGTV.

----------------------------------------

 

Devon has been sharing his time and expertise with learners at our lab for over 7 (!) years. His Sunday afternoon is a great glimpse into how education works at Genspace: practicing scientists teach the fundamentals while helping new learners explore questions that are anything but basic.

Importantly, this learning isn't just one-way. Teaching at Genspace is paid work for scientists. And it's a great way to grow as an educator and science communicator because working scientists have an opportunity to share what they know with people who don’t spend every day in a lab, and to learn from them in return. Our learners bring fresh questions, different expertise, and lived experience that don't always show up in traditional lab settings.

This kind of exchange between people who do science for a living and people who are invested in it for any number of reasons is just one of the many important things a community lab can offer.

Thanks for being here,

Casey Lardner, PhD

Executive Director, Genspace
