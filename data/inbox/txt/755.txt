FILM HIGHLIGHT | October 9, 2026

Peeping Tom + Southland Tales

This weekend we’re pleased to welcome the great film critic J. Hoberman, who has a new anthology of his writing titled Across the Movi-verse: Writing on Film 2011–2021 (2026, The Film Desk). One of his essays in the book concerns movies that were despised by critics and audiences upon release but were eventually reassessed many years later. As Hoberman writes, “Cursed with an unhappy destiny, a film maudit may have been ripped untimely from its director’s womb or mutilated by vengeful producers, it is often buried on release and always reviled by critics.”

Hoberman will present two such films maudit on Sunday. First, at 12:30, Richard Kelly’s 2006 multi-verse political comic book saga Southland Tales (screening on 35mm), which has been embraced by a major and passionate contingent of critics and movie-lovers. Hoberman called Southland Tales “a visionary film about the end of times. There hasn’t been anything comparable in American movies since Mulholland Drive.”  Then at 4:15, Michael Powell’s 1960 meta-slasher on voyeurism, Peeping Tom, which is now widely accepted as a horror classic.  Martin Scorsese has described Peeping Tom as a film that “says everything that can be said about filmmaking, [and] the process of dealing with film.” 

Both movies deserve the big-screen treatment. Hoberman will introduce each selection and sign copies of his new book in the lobby between the screenings.
