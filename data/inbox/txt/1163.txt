Hi Will,

Whether you joined us for last week’s event at the New York Public Library’s Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library or are coming to this conversation for the first time, thank you for caring about who gets a say in how AI shapes our communities. At How AI Civics Builds Community Power with Data & Society, our panelists explored a question we hear often amid the rapid advancement of AI: What can I do?  

Our expert panel had a lot of ideas about this, including:

 * People who are affected by AI-driven decisions should be part of the decisions being made about AI. Technical expertise is not the only kind of expertise that matters, and it must not be a prerequisite for having a voice in your school, workplace, or local government.

 * Transparency is a starting point, not the finish line. While knowing where and how AI is used matters, communities also need ways to challenge its use and shape what happens next.

 * Collective action can change the balance of power. By joining forces,  workplace organizing efforts, local campaigns, and public institutions can make demands they could not on their own.

 * A better future is about more than better tech. What do communities need and want, apart from technology? By collectively understanding their specific needs — which might include things like libraries, educational support, employment opportunities, or spaces for connection — communities can come together to demand them.

It’s essential to keep conversations like this going, and we hope you’ll bring them into the places where you live or work. How is AI being used in the institutions you’re a part of, and who has had a chance to weigh in?
