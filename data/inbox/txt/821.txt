READING AND CONVERSATION: THE CAVALIER
WITH NATHALIE QUINTANE, JONATHAN LARSON, JUSTINE LEXTRAIT, AND ANDREAS PETROSSIANTS
SUNDAY, OCTOBER 25, 6PM

Join us for an evening with French author Nathalie Quintane, around her 2021 book, The Cavalier, published in an English translation by Jonathan Larson (Winter Editions, 2025). A bilingual reading by Quintane and Larson will open onto a conversation with writers Justine Lextrait and Andreas Petrossiants on the book’s sprawling account of anticapitalist and antifascist forms of living. The Cavalier addresses the long shadow of 1968 and its utopian visions, while attending to questions of labor and social relations beyond the enclosure of the state. This discussion will follow the book’s attention to seventy years of state-sanctioned counterrevolutionary and repressive tactics and media spectacle as they come up against attempts at self-organization, radical reimaginings of the education system, and proletarian struggle outside of the metropole.

AFTER HOURS FILM SCHOOL:
NATALIA LASSALLE-MORILLO
SUNDAY, NOVEMBER 1, 2–4:30PM

After Hours Film School is a workshop series devoted to new currents in cinematic practice. The series invites moving-image makers to share new work or work-in-progress and engage with participants in a seminar-style discussion around methodology, production, and key questions animating their work. After Hours is organized by Kirsten Gill and Rachel Valinsky.

In this workshop, Natalia Lassalle-Morillo looks at the porous boundaries between theater and filmmaking within her practice, using her three-part film and theatrical project En Parábola/Conversations on Tragedy (2024–26) as a point of departure. Developed over a multiyear process with an ensemble of nontraditional performers residing across the Puerto Rican archipelago and its New York City diaspora, En Parábola/Conversations on Tragedy is a reassemblage of the Greek myth of Antigone emerging from the ensemble’s revision and rewriting of the myth through their lived experiences of migration and belonging. A project developed across lands and consciousnesses, En Parábola considers the process itself as a site of rehearsal: a meeting place where Puerto Ricans across waters come together after decades of geographical fragmentation and cumulative environmental, political, and economic tragedy, to collaborate in the reenvisioning of the classical Greek myth.

This session unpacks both films, as well as process materials, scores, and annotations created by the artist and her collaborators to explore her approach toward adapting a classical reference and developing filmic text, visual language, and characters with nontraditional performers.  Expanding on a project over ten years in the making, this After Hours opens up a dialogue around the boundaries of life, affective relationships and collaboration, and how a creative community is sustained over an extended period of time.

Format: In person
Capacity: 15 participants
Cost: $25–75 (Sliding scale, no one turned away for lack of funds)

Wendy’s Subway is a non-profit reading room, writing space, and independent publisher located in Bushwick, Brooklyn. We support emerging artists and writers in making experimental, urgent work and create alternative modes for learning and thinking in community. Wendy’s Subway is dedicated to encouraging creative, critical, and discursive engagement with arts and literature.

Our interdisciplinary programs offer free readings, talks, performances, and reading groups, as well as sliding-scale workshops and courses. Our multiseries publishing initiative includes works in translation, artists' books, poetic texts, and hybrid-genre works by time-based artists. Our non-circulating library holds a collection of over 5,000 titles, with a focus on international independent publishing, art and artists’ books, poetry, theory, and criticism.
