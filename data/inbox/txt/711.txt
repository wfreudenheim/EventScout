WE’RE SICK TO DEATH OF ALL THIS NONSENSE: DIVAS DE MAS MUERTAS

----------------------------------------

Readings, moving images, amulets, spells, and an invocation of the divas who never really leave us.

Thursday, October 8 — 7:30PM

Tomorrow! A true diva never really dies, does she? We’re Sick to Death of All This Nonsense, our multimedia series selected by poet Christine Larusso and writer and curator Rachael Rakes, returns for round four: Divas de Mas Muertas. We’re so excited to add another poet and diva into the mix for this special night — Marisa Crawford!

Bringing together live performance, reading, sound, and moving images, this edition features readings from poets Dorothea Lasky and Hafizah Geter alongside Jesse McLean’s short film Somewhere Only We Know and Cecilia Condit's Possibly in Michigan. It’s October, and we’re invoking those merely departed from this plane — the baddies in our bones, keeping us keeping the flame.

We, the nearly departed, will lay the marigolds on the altar together. We encourage all attending to bring photographs, amulets or objects of your favorite, most beloved late divas to add to our ofrenda. While we will have marigolds (cempasúchil), you are welcome to add your own. Come through!

SHOWGIRLS OF PAKISTAN

----------------------------------------

Enter the universe of three mujra dancers in Pakistan as they dodge state censors and violence to vie for stardom.

Sunday, October 18 — 7:30PM

We’re delighted to come together with our friends at 犄角旮旯 Nooks & Crannies for a special Queer History Month screening of Saad Khan’s Showgirls of Pakistan, presented with Khajistan and DecrimNY. Following three mujra dancers as they navigate the Punjabi theater business, the film draws on intimate vérité, phone calls, social media streams, and home videos to follow women determined to play by their own rules. After the screening, director Saad Khan and art director and co-editor Joey Chriqui will join us to discuss the film as well as their project Khajistan, the New York-based archive and press preserving censored, banned, and overlooked print and audiovisual culture from the Middle World. The evening will also feature a selection of Khajistan publications and merch, and will raise funds for DecrimNY’s work to decriminalize, decarcerate, and destigmatize the sex trades in New York. CAMERA MINISTRY: LENS-BASED ENCOUNTERS

----------------------------------------

A workshop exploring lens-based storytelling as a practice of presence, connection and encounter.

Nov 13 — Nov 15

In this three-day intensive workshop, photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah invites participants into the methods and philosophy behind his singular lens-based practice and what he calls the “Camera Ministry.” Moving between street photography, portraiture, and poetic documentary, we’ll consider image-making as a practice of presence and encounter: what does it mean to truly see another person, and how can the camera become a means for connection rather than simply a tool for observation? Through screenings, discussion, and hands-on exploration, participants will think through how intuition, repetition, proximity, conversation, trust, and vulnerability enter into making an image, alongside the technical knowledge that allows the camera itself to become increasingly unobtrusive.

 

Khalik Allah
Workshop Lead

Alongside Khalik, filmmaker, cinematographer, and teacher Shevaun Mizrahi will discuss her transition from cinematographer to director and the ways magical realism and vérité interact in her work, while legendary hip-hop photographer and cultural archivist Jamil GS will reflect on photography as a form of cultural memory. Participants will also have the opportunity to share their own lens-based work with Khalik in a creative feedback session, bringing questions of attention, intimacy, responsibility, and representation back into their own practices.

THIS WEEK ON THE UNDO MEMBERSHIP

----------------------------------------

Practice Thing with Carl Elsaesser

We’re back with a new Practice Thing from experimental filmmaker Carl Elsaesser, who recently led an incredible workshop here at UnionDocs titled The Magical Image: Special Effects in Documentary. Carl’s work blends genres and materials to explore the presence of history alongside intimate experiences of human connection. His films have screened widely at the Berlinale, New York Film Festival, Cinema du Réel, the National Gallery of Art, and the Criterion Channel, and he was recently honored with a 2026 Creative Capital Award. Listen in as Carl shares what he turns to when the very heady work of filmmaking leaves him feeling creatively stuck, and how stepping outside the practice altogether can sometimes offer a way back in.
