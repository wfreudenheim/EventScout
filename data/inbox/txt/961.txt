CITIZEN TOXIE: THE TOXIC AVENGER IV

TROMA-GEDDON
October 23 – November 1
52 years young, and still going strong – in a landscape littered with the remains of countless failed film studios and distributors – the proudly low-budget, defiantly tasteless, and astonishingly prolific Troma Entertainment is a goddamn national treasure! Founded in 1974 by the indefatigable Lloyd Kaufman and Michael Herz, and with a catalogue that currently numbers more than 450 titles, Troma picked up the mantle from the likes of American International Pictures and Roger Corman’s New World Pictures, extending their fast-and-dirty approach to filmmaking into the era of home video and R-rated permissiveness, and creating a space where creativity, ingenuity, collaboration, and sheer lunatic energy were more important than the niceties of good taste. Over the years, Troma has produced and/or distributed hundreds of sublimely over-the-top movies, nurtured the talents of untold numbers of filmmakers, performers, and crew members, and made an indelible mark on American popular culture, thanks to the likes of the TOXIC AVENGER series, CLASS OF NUKE ‘EM HIGH (1986), SGT. KABUKIMAN NYPD (1990), CANNIBAL! THE MUSICAL (1993), and many other lesser-known but deliriously inventive and entertaining films.

Back in the year 2000, Anthology presented a 25th-anniversary tribute to Troma. That series was wholeheartedly endorsed by Jonas Mekas – godfather of experimental cinema and co-founder of Anthology – who recognized in Lloyd Kaufman and his upstart studio a filmmaker who – however populist his sensibility and filmmaking philosophy – was in the tradition of the gleefully transgressive, anti-bourgeois, and defiantly independent spirit of much of 1950s-70s underground cinema. Twenty-six years later, Anthology welcomes Lloyd Kaufman and his team back for a new celebration of their half-century of unrestrained, raucous cinematic shenanigans – a body of work that is, simultaneously and paradoxically, politically incorrect yet politically uncompromising (the Troma filmography is suffused with Kaufman’s deep-seated anti-corporate convictions).

Featuring recent restorations of Troma classics, rare screenings of deep cuts from the company’s back-catalogue, and appearances from cast, crew, and of course Kaufman himself, this series – while it may only scratch the surface of Kaufman and company’s output – contains enough comedy, gore, and cinematic resourcefulness to animate a whole year’s worth of programming. Come dive into the world of Troma – you’ll never be quite the same again.

Special thanks to Lloyd Kaufman, Michael Herz, and Sabrina Mendoza (Troma Entertainment); Bret Berg (American Genre Film Archive); and Jonathan Lees.

BOOK RELEASE EVENT!
TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE
November 1
This evening features a screening of Eduardo Coutinho’s TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE (1984), introduced by the internationally acclaimed and Booker-shortlisted writer Itamar Vieira Junior. The evening also celebrates the English publication (by Verso) of Vieira Junior’s new novel, Saving the Fire, which presents the ghosts of a family’s past, lost amid the shadows of Brazil’s history. Vieira Junior masterfully blends the intimate journeys of his characters with faithfully rendered elements of Brazilian life in a world where the scars of colonialism still ache.

For more info about Itamar Vieira Junior’s Saving the Fire, visit the Verso Books website.

Eduardo Coutinho
TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE / CABRA MARCADO PARA MORRER
1984, 119 min, 35mm-to-digital. In Portuguese with English subtitles.
“TWENTY YEARS LATER rescues a history doubly interrupted by the brutal military coup of 1964 in Brazil: that of Peasant League activism and of Coutinho’s project, A MAN MARKED TO DIE, a film that was meant to reenact the story of Agrarian Reform struggles leading to the assassination of the Peasant League leader João Pedro Teixeira in 1962. Featuring the leader’s widow and their children, as well as people from a similar community, the film was never finished, and the crew had to flee. In 1981, Coutinho set about locating the actors, in order finally to narrate the history of the Peasant League up to 1964. The 1984 film radiates centripetally from the absence at the core of the interrupted film: its dead protagonist, the assassinated leader. Coutinho was haunted for years by the rushes hidden in a suitcase at a friend’s. He admitted that the original film patronized and idealized the people but claimed to value the unfinished A MAN MARKED TO DIE precisely because of the participation of the organized rural movement. Besides its inordinate value as a record of a traumatic history, the film marks the passage from an idealized documentary representation to a testimonial, reflexive mode incorporating memory and commentary.” –Ivone Margulies
Sun, Nov 1 at 7:00

BOOK RELEASE EVENT!
RIM BATTAL + MUSTANG
November 2
To celebrate the publication of the English translation of writer Rim Battal’s autobiographical novel I’ll Look Myself in the Eyes, Anthology presents a special screening of MUSTANG (2015), by Turkish filmmaker Deniz Gamze Ergüven, a film that’s closely tied thematically to Battal’s text.

In I’ll Look Myself in the Eyes 17-year-old Rim is caught smoking out her window in her home in Marrakesh, Morocco. This minor transgression unleashes the fury of her mother, who demands that Rim complete a gynecological test certifying her virginity. So begins a nuanced journey into the tangled web of a patriarchal society in which various generations of women seem to complicitly preserve the status quo. Ultimately, it is Rim who breaks the cycle of dysfunction, rebels against tradition, confronts betrayal from the women closest to her, and dares to be her true self.

MUSTANG, co-written by Alice Winocour, similarly explores the internalized psychological and moral impact of the patriarchy, through the story of five orphaned sisters growing up with their grandmother and uncle. When, on their way home from school, they play an innocent game with a group of boys on the beach, their grandmother flies into a rage, accusing them of having “pleasured themselves” and forbidding them from leaving the house. Things quickly escalate with the threat of arranged marriages. As in Rim’s novel, we see how far families can go wrong in their perceived mission of protecting the purity of their girls.

The screening will be followed by a bilingual reading from the novel by author Rim Battal and her translator Liza Tripp, along with a discussion and audience Q&A.

For more info about I’ll Look Myself in the Eyes, visit the Galiot Press website.

LOST & FOUND: CINE(MA)S LATINOAMERICANOS RE-UNIDOS
October 28
New York City, despite its status as a world capital of cinema, regularly misses out on screenings of many key international films. Though the exhibition of Latin American cinema in the city has drastically increased over the years, a considerable number of influential movies from the region still fail to premiere locally. In 2017, Anthology Film Archives and Cinema Tropical joined forces to create an ongoing series – programmed by Matías Piñeiro and Carlos A. Gutiérrez – of monthly screenings featuring remarkable Latin American films making their local premiere. We paused the series (formerly entitled “If You Can Screen It There”) following the pandemic, but we’re delighted to revive it now, rechristened as “Lost & Found: Cine(ma)s Latinoamericanos Re-Unidos”. Far from minor works, the films included here are by some of the region’s most important filmmakers, have garnered major awards at international festivals, and provide an important window into the often-overlooked world of Latin American cinema.

Co-presented by Anthology Film Archives and Cinema Tropical. Programmed by Matías Piñeiro and Carlos A. Gutiérrez.

U.S. PREMIERE!
Nuria Ibáñez Castañeda
EL GUARDIÁN
Mexico/Spain, 2025, 102 min, DCP. In Spanish with English subtitles.
Basilio is hired to watch over a remote beach on Mexico's Baja California coast, a seemingly idyllic stretch of shoreline where little appears to happen. But beneath the tranquil surface lies a community governed by its own codes, where illicit activities coexist with everyday life. Guided by an unwavering moral compass, Basilio is determined to do what he believes is right – only to discover that justice is far more elusive than he imagined. Set against a breathtaking coastal landscape, EL GUARDIÁN, the striking fiction debut by acclaimed documentary filmmaker Nuria Ibáñez (THE NAKED ROOM, A WILD STREAM), is a gripping and quietly unsettling meditation on ethics, belonging, and the fragile boundaries between duty and survival.
Wed, Oct 28 at 7:00. Followed by a Q&A with director Nuria Ibáñez Castañeda!

COMING SOON:
• GERMAN PUNK
November 4 – 11
On these shores we tend to think of the punk movement as one that, in its heyday, was predominantly an American and British phenomenon. But punk music, punk culture, and its ethos spread quickly and widely in the late 1970s and 80s, to (among many other places) Australia, Japan, Brazil, and to the countries of the Eastern bloc in Europe. This series – guest-programmed by Sukhdev Sandhu, Sara Stevenson, and Zach Feldman – focuses on the impact and development of punk in East and West Germany in the 1970s-90s. The series gathers a wide variety of films – all of them shot between 1979-99 and most of them very rarely screened in the U.S. – that document not only the bands that proliferated throughout Germany at the time, but the social and economic factors that influenced their emergence, and the response of German society at large. Taking place in West Berlin, East Berlin, the industrial Ruhr region, and elsewhere – both before and after the fall of the Wall – they represent a fascinating and vibrant snapshot of an era, and feature invaluable contemporaneous glimpses of bands and musicians including Einstürzende Neubauten, Die Tödliche Doris, Die Kanzlers, Das Freie Orchester, and Nina Hagen. This series is co-presented with the German Film Office and the Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung—New York Office, as part of a multi-city initiative of the Goethe-Institut marking the 50th anniversary of the punk movement; with support from DEFA Film Library at UMass Amherst.

• WHITE SNAIL – NEW YORK PREMIERE! FILMMAKERS IN PERSON!
November 7 – 9
“Following two unconventional documentaries – SPACE DOGS (2019) and DREAMING DOGS (2024) – focused on the lives of stray canines, filmmaking duo Elsa Kremser and Levin Peter return with the narrative feature WHITE SNAIL, where the two protagonists also exhibit a fondness for various animals. Set in contemporary Belarus, the film tells the story of Masha, a blonde, thin, fair-skinned model pursuing her dream job in China, and Misha, a tattooed, thickset painter working at a morgue. Played with extreme naturalness by non-professionals Marya Imbro and Mikhail Senkov, these two loners strike up an unlikely but profound friendship that will force them to confront their secrets and wounds...By depicting how we and our bodies relate to sight and touch, life and death, reality and imagination, flaws and perfection, Kremser and Peter map two universes on to one another, leaving the viewer to determine where they intersect and whether they diverge.” –Cristina Álvarez López, VIENNALE

• J. HOBERMAN: ACROSS THE MOVI-VERSE
November 10
To celebrate the publication – by the Film Desk – of Across the Movi-verse: Writing on Film, 2011–2021, a new collection by the one and only J. Hoberman, Anthology has invited Hoberman to guest-curate two evenings worth of programs, featuring films discussed in the pages of the new volume. Gathering together pieces written by Hoberman since his departure from the Village Voice in 2010, and originally appearing in Film Comment, Artforum, The Nation, Harper’s, Tablet, and elsewhere, Across the Movi-verse is a characteristically invaluable treasure trove of essays, articles, and reviews by one of the most thoughtful, penetrating, wide-ranging, and culturally astute critical minds in contemporary film culture. For the Anthology screenings, we’ll be devoting one evening to Jack Smith (via Jerry Tartaglia’s archival reclamation HAMLET IN THE RENTED WORLD and Smith’s beloved COBRA WOMAN) and one to a very special pairing of the 1960s sci-fi oddity THE CREATION OF THE HUMANOIDS (reportedly a favorite of Andy Warhol’s) and its spawn, the Kuchars’ SINS OF THE FLESHAPOIDS.
