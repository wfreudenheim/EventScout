Artists on the mysterious histories of the sea
Artists on the mysterious histories of the sea

View with images

This month, we’re bringing together three powerful discussions about water, the life force that surrounds us.

“You can slow down and pay attention both to the natural world and technology,” the artist Jakob Kudsk Steensen recently explained in an interview about his installation at MoMA. His real-time simulation Berl-Berl examines Berlin’s forgotten wetlands, integrating game engine technologies, ecological research, and local folklore. Steensen hopes his story of Berlin’s swamps might allow an audience to “feel emotionally connected to that place in a way they may have forgotten.” Read the interview, then come experience Berl-Berl yourself.

Artist Torkwase Dyson calls scuba diving a “sacred practice.” In our short documentary, we go into the ocean with Dyson to explore a lesser-known aspect of her process, revealing how her experiences underwater shape the materials, histories, and depth of her paintings and installations.

Bettie-Sue Hertz, director of Columbia University’s Wallach Gallery, looks at recent art depicting the grueling labor of “shipbreaking” at the end of a container ship’s life. These projects are a kind of sequel to Allan Sekula’s 1995 meditation on the shipping industry, Fish Story, recently called “the greatest sea-faring epic in contemporary art” by the New York Times.
