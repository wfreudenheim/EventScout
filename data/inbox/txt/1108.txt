THE SET PIECES OF MARTHA GRAHAM'S PERFORMANCES

Monday, November 16 | 7 PM | Online
Sculpture and set objects play a central role in Martha Graham's dances. In works like Appalachian Spring, Isamu Noguchi's minimalist frames do more than divide space—they shape choreography and express American movement. Former Graham student Alexa West drew on her dual background in dance and visual arts to research Graham's sculptural set designs across pieces like Maple Leaf Rag and Acrobats of God. As part of her Dance Research Fellowship at the Library, she created original set elements and a dance that activates them. Watch the online rebroadcast of the 2024 Dance Division Symposium to see how the Dance Research Fellows engaged with the Martha Graham Dance Company archives.

LYNN DALLY ON JIMMY SLYDE AND THE JAZZ TAP ENSEMBLE

Wednesday, November 18 | 1 PM | Library for the Performing Arts & Online
In this month's edition of The Dance Historian Is In, Lynn Dally explores the extraordinary partnership between the Jazz Tap Ensemble and legendary tap master Jimmy Slyde. Through rehearsal clips of Slyde, Dally, and a young Derick Grant in Boston alongside ensemble footage from Los Angeles, Dally illustrates how Slyde's core "motifs" developed into the work Interplay for its 1995 Joyce premiere—and how his creative vision for the piece continued to evolve across more than a decade in the company's touring repertoire.

WATCH A NEW RESTORATION OF WILLIAM GREAVES' THE FIGHT

Thursday, November 19 | 6:30 PM | Library for the Performing Arts
Watch a new restoration of William Greaves' 1973 documentary, The Fight, about Muhammad Ali's 1971 comeback fight with Joe Frazier. Greaves' film explores the socio-political dynamics and the nature of sports promotion surrounding the match. This special screening is the result of a six-year restoration collaboration between The New York Public Library, Criterion Collection, and The Film Foundation.

HOW BUDDY BRADLEY'S DANCE GENIUS TRANSFORMED BALLET MOVEMENT 

Monday, November 23 | 6 PM | Library for the Performing Arts
In her new biography Feel the Floor, Maureen Footer explores how jazz dancer and choreographer Buddy Bradley influenced mid 20th–century dance, inspiring legends like George Balanchine, Frederick Ashton, Jerome Robbins, and Agnes de Mille. A panel of tap, jazz, and ballet dancers will discuss and demonstrate how genres cross-pollinate. Dancers will demonstrate these creative synergies through works like The Four Temperaments, Who Cares?, Rubies and Concerto Barocco.

NEW MUSIC COMPOSITIONS MADE AT AND INSPIRED BY THE LIBRARY

Monday, December 7 | 6 PM | Library for the Performing Arts
Composition students from The New School perform their original music compositions inspired by the archive of the Library for the Performing Arts. Saxophonist, composer, and educator Jane Ira Bloom encourages her students to immerse themselves in rare manuscripts, archival recordings, score manuscripts, and historic documents from the Library, synthesizing these primary source materials into vibrant new performance pieces. The resulting showcase highlights innovative multi-genre works that bridge past and present, celebrating the enduring power of the Library's performing arts archives to generate contemporary artistic dialogue and performance.

MEG BOOKER ON THE NOYES SCHOOL OF RHYTHM

Wednesday, December 9 | 1 PM | Library for the Performing Arts & Online
In 1912, women's suffrage performer and progressive education advocate Florence Fleming Noyes founded the Noyes School of Rhythm. The school has maintained over a century of continuous history, primarily through annual summer programming at Shepherd's Nine in Portland, CT, where several generations of dancers, living in community, have preserved and passed down Noyes' image-driven somatic dance practice. Meg Brooker, dance professor and certified teacher of Noyes Rhythm, joins our Dance Historian program to contextualize the Noyes School within the early modern dance landscape, including intersections with Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn.

DISCOVER THE PETS BEHIND THE PERFORMING ARTISTS

Through May 8, 2027 | Library for the Performing Arts
History abounds with performing artists devoted to their animal companions. From Billie Holiday's boxer Mister and Anna Pavlova's pet swan Jack to Larry Kramer's beloved hound Molly, animals have quietly shaped the lives and work of some of the world's most iconic performers. Drawing from the rich archives of the three Library for the Performing Arts' divisions—the Jerome Robbins Dance Division, the Billy Rose Theatre Division, and the Music and Recorded Sound Division—Unleashed: Pets of the Performing Arts gives center stage to the animals behind the artists. The result of a collaboration between the assistant curators of each division, Jenai Cutcher, Annemarie van Roessel, and Jessica Wood, this exhibition celebrates a unique cast of companions who mastered the very instincts performance demands.
