The Play's the Thing
 
24 October 2026
247 East 82nd Street
New York, NY 10028
 

 

This roundtable explores the nature of the stage play, and what it takes to ideate, develop, and present them. The panel will pose and examine queries surrounding both artistic philosophy and audience access: In an age of flourishing multimedia, what exactly defines a play? How does the collaborative nature of this art form impact the dynamics among creative team members? How does a playwright know that a play is “finished”? Why does watching a play live as an audience member land in our bodies differently than watching a movie on a screen, and what importance do such communal experiences hold in a post-pandemic world? Our discussion will span from the Ancient Greeks’ psychological theories surrounding the social benefits of catharsis to the current systems sustaining the American theater.

 

This roundtable will also be streamed live and can be watched on Youtube.

THE PARTICIPANTS

Mia Fowler is a New York based actor and writer (and sometimes director, dramaturg, and producer). While she cannot help but love the classics, she is passionate about new work that allows folks to bring their full divinity to the room. Recent credits include: Off-Broadway: As You Like It (The Public Theater, Mobile Unit); New York: benevolent (NYT Critic’s Pick, Good Apples Collective), Object Permanence (Kestrels), cityscrape (Good Apples Collective), before the flood (The Chain Theatre); Regional: You Are What You Eat (Westerly Theatre Group), Much Ado About Nothing (Utah Shakespeare Festival), The Wolves (Colorado Springs FAC); TV/Film: Adults (FX), Dumb Money (Sony Pictures).

Mia has been published in Lit Hub and maintains a Substack on books and theater. B.A. Yale University.
 

Billy McEntee is a Brooklyn-based writer. He is Theater Editor of The Brooklyn Rail, a publication that has been free and independent for 25 years, where Billy commissions essays and Rail Shorts (short scripts from playwrights) while also contributing monthly essays, often on experimental plays with shorter runs that receive less coverage. These responses aim to crack open a play to reveal its dramaturgy, rigor, and intention. As a freelance arts journalist, Billy has contributed to outlets including Time Out New York, The Boston Globe, The Washington Post, San Francisco Chronicle, Interview, Vanity Fair, The Nation, and Hyperallergic. Billy is the winner of the 2024-25 George Jean Nathan Award for Dramatic Criticism, a 2025 Silvers Grant, and the inaugural Helbing Fellowship for the American Theatre Critics Association.

Billy is also a site-specific theatermaker; his plays The Voices in Your Head, staged in 2024 at St. Lydia’s, and Slanted Floors, staged in 2025 and 2026 in his Greenpoint apartment, were both Drama Desk Award nominees for Unique Theatrical Experience. Billy has also taught with The School of The New York Times and the American College Theater Festival. Billy graduated from Boston College and is originally from New Jersey.
 

Sophie McIntosh (she/her) is a playwright from Wisconsin currently based in New York. Alongside director Nina Goodheart, Sophie is the co-founder of Good Apples Collective, a developmental orchard for new theatrical works.

Recent productions include macbitches (New York Times Critic’s Pick), the premieres of benevolent (New York Times Critic’s Pick), Road Kills (recommended by The New York Times and The New Yorker), cunnicularii, and cityscrape with Good Apples Collective, and Eleven Weeks of Nuclear Summer at Notre Dame University and the University of Michigan. Her latest play, what if everyone lives, will premiere at The Tank in January.

She is a proud recipient of a BA in drama from the University of Wisconsin-Stevens Point and recently graduated with an MFA in playwriting from Columbia University.
 

Katie Devin Orenstein (she/her) is a director, writer, producer, and intimacy choreographer born and raised in New York City. She has worked with Berkshire Playwrights Lab, New York Theatre Workshop, The Drama League (NV/NV Fellow), The Muny, Guggenheim Works & Process, Irony Point, and indie companies including Firebird and Imaginarium. She trained in intimacy choreography with Chelsea Pace and Theatrical Intimacy Education.

She graduated from Dartmouth College in 2023 with a degree in Film, Theater, and Middle Eastern Studies, and is a two-time winner of Dartmouth’s Eleanor Frost Playwriting Award and winner of the Clifford Gurdin Undergraduate Directing Prize. She currently works in Broadway production, including Purlie Victorious, Our Town, and Maybe Happy Ending.
