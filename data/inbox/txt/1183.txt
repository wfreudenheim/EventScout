nuvolario is an assault to the sky by theatre-maker Filippo Andreatta, an observation of the elusive vertigo of clouds and their precipitation in our present.

As a celestial practice, nuvolario (Italian word for “cloud atlas”) takes its name from atlases meteorologists and scholars began compiling in early XIX century attempting to name and identify those unclassifiable phenomena. Conceived as a cloud in constant transformation, nuvolario has each time different forms and yet deeply interconnected; performance, music theatre, installation, chamber theatre, text, exhibition. At their centre are clouds, whose material and immaterial dimensions become instruments of imagination, perception and knowledge.

This performative practice now takes exhibition form with nuvolario #A_Cloud_In_A_Room, hosted in the Project Room of MUSE—Science Museum of Trento. Photographs, archival videos, and research materials trace an extensive investigation in which artistic vision and scientific inquiry remain in constant dialogue, suggesting that imagination is an essential component of scientific rationality.

Long relegated to the realm of vagueness because of their tendency to obscure astronomical references, clouds have often been regarded as enemies of clear thinking or reduced to romantic images of inner states. Yet they are also “living documents” capable of deciphering reality: they expose the deceptive transparency of air and reveal how celestial landscapes are shaped by the similar dynamics of power and violence that traverse the earth.

The clouds in the Project Room draw on the disparate meteors Andreatta has encountered: from the Svalbard archipelago to the literary worlds evoked in research texts. The clouds he has created in theatres become forms to contemplate, phenomena to investigate, and traces of a world in transformation, all at once. A taxonomy of celestial and stage forms, which share ephemeral nature:

nuvolario #Music_for_18_Musicians materialises the breaths of Steve Reich’s masterpiece as clouds. A tribute to the visual power of clouds in the simplest of theatrical actions; to see what is not there, a theatre of air. An ocular examinationinto the persistence of clouds in our imagination transforming inner landscapes into outer ones, the appearance and disappearance of clouds into the moment of suspension between inhalation and exhalation.

nuvolario #Helena evokes Helen of Sparta through Euripides’ and Anne Carson’s reinterpretations of the Homeric myth, presenting her as an evanescent meteor that defies classification. Mythology and hydrology reveal the logics of domination that discipline landscapes and bodies through maps, borders, and systems of classification. Against the fixity of the line, the performance sets the mutability of water as it evaporates, a substance that continually eludes control.

nuvolario #miniatura is a chamber theatre piece that draws the audience closer, collapsing representational distance through minute scenic and celestial artifices of a mini-scientific-theatre with music by Luois Andriessen. Minute atmospheric set-designs overturn nineteenth-century Romanticism revealing material clouds made of organic matter, inert particles, and everything that evaporates from the ground.

On December 5 and 6, the exhibition will host performances of nuvolario #miniatura.

Filippo Andreatta is a theatre-maker and visual artist creating works that disrupt the hierarchy of sight and listening; music theatre, installation, performance, opera and unconventional formats in urban, rural and extreme contexts.
