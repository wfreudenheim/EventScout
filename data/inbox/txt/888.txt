UPCOMING EVENTS FROM CHINA BOOKS REVIEW

China Books Review, a digital magazine focused on all things China and bookish, is a joint project of Asia Society and The Wire China. In addition to publishing features, reviews, interviews, and podcasts, China Books Review hosts book talks and book clubs, including the upcoming events featured below.

Subscribe to their newsletter for the latest publication updates, event announcements, and more.

EMILY FENG WITH MICHAEL FORSYTHE: OUR HEARTS BURNED FOR HOME

October 28 from 6:30—8:00 PM

Drawing on years of reporting across China, Central Asia, Turkey and the Middle East, NPR correspondent Emily Feng uncovers the little-known journey of Uyghur families and militants who fled China’s intensifying surveillance state and mass repression in Xinjiang. Exile led them into the chaos of the Syrian Civil War, where some Uyghur militants became an unexpected and influential force in the conflict that helped reshape the region and contributed to the eventual collapse of Bashar al-Assad's regime. 

Feng will be joined in conversation by New York Times journalist Michael Forsythe.

XIAO HAI WITH TONY HAO: ADRIFT IN THE SOUTH

October 30 from 6:30—8:00 PM

Xiao Hai is author of Adrift in the South, a gripping autobiographical account of his 23 years as a migrant laborer, working 17 different jobs across seven cities in China. As migrant worker writing gains increasing recognition both within China and internationally, Adrift in the South offers a vital lens into questions of class, mobility, inequality and belonging in contemporary Chinese society. 

Xiao Hai will be in conversation with Adrift in the South translator Tony Hao, moderated by China Books Review editor Alec Ash.

On November 3, China Books Review will host an intimate book club discussion of Adrift in the South. Register to attend here.

JUNG CHANG WITH JIAYANG FAN: FLY, WILD SWANS

November 20 from 6:30—8:00 PM

China Books Review hosts a special conversation with Jung Chang, the internationally acclaimed author of the multimillion-copy bestseller Wild Swans, as she discusses her powerful new memoir, Fly, Wild Swans: My Mother, Myself, and China. Moderated by New Yorker staff writer Jiayang Fan, this event offers a rare opportunity to hear from one of the most influential chroniclers of modern Chinese history and memory.
