Paris Photo Book Talks

Curated by Printed Matter

Nov 12–15th, 2026

Hans Gremmen, In Between, An Incomplete Index (2001-2026) – Courtesy of the artist and FW:Books

Printed Matter is pleased to collaborate with Paris Photo for the second year of curated Book Talks at the Grand Palais. The program includes 19 conversations held over the course of four days in the Galerie Courbe, situated alongside the Book Sector, which brings together dozens of publishers and specialist booksellers. The discussions pair photographers, publishers, designers, critics, and curators to highlight newly released publications,  and to encourage a lively and generative dialogue around the contemporary photobook and artists’ book publishing today.

Highlighted programs include Sofia Coppola and Michael Mack on publishing and the influence of photobooks;  Alejandro Cartagena and Patrick Pound in conversation about archives & found photos; Adam Rouhana in conversation with Taous Dahmani; Pia Paulina Guilmoth in conversation with Gem Fletcher, and programs with Hans Gremmen, Elena Helfrecht, Gerald Cyrus, Gabriele Stötzer, and many others.

The Book Talks are curated by Lesley A. Martin, Executive Director of Printed Matter, with the support of Keith Gray and in collaboration with Paris Photo.

Fishworm

Pia Paulina Guilmoth & Jesse Bull Saffire

Void
Athens, Greece, 2025

$70

Guilmoth and Saffire have been scavenging within a sixty-mile orbit of their home, collecting family photos, town reports, forgotten newspapers, and fragments of lives slipping out of memory. ‘Fishworm’ collects these rescues, re-assemblies, and accidents, transforming the cast-offs of rural central Maine into a jumbled chronicle, with material both tender and grotesque, mundane and uncanny. All in all, ‘Fishworm’ is about dykes digging through trash. Signed by Pia Paulina Guilmoth. Includes a poster dustjacket.

Purchase

Ground Rules

Alejandro Cartagena

Aperture
NY, New York, 2025

$65

The first comprehensive, bilingual survey of photographer Alejandro Cartagena, whose work charts the dreams and dystopias of Mexico today. Known for his photobooks, Cartagena explores the politics of the US-Mexico border, suburban sprawl, and increasing wealth disparities in North America. Bringing together documentary photography, collage, vernacular photographs, and AI-generated imagery, his work addresses Mexico’s most pressing social and environmental issues with humor and pathos.

Purchase

Harlem Nights 1990–2001

Gerald Cyrus

Cafe Royal Books
London, UK, 2022

$10

In 1990, Gerald Cyrus moved from Los Angeles to New York City. While studying at the School of Visual Arts in 1992, he interned at the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture under Deborah Willis. During this time, Cyrus photographed Harlem’s nightclubs and the vibrant music scene of the historic neighborhood.

Purchase

Love Pictures

Jess T. Dugan & Charlotte Cotton

Radius Books
2026

$75

Love Pictures, a collaboration between photographer Jess T. Dugan and writer Charlotte Cotton, centers around conversations exploring the core themes of Dugan’s practice, including gender and identity, family and politics, writing and language, the book as object, and the dynamics of the exhibition space. Featuring intimate exchanges with friends, colleagues, mentors, and subjects of Dugan’s work—including Dawoud Bey, Kelli Connell, and Dorothy Moss—the conversations are woven together with the first comprehensive survey of Dugan’s work in book form.

CORRESPONDENCES: DE APPEL 1975–2025

Archival display at Printed Matter

November 10 - 20, 2026

Conversation: November 11, 6-8pm

Join us for the opening of an archival display of Correspondences: de Appel 1975–2025, a publication drawing on fifty years of correspondence from de Appel Archive to trace the institution’s relational history. Jeremy Johnston and Ben DuVall (Darling Green), Martha Jager (independent curator/editor), Nell Donkers (de Appel Archive) and Rachel Churner (director, Carolee Schneemann Foundation) will explore the nature of the archive and how it reveals the contributions of many artists and cultural workers through letters, telegrams, postcards, faxes, and emails over a 50 year period.

PANEL DISCUSSION:

HOMES FOR PEOPLE, NOT FOR PROFIT

with Art Against Displacement, Bench Ansfield, Renee Keitt, Alex Strada, and Storefront for Art and Architecture

Join us for a panel discussion on the financialization of housing by public-private mechanisms and tenant-led resistance, held in conjunction with Homes for People, Not for Profit, our window installation now on view that reactivates Homeless At Home, a 1985–86 project in which dozens of artists and architects designed and deployed stencils about the necessity of homes for everyone. Storefront for Art and Architecture, Alex Strada, and AAD will introduce their intertwined projects, followed by presentations from Bench Ansfield, author of Born in Flames: The Business of Arson and the Remaking of the American City, and Renee Keitt, president of the Elliott-Chelsea Houses Tenants Association and member of FEC Tenants Against Demolition.
