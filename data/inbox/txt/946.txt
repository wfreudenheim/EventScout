Dear readers, 

It’s been fifteen years since n+1 founding editor Chad Harbach published his beloved first novel, The Art of Fielding. As Dwight Garner points out in his New York Times review of Harbach’s highly anticipated followup, The Brightness:

> Fifteen years is a long time. Fifteen years ago, Taylor Swift was a country singer and Prince William still had hair. Yet “The Brightness” is so full of life, surprises, close observation, spiraling and interlocking plots, sharp new characters wheeling into view, striking set pieces, overlapping literary allusions and neo-Updikean craftsmanship that one can feel, on nearly every page, where every month of that time went.

Today, The Brightness is finally here, and we hope you’ll agree with us—and with Garner—that it is well worth the wait. 

Every copy of the book ordered through the n+1 shop will be signed, so be sure to get yours now! We can’t wait for you to return to the world of Westish.

Yours, 
The Editors

P.S. If you’re in New York, don’t miss Chad in conversation with Leslie Jamison at The Strand tonight at 7 PM! There are still spots left.

Praise for The Brightness

“Beautiful and perceptive… Passages like [his] are not only a joy to read―they convey something fundamental about people, who are always in conflict with themselves, managing a jumble of desires that pull in wildly different and sometimes mutually exclusive directions.”― The New Yorker

“[Harbach’s] stuff has a candor, simplicity and seriousness that puts you in mind of the work of Sherwood Anderson (born in Ohio), Willa Cather (raised in Nebraska) and Jonathan Franzen (born in Illinois).” — The New York Times

“You might think of this novel as a rebuttal or counterpoint to its forerunner, a book that replaces roseate campus fantasy with spikier societal conflicts . . . [Harbach’s] characterizations are generous and insightful.”― The Wall Street Journal

“Masterfully captures the dazzling ennui of being in your 20s.”― Marie Claire

“What may surprise readers this time out is the depth of feeling and insight Harbach brings to The Brightness’s female characters, especially Pella and Irma. The ache that sheltered, studious Irma feels to be closer to the wilder and more charismatic Dahlia rings with authenticity, as does the mix of pain, jealousy, and pride Pella experiences watching Irma come into her own.”― Publishers Weekly

“Brilliant, poignant, and absorbing.”―Kirkus Reviews (starred)
