FREE TALK: ANDREW HAIGH & IRA SACHS

Andrew Haigh (A Long Winter) and Ira Sachs (The Man I Love) reflect on the legacy of 1990s New Queer Cinema, the expressive functions of naturalism and melodrama, and the challenges of dramatizing untold histories of queer life for a contemporary audience.

Article preview: Arthur Harari’s The Unknown

“The Unknown is notably uninterested in solving its implicit mysteries: how and why exactly the body swaps happen, how widespread the phenomenon may be, how it might be reversed, and so on. What emerges instead, as the film allows the implications of its outlandish premise to sink in, is an unnerving thought experiment about the utter strangeness of consciousness and embodiment.”

—Arthur Harari on The Unknown by Dennis Lim

STAFF SPOTLIGHT

BETSY BLACK – FRONT OF HOUSE STAFF

What film are you most excited to see at NYFF64 and why? 

The Man I Love! Ira Sachs speaks a cinematic language that resonates with me deeply and I am thrilled that he has my favorite actor, Rami Malek, starring in his newest film. Feels like an early Christmas present!

What do you love about NYFF? 

It’s always bittersweet when the summer ends, and so I think NYFF is the perfect way to usher in the start to a new season. There’s a buzz and a chill in the air, and it feels so good to be at Lincoln Center surrounded by movie lovers from all over the world.
