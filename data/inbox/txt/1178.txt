COMMUNITY DIGEST

Whether you're unfolding a road atlas, searching for buried pirate treasure, studying the MTA routes, or following the GPS to your final destination, maps are awesome! From early cartography to online guides, maps play such an integral role in our society. In my life, I remember getting the maps at entrances to museums and amusement parks and taking pride in being my group's guide. Maps don’t only help us get from point A to B; they also help us identify demographics and societal trends. They help us find coffee and gas stations, and they help us identify community.

Organizations are getting more specific and creative in how they are using maps to help unite people and tell our stories. By now you should all know about our Queens Jazz Trail Map, but here are some others you might find interesting: Our friends at the Clemente have made a wonderful NY Latino Historias (Historial) map. Museum Hue has a statewide BIPOC arts map.

Here at FTH, we have our wonderful Queens Art Map, a project aimed at documenting the borough’s dynamic artistic and cultural landscape. This also led to our Mapping Queens panels and Queens Cultural Connect, a one-stop digital hub for everything arts and culture in Queens, connecting artists, collectives, organizations, and opportunities in a single centralized space. If you’re not looking for them, you might not always see that in NYC there are maps everywhere. This week, while you’re out and about, try to identify the maps in your everyday travels, and think about some of your favorite maps. I’m sure they bring you some joy.

Palante (Onward) 

—Eliel Lucero
