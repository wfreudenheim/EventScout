John Fitzgerald and Godfrey Reggio, Techno Firma, 2026. Three-channel interactive installation with custom computer software (machine learning model), computer vision sensors, and HD color video, with sound, infinite duration.

MORE INFORMATION

Techno Firma is the first artist-driven AI exhibition at Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM), featuring a monumental interactive sculpture by John Fitzgerald and Godfrey Reggio. Audiences constantly transform a massive prism of moving images to experience how humanity’s growing dependence on technology is reshaping our planet.

This immersive artwork reimagines Godfrey Reggio’s groundbreaking 1982 film Koyaanisqatsi. Using a custom machine learning model that John Fitzgerald trained on the Reggio Foundation Archive, vast natural landscapes, industrial sites, and cities appear and collapse across three screens on a colossal obelisk.

For the first time at PAMM, visitors shape how a digital artwork unfolds through their movement, numbers, and attention. In the gallery, computer vision responds to audience activity, linking participants with worlds and soundscapes in real time. A rocket launch, a factory line, and a forest in retreat all pulse with visitor presence, creating a collective experience of how people—and their use of technology—impact the Earth.

Techno Firma is co-produced by Onassis ONX, Epson, and Pérez Art Museum Miami. The exhibition is organized by Lauren Monzón, PAMM New Media Program Manager, and Jazia Hammoudi, Independent Curator. Ongoing support for PAMM’s project galleries and digital initiatives from the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation is gratefully acknowledged.

ARTPORT: A HISTORY OF INTERNET ART

OPENS NOV 21 AT THE WHITNEY, WITH A NEW COMMISSION BY JAZSALYN

MORE INFORMATION

artport: A History of Internet Art marks the twenty-fifth anniversary of artport, the Whitney’s online gallery space for commissions of net art, which launched in 2001. Since its inception, artport has presented a snapshot of the varied practices artists use to engage with an ever-evolving online environment. Taking place online and in the gallery, the exhibition tells a history of the evolution of net art over a quarter century and features more than one hundred works commissioned specifically for artport.

The exhibition presented on the Whitney Museum’s sixth floor is structured around the three main commissioning categories of the artport website: the Gate Pages, created from 2001 to 2006 as portals to artists’ websites; the main Commissions, which have been ongoing since 2001; and the Sunrise/Sunset series, which ran from 2009 to 2024, along with its successor, On the Hour. In the galleries, the open floor plan creates the impression of floating browser windows, bringing the history of online art into physical space.

Still from Jazsalyn: Warpmode, This is not a game! 2026

The performance on November 21st at 1PM EST / 8PM EET will be livestreamed to audiences worldwide, including at Onassis Ready in Athens, as part of Extended Bodies, Screens, Worlds (CIRCLE Expanded Orbit × Onassis ONX). HELLO YOU PREMIERES IN THE IMMERSIVE NON-FICTION COMPETITION AT IDFA DOCLAB

MORE INFORMATION

Anagram’s new interactive installation, Hello You, premieres in the Immersive Non-Fiction Competition at the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam (IDFA), opening on November 12. Directed by ONX Fellow May Abdalla and Barry Gene Murphy, the work continues the themes of Anagram’s “Playing With Reality” series, exploring mental health and self-perception through immersive technology and embodied storytelling.

For many of us, looking in a mirror is already an act of comparison: with a younger self, an imagined self, or the person we think we ought to be. Hello You explores how memories, expectations, and judgments become entangled with what we see. Through light, sound and illusion, a familiar daily encounter becomes strange. How do you recognize yourself? What makes a body feel like yours? And what happens when the image you know begins to slip?

Hello You is an autopsy of your reflection. Enter a darkened room for an intimate encounter with the person looking back. This mirror has two sides: one that changes with what you look for, and another that follows what you miss. Exploring stories of altered perception, from phantom limbs to the feeling of being a stranger in your own body, the installation stretches the split-second act of self-recognition into something you can watch unfold. Here you are invited to examine the lens through which you see yourself.

Six Rings: A New Athens Space Combining Fashion and Culture

Athens, Greece

Launching in Dec 2026

In December 2026, the Onassis Foundation will launch Six Rings at 99 Patission Boulevard in the heart of Athens, a new cultural-retail experience created by Afroditi Panagiotakou, Artistic Director of the Onassis Foundation, and Stavros Karelis, the Head of Fashion at the Onassis Foundation. More than a store, Six Rings takes the form of a two-story house, open to the public and designed to invite curiosity and discovery, as well as learning through workshops and mentoring initiatives. Six Rings takes shape as an urban experience, weaving fashion and culture into the fabric of everyday life. At its core is a curated selection of Greek and international brands. Beyond fashion, the space’s atmosphere will be further enriched by special commissions and collaborations with artists from the Onassis Foundation’s cultural program, spanning music, dance, theater, visual arts, and digital culture, as well as with Onassis ONX, the Foundation’s global platform for art and advanced technologies, active in Athens and New York.

Funny Weather by Theo Triantafyllidis, a Behind the Screen Commission
Factory International, Manchester, UK

On view from Nov 11 to Mar 26

Quiet cloud formations transition into epic sunsets. Migrating flocks become colourful kites. Experience a weather forecast for a world changing faster than can be predicted in Funny Weather – a new live artwork now on view at Factory International. Unpredictable and ever changing, Funny Weather confronts a new era of climate uncertainty in which the weather is a main character in our lives. Combining real-time atmospheric data with recent bird sightings and bespoke locally hosted generative technology, termed an AI Oracle by the artist, Funny Weather creates a constantly evolving picture of the skies above us.

Funny Weather is the first commission by Behind the Screen – a brand-new initiative to support the development of digital art and the evolution of screen-based practices. The network is led by Factory International with three of the world's leading institutions in this field: Australian Centre for the Moving Image (Melbourne), Onassis ONX (Athens + New York) and M+ (Hong Kong).

Open Call: Rhizome Microgrants

Applications close on Nov 20

If you are seeking funds to help realize a creative project related to digital art and networked culture, consider applying to Rhizome’s annual microgrants open call. Since 2014, Rhizome has awarded small grants ($500-$1500) to support the creation of new web-based works. In recent years, awarded projects have included a multilingual archive of printed “Internet directory” books, a browser-based adventure game where you care for “small but alive” creatures, a free pop-up school in Portland, hacking inactive Twitter accounts to acquire the username “everyoneisugly,” and many more.

Free Yourself from Big Tech

MoMI William Fox Amphitheater

Nov 13 at 5:00 PM

A practical workshop for taking back control of your digital life, followed by a reception. Big Tech knows what you search, who you know, and what you buy. And increasingly, AI companies know what you ask, write, and create. This hands-on workshop led by Prashast Thapan explores practical alternatives to some of the biggest technology platforms and services we use every day. We’ll look at open-source tools, such as local AI models, that can run directly on your own computer without connecting to the cloud, alternative search and communication platforms, and simple ways to keep more of your data under your control.

Naoko Tosa: Generative Beings, Art & Technology Today

Kyoto City KYOCERA Museum of Art, on view from Nov 7 - 22

In an age of AI-generated art, let nature create its own art. This exhibition offers a comprehensive overview of the trajectory of "creation," a theme contemporary artist and ONX Fellow Naoko Tosa has explored over approximately 40 years. From "An Expression," a work acquired by MoMA in 1985, to emotional dialogue AI, cultural computing, "Sound of Ikebana," and the first public showings of "Tosa Rinpa: Kiin Seido" and "Sound Vibration Screen: Fujin," this exhibition showcases a wide range of works.
