If you’ve ever passed through Tokyo’s Ueno Station, chances are you’ve walked beneath the work of a modern master without even realizing it.

Spanning the Central Gate concourse is “Freedom", a monumental public mural created by Gen’ichirō Inokuma in 1951. Commissioned during the postwar era, the piece was designed to bring optimism and human connection to millions of everyday commuters.

While already a household name in Japan, Inokuma moved to New York City in 1955, building deep connections across the mid-century art world that reached right to our doorstep. During his two decades in New York, he became an integral part of Japan Society’s story, even designing early logos for the organization.

Learn more about his time in mid-century Manhattan and the evolution of his art in Gen’ichirō Inokuma’s NYC Salon, opening October 6. Link in bio to get your tickets.

Inokuma’s mural Freedom (1951) at the Ueno JR Station Central Gate
Tokyo, Japan
