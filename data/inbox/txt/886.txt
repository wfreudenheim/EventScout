MoMI NEWS | October 21, 2026

Dear will,

We're thrilled to announce Kubrick, our complete film retrospective of the legendary director, presented by Criterion! The full schedule is online and opens Nov. 1 with the U.S. theatrical premiere of the International Release cut of The Shining, followed by a screening of the American version we all know and love. It will all be preceded by a conversation with special guests: Steadicam inventor Garrett Brown, archivist and producer of Criterion's The Complete Kubrick box set Michael Chaiken.

This week, see a 60th anniversary screening of Ingmar Bergman's masterpiece Persona, co-presented with NewFest; Handcrafted Horrors continues with three creature features: Universal's monster classic The Wolf Man. the humor-tinged horror classic An American Werewolf in London, and the truly terrifying The Exorcist. Plus, UAE horror film Baab makes its U.S. premiere in our Infinite Beauty series; and Richard Attenborough's sinister cult classic Magic, and the '80s comedy-horror freakout Vampire's Kiss, both in 35mm. Also, join filmmakers and special guests for a free screening of the new documentary Seat at the Table.

Open Worlds programming continues with a screening of rock music videos with director Nigel Dick (standby tickets may become available on the day of), and our annual Halloween party on Saturday evening! Coming up: celebrate a vibrant and authentic Day of the Dead on Nov. 1, and free yoga with The Yoga room returns on Nov. 8.

MoMI LAB, our co-creation space for technology and culture, presents workshops on making 3D models and using AI Agents, and Phone-Free Fridays continues through December. And, there are limited spots still available for Month Offline, a series of meetups for those who want to go phone free for 30 days.

Coming up: director Manuela Martelli in person with a sneak preview of Chilean Oscar submission The Meltdown, Tsai Ming-liang's brilliant Goodbye, Dragon Inn; and Handcrafted Horrors concludes with the sure-to-be cult vampire movie The Vourdalak, the original Poltergeist, and more. Don't forget, our expansive series The Minnellis kicks off in November with a lineup of unparalleled classics! 

Please note that due to a private event, the Museum will be closing at 5:00 p.m. on Thursday, October 22.

NOW SHOWING

Persona: 60th Anniversary
Friday, October 23, 6:15 p.m.

With this landmark experimental drama, which celebrates its 60th anniversary this year, Ingmar Bergman reached new heights of visual poetry. The Museum is pleased to co-present this masterpiece on the big screen with NewFest, New York’s largest queer film festival. 

Part of Special Screenings

Bram Stoker’s Dracula (35mm)
Friday, October 23, 8:15 p.m

For his rendition of the Dracula story, Coppola produced a quirky, idiosyncratic, and over-the-top cascade of lush and violent horror imagery that was, miraculously, a box-office smash, and even the notoriously horror-averse Academy Awards honored it three times. 

Part of Handcrafted Horrors

Vampire's Kiss (35mm)
Saturday, October 24, 12:30 p.m.

Robert Bierman's eighties comedy-horror freakout gave Nicolas Cage one of his most whacked-out roles, as a New York City yuppie literary agent who descends into cockroach-eating madness after believing he’s fallen for a sexy vampire (Jennifer Beals).

Part of New York Stories, free for MoMI Members

An American Werewolf in London
Saturday, October 24, 5:30 p.m

The rare humor-tinged horror movie that manages to be entirely terrifying and quite funny, John Landis’s film follows two American backpackers whose destinies are changed forever when they decide to stray from the roads while traveling across rural England. Features dazzling Oscar-winning makeup effects.

Part of Handcrafted Horrors

The Wolf Man
Sunday, October 25, 1:00 p.m.

In the classic original starring Lon Chaney Jr., happy homecomings and family reconciliations take a ghastly turn when Larry Talbot is attacked by and kills a wolf during an evening out, though only a man’s body is found after the tragedy.

Part of Handcrafted Horrors and Disreputable Cinema, free for MoMI Members

Baab
Sunday, October 25, 2:00 p.m

Haunted by the unceasing hum of tinnitus in her left ear and sudden death of her twin sister, Wahida (Shaimaa El Fadul) goes to the remote Ras Al-Khaimah mountains to find solace. Nayla al Khaja's Baab has never been invited to screen in the U.S.…until now! 

Part of Infinite Beauty: Muslim and MENASA Identity Onscreen, free for MoMI members

Magic (35mm)
Sunday, October 25, 3:00 p.m.

In Richard Attenborough's sinister cult classic, a brilliantly unbalanced Anthony Hopkins plays Charles “Corky” Withers, a failed magician turned ventriloquist who begins to show symptoms of psychosis—and allows his dummy, the foul-mouthed “Fats,” to take over his life.

Part of The Originals, free for MoMI members

Seat at the Table
Sunday, October 25, 5:30 p.m

Seat at the Table chronicles the rise of South Asian Americans in U.S. politics, tracing a remarkable journey from political disenfranchisement to an increasingly powerful presence in American civic life. The screening will be followed by a conversation with filmmakers and special guests. 

Free with RSVP

COMING SOON

Kubrick
November 1–December 26

Stanley Kubrick’s films are so magisterial, so monolithic, so meaningful to generations of moviegoers—and inseparable from a particular idea of cinema itself—that it’s also easy to overlook their idiosyncratic genius. MoMI is pleased to present the legendary director’s full cinematic corpus on the big screen, where his films all must be experienced! Presented by Criterion, the retrospective coincides with the release of Criterion’s The Complete Kubrick box set, an unprecedented collection that brings together Stanley Kubrick’s entire directorial output for the first time.

Goodbye, Dragon Inn
Friday, October 30, 6:00 p.m

Astonishingly beautiful, and filled with expertly timed sight gags, Goodbye, Dragon Inn is Taiwanese master Tsai Ming-liang's rueful backwards glance at the disappearance of the filmgoing culture of his youth, set entirely in one decaying movie theater. One of the seminal films of the 21st century. 

Part of Part of Seeing the Cinema: Theaters on Screen

Sneak Preview: The Meltdown (El deshielo)
With director Manuela Martelli
Friday, October 30, 7:00 p.m.

Set in 1992 against the backdrop of Chile’s post-Pinochet “economic miracle,” The Meltdown follows nine-year-old Inés during a stay at an Andean ski resort, where she befriends Hanna, a rebellious German teenage athlete. Chile's 2027 Oscar submission for Best International Feature.

AROUND TOWN

Our Century: Spiraling Paths Through Armenian Cinema
October 23–25 at Asia Society, New York

Centuries of displacement, the absence of statehood, the Armenian Genocide and the formation of one of the largest diasporas in the world have made movement an enduring part of Armenian life. See five films made by Armenian filmmakers from different parts of the world and across different periods that follow characters who are constantly on the move.
