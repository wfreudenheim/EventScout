HOW TO FUND A COMMUNITY LAB

By Casey Lardner • 20 Nov 2026 View in browser View in browser

 

 

Hey Friend,

Before I dive in, a quick heads-up that Genspace’s 2026 year-end campaign launches on Giving Tuesday, December 1.

This year, we're raising funds to make our classes more affordable than ever, and to welcome 1,000+ learners into our hands-on workshops in 2027. That's roughly double the number of learners we’ve been able to reach in past years. 

But first, I want to address a question I get all the time.

As Executive Director of Genspace, I'm often asked how the lab is funded. 

It's a great question. I think a better one is: what makes a community lab sustainable?

For me, the answer every single time is our amazing staff. We love to boast the title of "the world's first community biology lab," but another thing that sets Genspace apart is our team. We're seven full-time employees, the most of any independent community biology lab in the world (to the best of my knowledge!).

Members of the Genspace team over the past year

Paid staff make everything possible at Genspace.

 * A lab that’s safe, organized (ask us about our inventory system!), and functional for fifty+ different kinds of users at once.
 * Six different arms of programming, so that all of the people who are interested in doing science here have a point of entry.
 * Partnerships that are built on trust.
 * Public programs that make science tangible and relatable.
 * And so much more. 

These are the reasons why, every year since 2020, more than half of every dollar we spend has gone to the people who make Genspace run.

So, how do we pay for it? 

Genspace has grown a lot in the past few years. Since 2021, our annual budget has more than doubled, up from about $460,000 to just over $1M now. This is because, as our programming and audience have grown, we’ve naturally needed a bigger team, too. In 2025, about a third of our income came from classes and memberships, about a third from government grants, and another third from foundations, companies, and individual supporters (like you).

For every dollar we spent in 2025, about 70 cents went to people – our staff and the instructors who teach our classes. About 13 cents went to supplies and materials. The rest kept the lights on, going towards rent, insurance, and everything else it takes to operate a lab. 

The thing is, while our earned income (from class tickets and membership fees) has also doubled in recent years, this revenue still only covers about a third of what those programs cost to run. 

As we’ve grown, one move could have been to raise prices. But if everyone paid what a class costs to run, Genspace would mostly serve people who probably already have access to science. That's the opposite of why we exist. 

Instead, we recently lowered our class prices, and we've held membership prices steady for many years. 

This is where you come in. Grants tend to fund specific initiatives; our government funding, for example, mostly goes towards workforce training. It's much harder to find funders for the everyday heart of Genspace: hands-on classes & workshops, and a community lab where anyone can walk in and start experimenting.

Those functions are core to who we are, and it’s actually individual donors (like you) who keep them going.

Put another way, growth has given us a bigger budget, a bigger team, and room for more programming. Our donors are the ones who make sure that growth shows up as more learners at the bench, not higher prices. 

In 2027, we're taking this commitment even further.

First, every class will offer three ticket tiers:

 * a community price for those who need it,
 * a standard price that covers the cost of the class,
 * and a supporter price for those who can give a little more.

Your year-end gift is what will make that community tier possible.

We're also committing to creating new classes and curricula and aiming to offer 80+ classes, so 1,000+ learners can take a seat at the bench.

Keep an eye out for our launch email on December 1. And if you've ever been curious about how a place like this keeps its doors open, I hope this helps! Hit reply with any questions; I can talk about this stuff all day. 

With immense gratitude,

Casey Lardner, PhD

Executive Director, Genspace
