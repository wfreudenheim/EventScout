Dear readers, 

Between 2017 and 2019, Elizabeth Schambelan published three brilliant essays in n+1 about sexual violence, male bonding, and the relationship between hazing and torture. Mind-bendingly original and preternaturally perceptive, these texts surfaced ancient artifacts and recent history to shed light on the events then confusing and concerning the public—namely, the confirmation of Brett Kavanaugh to the Supreme Court and the debunked Rolling Stone story about a grisly rape at UVA.

Last week, as news of the Cornell rape case reverberated around the country, we decided to return to Schambelan’s work. Distressingly, the essays are evergreen. Take this line from “Special Journey to Our Bottom Line,” in which Schambelan observes that in frats, “libertinage is a group activity, and morality begins and ends with esprit de corps.” Or this passage from “Everybody Knows,” regarding a 1985 report commissioned by the Association of American Colleges called “Campus Gang Rape: Party Games?”:

> In every one of the frat-party rapes, “the scenario is basically the same”: an intoxicated young woman becomes the target of “the ‘friendly’ persuasion of the brothers,” unaware that their blandishments are “actually a planned pursuit of easy prey.” Once the attack gets under way, “her confusion has turned to fear and panic, and escape seems impossible. She is unable to protest or her protests are ignored.” . . . Fraternity members viewed pulling train as “‘normal’ party behavior. In fact, in almost all instances the men involved are unaware that their behavior is gang rape.” Some frats even mentioned the activity on party invitations.

Party invitations, group texts. . . times may have changed, but they haven’t changed enough. Readers who missed “League of Men,” “Everybody Knows,” and “Special Journey to Our Bottom Line” the first time around will find much in them to chew on. So will readers encountering Schambelan's essays again with the distance of several years. You can find them all unpaywalled on our website. 

Yours,
The Editors
