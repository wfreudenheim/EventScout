CAMERA MINISTRY: LENS-BASED ENCOUNTERS

----------------------------------------

Spend three days with photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah exploring the art of seeing, connecting, and making images.

Nov 13 — Nov 15 

This weekend! Only a couple of spots remain, grab yours today.

What happens when the camera becomes a way of getting closer to the world, rather than simply recording it? Join acclaimed photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah for a three-day intensive exploring the methods, instincts, and encounters that shape his extraordinary lens-based practice. From street photography and portraiture to poetic documentary, Khalik will share his approach to working with people, building trust, and following intuition. Through screenings, conversations, and hands-on exploration, participants will discover new possibilities for their own image-making, with the opportunity to share their work and receive direct creative feedback from Khalik.

 

Khalik Allah
Workshop Lead

Joining the workshop are two exceptional guest artists: filmmaker and cinematographer Shevaun Mizrahi, who will explore the interplay of magical realism and vérité in her filmmaking, and legendary photographer and archivist Jamil GS, whose portraits of Jay-Z, Nas, D’Angelo, Mary J. Blige, and A Tribe Called Quest helped define the visual culture of hip-hop in the 1990s. Jamil will reflect on photography as a form of cultural memory. Documentary cinematographer Derek Howard will walk us through the ethical questions confronting him regularly filming in the field.

FIELD NIGGAS

----------------------------------------

Shot entirely at night on the corner of 125th Street and Lexington Avenue in Harlem, the film draws us into a world shaped by proximity, presence, and the rhythms of the street.

Thursday, November 12 — 7:30PM

Tomorrow! Join us for Khalik Allah’s extraordinary feature Field Niggas, kicking off this weekend's workshop Camera Ministry: Lens-based Encounters! Through striking portraits and an intricate, non-synchronous soundscape of dreams, regrets, arguments, and everyday observations, Allah creates an immersive portrait of a place and its people. His camera moves with uncommon intimacy, dissolving the distance between filmmaker and subject. His approach transforms the act of looking into a practice of care, trust, and connection. Come experience a powerful introduction to Allah’s singular approach to image-making. Khalik will be in conversation with cultural organizer, curator and writer Kazembe Balagun. Come through!

UNDO SPOTLIGHT: A SHOWCASE OF SUPPORTED PROJECTS

----------------------------------------

Two nights celebrating bold new work from artists supported by UnionDocs’ Fiscal Sponsorship program.

Thursday, November 19 & Saturday, November 21 — 7:30PM

Come through for two special evenings celebrating the artists supported by our Fiscal Sponsorship program! Across both nights, this talented group of filmmakers and artists will be sharing their works in progress, spanning documentary, audio storytelling, experimental cinema, and beyond. These projects traverse remarkable terrain, from the fragile myths of American masculinity and the lives of queer men exiled under Italian Fascism to the hidden histories of labor, migration, and political resistance. They explore the politics of surveillance and technology, the shifting landscapes of cities and communities, and intimate questions of memory, identity, family, and belonging. Both evenings are FREE and feature the artists in attendance, so come through, discover some exciting new projects, and raise a glass to the brilliant minds dreaming them up!

All presenting artists are recipients of a 2026 Support for Artists grant, administered through UnionDocs’ Fiscal Sponsorship program and made possible by the New York State Council on the Arts with support from the Office of the Governor and the New York State Legislature.

Practice Thing with Brian Becker

Brian Becker's documentary Mall Country, fiscally sponsored by UnionDocs, is screening at DOC NYC tomorrow! Following his acclaimed archival documentary Time Bomb Y2K, co-directed with Marley McDonald, Becker turns his attention to the American shopping mall in a new feature exploring its place in the country’s cultural imagination. As he gears up to share the film with audiences, Brian invites us into his home office to share a few of the tips, tricks, and rituals that help him get his projects across the finish line. Listen in for some wisdom on navigating the pressures of filmmaking, and catch Mall Country at DOC NYC tomorrow, Thursday, November 12 and Sunday, November 15! Screening Details & Tickets.
