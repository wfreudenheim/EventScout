Dear readers,

When Annie Dillard passed away last month at the age of 81, Grace Glass penned a eulogy of Dillard for our website titled “The Freedom of the Weasel.” Glass wrote:

> Annie Dillard was not a materialist—probably because her sentences were transcendent. “Fine prose in this sense is like Shakespeare’s dramatic poetry, or Milton’s epic poetry, or even Homer’s. If you scratch an event, you get an idea.” How insipid, I think sometimes, wanting to shake her out of her solipsism, harangue her with a rant about the metabolic rift. Nothing could be more important, I think later, when I can’t sleep, after five continuous days on the computer, than learning how to notice, really notice, a fern.

Glass’s essay—by turns reverent and irreverent, clear-eyed but conflicted—reflects what we observed in 2023: that the deaths of artists and thinkers has inspired some of the best critical writing in the n+1 archives, writing that is “beautiful, complex, and written from a place of great familiarity and investment.”

Below, we’ve gathered some of our favorite obituaries we’ve published over the years. In addition to Glass’s piece, you’ll find:

 * Amelia Soth on Carlo Ginzburg 
 * Nate Wooley on Sonny Rollins
 * Elias Rodriques and Omari Weekes on Toni Morrison
 * Carlos Valladares on Joan Micklin Silver
 * Charlotte Shane on Brian Wilson
 * Gabriel Winant on Barbara Ehrenreich

Yours,
The Editors
