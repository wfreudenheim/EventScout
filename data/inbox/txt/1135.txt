The Encounters Series: “Ibero-American Patterns”

Today at 12:15pm
East Gallery, Buell Hall

Fernando Luis Martínez Nespral (IAA) presents “Ibero-American Patterns,” followed by a response from Faculty Ziad Jamaleddine.

Orientalizing patterns, building elements, and tropes have suffused Latin American architecture since the Early Modern period. Fernando Nespral’s comprehensive new study shows that these designs were primarily channeled through Spain, demonstrating how deeply this longstanding imperial legacy is imprinted on the hemisphere.

CONSTRUCTING (unorthodox) PRACTICE

November 13, 10am
Avery 114

CONSTRUCTING (unorthodox) PRACTICE traces the trajectories of emerging firms from around the globe that are critically engaged with contemporary design challenges.

The symposium focuses on the transition from education to professional practice and on the idea that one’s own practice can be understood as a project in itself—designed, built, and sustained—necessarily shaped by meaningful commitments in today’s contradictory and increasingly complex climate.

Speakers include Mahsa Malek and Alex Yueyan Li (11x17), Laurens Bekemans (BC Architects), Anna MacIver-Ek and Axel Chevroulet (Maciver-Ek Chevroulet), Martinez Barat, Benjamin Lafore, and Florian Jomain (MBL Architects), Ardy Hartono (DUA Studio), Gigi Shukakidze (Wunderwerk), Dechophon Rattanasatchatham, Pongsatorn Swadchatchwan and Rungroj Tansukanun (Yangnar Studio), Nguyễn Hà (ARB Architects + Material Matters), Juliana Ayako, Carlos Zebulun, Anna Jankovic and André Bonnice (SIMULAA), Alkistis Thomidou and Berta Gutiérrez Casaos (Forty-Five Degrees), Igor Bragado and Miles Gertler (Common Accounts). Introductions by Dean Andrés Jaque and Faculty Juan Herreros, moderation by Faculty Marc Tsurumaki, Mireia Luzárraga, Lola Ben-Alon, and Director of MSAAD Lydia Kallipoliti.

In Memoriam: Victoria Sanger

Columbia GSAPP is deeply saddened by the passing of Adjunct Assistant Professor and architectural historian Victoria Sanger, whose teaching and generous intellectual presence enriched the School for over two decades, first through the New York/Paris Program and later at Avery Hall.

As a specialist in early modern architectural and urban history, with a Ph.D. from Columbia University, Sanger’s work explored the ways warfare and cartography shape the development and representation of cities. Her research focused particularly on seventeenth-century France and the work of military engineer Sébastien de Vauban. Through her teaching, she brought these historical entanglements into dialogue with questions of development and representation of cities.

She was deeply committed to preserving architectural history, notably through her work to bring the papers of her great-uncle and grandfather, Percy and Harold D. Uris, to Columbia’s Avery Architectural & Fine Arts Library, and, more recently, championing Uris Hall’s history reflecting the intersection of architecture, computing, and urban development in New York City. She also supported the acquisition of materials from Emery Roth & Sons, helping preserve important records of New York City’s architectural history.

Columbia GSAPP honors Victoria Sanger’s legacy.

In Memoriam: Paul Segal

Columbia GSAPP is deeply saddened by the passing of architect and Adjunct Professor Paul Segal, who taught Professional Practice at the School for over three decades. Through his teaching and mentorship, Segal taught generations of students how designs become buildings.

Segal earned his M.F.A. from Princeton University in 1969 and founded Paul Segal Associates Architects. His contributions extended beyond architectural practice into the civic life of New York, where he helped preserve historic Broadway theaters and served as president of AIA New York. He shared his knowledge of the profession through his book, Professional Practice: A Guide to Turning Designs into Buildings (2006).

Columbia GSAPP honors Paul Segal’s legacy.
