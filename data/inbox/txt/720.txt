EXHIBITION HIGHLIGHT

Seeing the Cinema: Photographic Visions of Movie Theaters
Opens Saturday, October 10 in the Amphitheater Gallery

Featuring photographs by Thomas R. Schiff, Hiroshi Sugimoto, John Margolies, and Joseph Holmes, this exhibition explores the movie theater as both architectural icon and cultural form through a range of photographic practices, offering a powerful meditation on what it has meant—and continues to mean—to gather in the dark to watch stories unfold on a screen.

Related: the new ongoing screening series Seeing the Cinema: Theaters on Screen begins Oct. 30 with Tsai Ming-liang's Goodbye, Dragon Inn!

COMING SOON

Greek Film Expo 2026: Michael Cacoyannis Retrospective
October 16–18

Still best known for his 1964 international, Oscar-winning breakthrough Zorba the Greek, director Michael Cacoyannis was a director of versatility and depth whose humane, funny, sometimes tragic films are rarely screened in the U.S. This year’s Greek Film Expo, organized by the Hellenic Film Society, presents a special retrospective of four films by this legendary figure of the cinema, theater, and opera, including, of course, Zorba the Greek, alongside three of his earlier breakthrough films of the fifties.

Fantasia
Saturday, October 17, 1:00 p.m.
Sunday, October 18, 12:30 p.m.

Though it baffled many in its day, Walt Disney's Fantasia went on to be one of the most beloved, influential films of all time. Set to works by Bach, Tchaikovsky, Beethoven, Stravinsky, and more, the wordless, exquisitely hand-drawn segments that make up this ambitious masterpiece constitute a high point in animation.

Part of Handcrafted Horrors and World of Animation, free for MoMI Members

Faust
Saturday, October 17, 3:30 p.m

Having already helped revolutionize the art of cinema with the German expressionist masterworks Nosferatu and The Last Laugh, F. W. Murnau created the equally astonishing supernatural Faust, adapting the classic German legend of an alchemist who makes a pact with the devil for eternal youth. New restoration of the original German version of the film! 

Part of Handcrafted Horrors and Silents, Please!, free for MoMI Members

REVERSE SHOT AND SLOAN SCIENCE & FILM

Reverse Shot Review:
Once Upon a Time in Harlem

Making its New York premiere at the 2026 New York Film Festival, William Greaves and David Greaves' Once Upon a Time in Harlem "is alive with historical figures who have long passed on," writes A. G. Sims. The film is an amazing documentary that took more than fifty years to finish, shot in 1972 and bringing together luminaries of the Harlem Renaissance.

Read the review here.

Sloan Science and Film Interview:
Virginia Woolf's Night and Day

Virginia Woolf's Night and Day is an adaption set in turn-of-the-century London starring Haley Bennett as a female astronomer desperate to make her mark. We spoke with the film’s writer and producer Justine Waddell about the process of adapting the book, stories she drew inspiration from, and the science in the film.
