SPEAKING WITHOUT TONGUES
WITH COOPER JACOBY, CHRISTOPHER KULENDRAN THOMAS & MAYA MAN

November 14, 2026
Doors at 12:30 p.m., event at 1 p.m.
Roulette
509 Atlantic Ave., Brooklyn, NY 11217
Free with RSVP

Over the past few years, AI voices have become more and more prominent as narrators of interfaces, news, disinformation, and entertainment. Across characters and contexts, the artists Cooper Jacoby, Maya Man, and Christopher Kulendran Thomas have each trained artificial voices and deployed them as mouthpieces for cultural critique. 

For Speaking Without Tongues, the three artists will discuss the practice and implications of using generated voices to mimic, memorialize, or manipulate human speech, and what the resulting work tells us about loss and desire, power and performance, labor and celebrity. Their conversation will be moderated by Mira Dayal, Triple Canopy’s senior editor.

TECHNOMISOGINARY
WITH SOPHIE LEWIS & KALINDI VORA

November 14, 2026
3 p.m.
Roulette
509 Atlantic Ave., Brooklyn, NY 11217
Free with RSVP

Oligarchs at the helm of tech empires prophesize a coming apocalypse, which they consciously accelerate, and then preach the necessity of preserving digitized human consciousness at the expense of actual human lives. Meanwhile, their dystopian ideals structure supply chains, corporate policy, digital platforms, and our so-called smart devices.

For Technomisoginary, the cultural theorists Sophie Lewis and Kalindi Vora will discuss the ways in which misogyny and colonial ideology shape contemporary technologies, and how we might combat tech leaders’ annihilatory ethos. Can our collective aspirations extend beyond the horizon of proper compensation and include, as Lewis has written, “living big, dying well, and making things look and feel like love”? Their conversation will be moderated by Rachel Ossip, Triple Canopy’s deputy editor.

BETS IN A BURNING HOUSE
WITH DAVID HILL, AYESHA A. SIDDIQI & JACOB SILVERMAN

November 14, 2026
5 p.m.
Roulette
509 Atlantic Ave., Brooklyn, NY 11217
Free with RSVP

From Kalshi to Polymarket, prediction markets have pitched themselves as forces of altruism and democratization, allowing users control and agency in the face of our current polycrisis. These platforms have been trumpeted as Silicon Valley and Wall Street’s latest innovation, with Andreessen Horowitz, the Bay Area–based venture capital firm, going as far to declare prediction “the successor to postmodernism.” 

For Bets in a Burning House, writers David Hill, Ayesha A. Siddiqi, and Jacob Silverman—who have all tracked the rise of prediction markets amid political uncertainty and economic instability—will address historical precedents as well as contemporary policies and politics. As prediction markets push to “financialize everything,” they will ask: Who profits from speculating on—or manipulating—the future, and at what cost? Their conversation will be moderated by Ha Duong, Triple Canopy’s editorial coordinator.
