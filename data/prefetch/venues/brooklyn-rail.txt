ARNO BRANDLHUBER WITH NILE GREENBERG [/2026/11/architecture/arno-brandlhuber-with-nile-greenberg/]

Arno Brandlhuber’s cooperative practice b+, based in Berlin, is a degree zero of contemporary architecture. Their work often reduces a building to the forces which it’s most affected by—not function or structure, but rather regulation, financialization, and the management of a climate. In this interview, Arno gives consideration to the biggest questions of our moment.

ROY LICHTENSTEIN: LIKE NEW [/2026/11/artseen/roy-lichtenstein-like-new/]

Organized as a series of thematic presentations rather than a comprehensive retrospective, Like New takes an anthropological approach, bringing together source materials and works by other artists, reconstructing Roy Lichtenstein’s 1962 Castelli Gallery debut, and recreating four murals that have been repurposed to serve as backgrounds for related works.

TARYN SIMON WITH JEAN DYKSTRA [/2026/11/in-conversation/taryn-simon-with-jean-dykstra/]

Taryn Simon’s expansive exhibition, Father Country I Do Love You, on view at the Guggenheim through March 2027, begins and ends with her father, whose presence shadows the show’s forty-two “chapters.” The main character, though, is an unnamed FBI agent Simon met a decade ago. 

/2026/11/in-conversation/sable-elyse-smith-with-amber-jamilla-musser/

SABLE ELYSE SMITH WITH AMBER JAMILLA MUSSER [/2026/11/in-conversation/sable-elyse-smith-with-amber-jamilla-musser/]

In May 2024, Sable Elyse Smith was awarded the Suzanne Deal Booth / FLAG Art Foundation Prize. It is a prestigious honor, and with it comes the opportunity of creating exhibitions in Austin, Texas and New York. This year Smith launched both exhibitions.

/2026/11/in-conversation/bosco-sodi-with-ginevra-de-blasio/

BOSCO SODI WITH GINEVRA DE BLASIO [/2026/11/in-conversation/bosco-sodi-with-ginevra-de-blasio/]

In Bosco Sodi’s new exhibition, Verdigris, he returns to oxidation after encountering Andy Warhol’s “Oxidation Paintings.” Sodi has developed a body of work centered on the green-blue patina that forms as copper reacts with its environment. 

/2026/11/in-conversation/susan-jane-walp-with-dian-parker/

SUSAN JANE WALP WITH DIAN PARKER [/2026/11/in-conversation/susan-jane-walp-with-dian-parker/]

A still life painter living in rural Vermont, Susan Jane Walp creates works which are worlds of quiet isolation amongst a community of objects, all interacting and yet still. Being with Susan is like being inside one of her paintings: she considers everything she says and doesn’t rush her thoughts or ideas.

NANCY OLNICK & GIORGIO SPANU WITH JENNIFER STOCKMAN & JOACHIM PISSARRO [/2026/11/art/nancy-olnick-and-giorgio-spanu-with-jennifer-stockman-and-joachim-pissarro/]

Magazzino Italian Art, in Cold Spring, New York, is a museum and research center devoted to postwar and contemporary Italian art, above all Arte Povera. It grew out of the collection of Nancy Olnick and Giorgio Spanu, who also collect Murano glass, Italian ceramics, artist-made jewelry, and Art Deco furniture, and who opened Magazzino in 2017 in the former Cyberchron factory and warehouse.

FIVE POEMS [/2026/11/poetry/five-poems/]

By Killarney Clary [/search/?q=%22Killarney%20Clary%22&c=6adb8521-d25a-457b-99ee-ed520fc20515]

The girl  thinks she’s fat and waits for bedtime when it will be too late for her to make
it  better  or  worse. Shouldn’t  it  be dark by  now? She  worried  about  free  dress  on
the field trip to the  dairy; she  fell on the  waxed gymnasium  floor  and  sprained  her
hand,  but  what she wanted to  know  was if  anyone  saw  her  underwear. Tomorrow
will  be blank  at the  start, then  each minute a question of how much. Stop, she says,
the day before she dies. Stop watching.

****

After saying  the  hurtful  words, she  lifts the  cold cup from  the well to her  lips  and
rattles the reflection of the canopy; she  says  she didn’t mean it that  way, she  wasn’t
thinking. I  don’t think thinking  means meaning. In  the  ordinary line  of  time, once
a  thing’s   been  put  out  and  taken  in, there’s  no way  to  return; all  the  explaining
in the world  will  not  matter. Matter  has  nothing  to  do  with  how  we are laughing.

****

That  blue  coat  I  left  in  your  car  after  we’d  argued  and  I’d  walked—I didn’t want
to  lose  it  so I  came by  to  pick it up; your  wife  let me in  and she and I talked in the
kitchen.  That was  our way, one  which  allowed  you  to  stay  in  the  bedroom  but to
open the door only enough to let your  canary fly out  and  land on my  shoulder. Your
apology.  Now it isn’t just your  absence  that makes me cry; it’s that  you’re  no longer
here to keep me from telling you I’m sad.

****

I take a  bath  while  the  sun  goes  down, eat  in  twilight  in my clean skin in  my  soft 
clothes  while  the  few petals  on the  Icelandic  poppy  drop to  the  table—a  child  in
her day which ends before dark.

Was dinner to be set down in front of me forever?

A sweet  smell of grain  and potatoes  lingers on the towel.  A sticky  finish of  my own
sweat  builds  up on  all  handles, leaves traces of  myself and  of another  who  is away
for awhile, will be back soon.

When  I  am  through  with  my  rehearsal  life,  I  will grow  my hair  long, I will watch
the dying until they die.

I  savor  the heat,  the  tick of  the  wooden  house,  the  photographed  faces  beaming
despite what I tell them.

****

I like to  think  of  her  as  she  opens  the  box  and  folds  back  the  tissue,  smells  the
laser-burned  pieces,   dumps  them  out  on  the  cool  table  and  turns them pattern-
side  up,   pulls  aside any  with a straight  edge  as  she  thinks  of  me,  how we  might
have fit this together under the yellow lamp, talking a little.

I  send  her  want—for a  time  on a  summer afternoon when we  listened to the radio
and  dreamt  of  TV-star  romances as we  played  a  board  game, taken  care  of.  She
is  too  far  away  for me to  hand her  the red  fragment shaped  like a  bear. I give her
what we won’t have again.

MUSEUM DIORAMA OF A SECRET GARDEN [/2026/11/poetry/museum-diorama-of-a-secret-garden/]

By Cynthia Clifford [/search/?q=%22Cynthia%20Clifford%22&c=f57d421c-22dc-46d1-ba14-b836a2a96a8f]

Long live everything.    Molar of the mouth and the nail that drove 
through each soulful creature. Even the bullfrogs under the rose

bush should keep on being speckled and diseased.
No one touches their rubber bellies, their tiny humps for eyes. 

They only stare towards the lakebed, around appearance
     of must. All of them get to stay in this glass nature box.

Gift of—the placard says, then a name etched in stone.
             Whoever made this felt like a god, another ecosystem 

drawn up and zipped tight. These figurine people inside
             must resemble someone once alive and important.

             The cherry-haired one, leaning against a white oak
has a wheat straw falling halfway out of her parted lips.

I know she wants to speak to me about the creaky screen door
and the four children she raised alone. Those Sunday nights alone.

How long these lives of ours are.             Do we always look hungry
from the outside?             I assign names to the couple stuck in a hug. 

I won’t say more about them. You know, there is a box coming
for me too.             I will try to get your widow’s peak right and string

your belt loops to the center column, so you can lick your fingers
     so you can stretch each morning out however you’d like.

I want to copy what I see here and wear it out until the wings
           are smooth as déjà vu. Up in the trees there is an idea, us

being up to some good again. Right foot. Right foot. Shoelace tip
           left and lost in the mist. Your filthy blue starboots stay.

For Bradley Andrews

[/2026/11/poetry/museum-diorama-of-a-secret-garden/]

TWO POEMS [/2026/11/poetry/two-poems-harris/]

By Mark Harris [/search/?q=%22Mark%20Harris%22&c=6c0381f2-e5b7-4078-bb62-1fa87b8477b0]

Nor

Having a tête-à-tête with your double in reverse is complicated
when both yous experience trauma. The surface starts to melt into
a sort of mist. The other you doesn’t always see eye to eye and
could be tired from work, dressing or undressing, practicing French,
reading a good book you also happen to be reading, or making love
with themselves in the way anyone might do. My own dissolution
happened slowly. Over time, I came to lose all confidence in myself.
Did one of us move in first, and then the other? I don’t remember.
Not that we two are alike aside from finishing each other’s stories,
sharing old humiliations, nightmares, the terrible torment of being
alone and crying, wanting out. What belongs to any of us, really?
Friends tell me I need to pull myself together. I’d rather die. Which
of us will go first is impossible to imagine. The phantom pleasure,
forget about the pain, would be utter agony to live without. Mind
deceives and what is desired, in a flash, inverts. Love, or the union
people call by that word, like quicksilver switches over to the other.

[/2026/11/poetry/two-poems-harris/]

COGNODITY [/2026/11/poetry/cognodity/]

By Matt Fallon [/search/?q=%22Matt%20Fallon%22&c=34705735-c21e-4f11-8280-64575ddb96d3]

harvest every sentence. pocket each image. deploy
Chicago’s ebon finger. scour the chart for grading
diamonds. uncover sepia. memorize every flaw.

all my prodded-up plums

flickering tweet-tweet & harpsichord. birdsong.
sounds like Tolkien. like Elwynn. sounds like
abandoned on the riviera. birthing Montesquieu.

windowless mornings

the regional rail. numbers & colors. sixteen stops.
eat the math. mouth the maps. tell everybody
it’s all logic in the aluminum shoebox with gills.

a monitor’s chartreuse glow

watch it flutter by. shape of a whale-bound arrow.
watch a family dive. then tame it. you are the eyeball.
the cubicle of God.
