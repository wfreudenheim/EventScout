PARSONS VISITING ARTIST LECTURE

JOYCE JOUMAA: PROVISIONAL RECOVERY

WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 28, 2026
7–8:30 PM EDT 
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER, THE NEW SCHOOL
63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL, NEW YORK

For the Parsons Fine Arts Visiting Artist Lecture, co-sponsored by the Vera List Center for Art and Politics, 2025–2027 VLC Fellow Joyce Joumaa shares recent and upcoming projects, including her VLC Fellowship project, Calibrated Alien. Joumaa's practice begins from a simple but radical premise: that collapse is not an event to be witnessed but a condition to be inhabited, and that the image, once it ceases to document a crisis with a beginning and an end, becomes a technology for living inside one instead. What emerges from her work is less a documentary than a meditation on the impossibility of documentation itself, on the paradox of bearing witness to something that refuses, structurally, to conclude.

STUDY GROUP

BLACK FEMINIST STUDY GROUP IV

SATURDAY, OCTOBER 31, 2–3:30 PM EDT
ARNOLD AND SHEILA ARONSON GALLERIES
SHEILA C. JOHNSON DESIGN CENTER, PARSONS SCHOOL OF DESIGN
THE NEW SCHOOL, 66 FIFTH AVENUE

In-person only

The Black Feminist Study Group is a four-part program shaped by the poetics and practice of Rosana Paulino and assembled within her prize exhibition Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?. The weekly series is anchored by seminal texts that touch on interconnected struggles for liberation and resistance throughout the African diaspora. OlaRonke Akinmowo, interdisciplinary artist and founder of The Free Black Women’s Library, joins us for the final session of the study group, with a reading and discussion of Saidiya Hartman’s Wayward Lives, Beautiful Experiments: Intimate Histories of Riotous Black Girls, Troublesome Women, and Queer Radicals. Hosted by the VLC's Re'al Christian.

BOOK LAUNCH & TALK

EYAL WEIZMAN: UNGROUNDING

THURSDAY, OCTOBER 29, 2026
6:30–8 PM EDT
WOLLMAN HALL, THE NEW SCHOOL
65 WEST 11TH STREET, 5TH FLOOR, NEW YORK

Join Eyal Weizman, founder and director of Forensic Architecture, for a lecture and conversation with VLC Senior Director and Chief Curator Carin Kuoni to mark the launch of Ungrounding, his groundbreaking investigation into the relationship between violence, territory, and the built environment in Gaza and Palestine. Drawing on decades of forensic research, Weizman traces how architecture, infrastructure, and militarized landscapes have shaped histories of displacement and violence, offering a critical framework for understanding the present. Seating is limited and will be on a first come first served basis.

PRIZE EXHIBITION

ROSANA PAULINO: ¿HISTÓRIA NATURAL?

OCTOBER 9–NOVEMBER 1, 2026
OPEN DAILY 12–6 PM EDT, THURSDAYS LATE UNTIL 8 PM
ARNOLD AND SHEILA ARONSON GALLERIES
SHEILA C. JOHNSON DESIGN CENTER, PARSONS SCHOOL OF DESIGN
THE NEW SCHOOL, 66 FIFTH AVENUE

Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural? features the work of Brazilian artist and Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice recipient Rosana Paulino. Anchored by her landmark artist book of the same name, the exhibition traces Paulino’s critical examinations of the entanglements between science, race, gender, knowledge, and power that played a central role in shaping the national project envisioned by Brazilian elites in the early 19th century. Featuring a range of new, recent, and rarely seen works, the exhibition showcases the poetic and conceptual breadth of her practice and its significance across Brazil, the African diaspora, and other international contexts.

Guided by Evans’s proposition that “Life is dance, dance is death,” the performance questions how bodies can and cannot be isolated from social, economic, and political urgencies.

Tomorrow, October 27, New School faculty members David Bering-Porter, who participated in the VLC Forum 2025: Matter of Intelligence, and Joshua Scannell join each other in a joint book launch at the university's Hoerle Lecture Hall. Offering novel treatments of the media, technological, and cultural infrastructures of contemporary racial capitalism, Scannell's The Carceral Surround and Bering-Porter's Undead Labor provide important theoretical frameworks for understanding how we have arrived at the current moment of polycrisis. 

And finally, VLC Forum 2026 participant Robbie Wing and Correction* Seminar 6 participant Jeremy Dennis are included in the current group exhibition Placing Water at Scenic Hudson's River Center in Beacon, NY (on view through November 22). The show examines embodied relationships to imperiled waterways, engaging with place-based methods of understanding entanglements between colonial history, industry, and ecology.
