Hassan Aliyu and Ade Ogundimu explore the enduring afterlives of empire in major exhibition.

Gallery Oldham presents Restless Objects: Migration, Memory and Survival in the Afterlives of Empire, a major exhibition of collaged paintings, multimedia works and sculptural installations by British-Nigerian artists Dr Hassan Aliyu and Dr Ade Ogundimu. Curated by Professor Paul Goodwin, the exhibition opens on Saturday December 5, 2026 and runs until February 27, 2027.

Drawing on more than 30 years of artistic collaboration, extensive doctoral research and personal experiences of migration between the UK and Nigeria, the exhibition examines the enduring impact of colonialism and reclaims histories obscured by empire.

Ogundimu’s work recovers indigenous West African writing and knowledge systems long dismissed by colonial administrations. His sculptural installations use charred wood, ceramics, vessels and other materials to evoke myths, memories and histories that persist through migration.

Aliyu’s collaged paintings and multimedia works make the abstractions of global finance visible, tracing connections between the auction blocks of the transatlantic slave trade and contemporary stock exchanges. Incorporating recycled consumer packaging, financial data and commercial print materials, his work considers how systems of extraction, debt and inequality continue to shape African nations and patterns of migration.

Oldham provides a resonant setting for the exhibition. Once known as “Spindleopolis”, the town was a global centre of cotton spinning, closely linked to Manchester’s “Cottonopolis” and to wider histories of empire, trade and migration.

For both artists, memory is material as well as historical. Their use of collage, print and reclaimed objects developed in part from the restrictions on imported art materials during their studies in Nigeria in the 1980s, transforming economic hardship into an enduring artistic method. Together, their works invite audiences to confront the structures inherited from empire and consider possibilities for survival and renewal.

The exhibition is accompanied by a catalogue and a wider public programme in Oldham, London and online. The launch on Saturday December 5 will include a talk by both artists exploring the exhibition’s themes.

Artists and curator bios
Hassan Aliyu
Dr Hassan Aliyu, DProf, FRSA is a British-Nigerian artist and curator whose multidisciplinary practice and postdoctoral research explore slavery, colonialism, global capitalism and their continuing impact on migration and inequality. Working across painting, collage, installation and moving image, he incorporates financial data, consumer packaging and found materials. He studied Fine Art at Ahmadu Bello University and completed a Doctorate in Fine Art at the University of East London.

He is President of the Nigeria Art Society UK, a Director and Trustee of 198 Contemporary Arts & Learning, and a member of the Guild of Professional Fine Artists of Nigeria. Recent presentations include the Havana Biennial, Epic Journeys in Cambridge, Legacies of Biafra in Bayreuth and a screening of A Dance of Wealth and Strife in Addis Ababa.

Ade Ogundimu
Dr Ade Ogundimu, DProf is a British-Nigerian painter and sculptor whose practice addresses postcolonial legacies, national identity, human rights and cultural heritage. His installations use charred wood, terracotta, canoes and vessels to connect contemporary migration to Britain with longer histories of movement between Africa and the Caribbean.

He began his training at Yaba College of Technology in Lagos, studied at the University of the Arts London and the University of Oxford, and completed a Professional Doctorate in Fine Art at the University of East London. He has exhibited in the UK, USA, Nigeria and India, including in the touring exhibition Legacies of Biafra.

Paul Goodwin
Professor Paul Goodwin is a London-based curator, researcher and educator whose work focuses on transnational art, urbanism, African diaspora art and visual cultures. At Tate Britain (2007–2012), he directed the Tate Cross Cultural Programme. He is Professor of Contemporary Art and Director of the Research Centre for Transnational Art, Identity & Nation (TrAIN) at University of the Arts London.
