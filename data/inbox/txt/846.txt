MATTER OF INTELLIGENCE SEMINAR

SEMINAR 7: INTELLIGENCE, UNCERTAIN

MONDAY, OCTOBER 19, 2026
2–3:30 PM EDT 
ONLINE

If intelligence has always been a contested site—attributed, withheld, performed, disputed—misinformation and disinformation reveal the political stakes of that contest in their sharpest form. Presented in partnership with Haus der Kulturen der Welt (HKW), Berlin, this seminar explores uncertainty in the context of contemporary cultural, political, and technological transformations. HKW Fellows Nora Al-Badri and manuel arturo abreu and neuroscientist and writer Suparna Choudhury consider how artistic practice might engage these conditions by holding complexity, sustaining uncertainty without surrendering to it, and refusing false clarity without collapsing into relativism. Hosted by the VLC's Eriola Pira and moderated by HKW's Sara Morais dos Santos Bruss.

The Black Feminist Study Group is a four-part program shaped by the poetics and practice of Rosana Paulino and assembled within her prize exhibition Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?. The weekly series is anchored by seminal texts that touch on interconnected struggles for liberation and resistance throughout the African diaspora. Scholar Cresa Pugh, Assistant Professor of Sociology at The New School, joins us for session III of the study group, with a reading from Denise Ferreira da Silva’s Unpayable Debt. Hosted by the VLC's Re'al Christian. Visit our website for the full series lineup.

PARSONS VISITING ARTIST LECTURE

JOYCE JOUMAA: PROVISIONAL RECOVERY

WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 28, 2026
7–8:30 PM EDT 
STARR FOUNDATION HALL, UNIVERSITY CENTER, THE NEW SCHOOL
63 FIFTH AVENUE, LOWER LEVEL, NEW YORK

For the Parsons Fine Arts Visiting Artist Lecture, co-sponsored by the Vera List Center for Art and Politics, 2025–2027 VLC Fellow Joyce Joumaa shares recent and upcoming projects, including her VLC Fellowship project, Calibrated Alien. Joumaa's practice begins from a simple but radical premise: that collapse is not an event to be witnessed but a condition to be inhabited, and that the image, once it ceases to document a crisis with a beginning and an end, becomes a technology for living inside one instead. What emerges from her work is less a documentary than a meditation on the impossibility of documentation itself, on the paradox of bearing witness to something that refuses, structurally, to conclude.

LECTURE & BOOK LAUNCH

EYAL WEIZMAN: UNGROUNDING

THURSDAY, OCTOBER 29, 2026
6:30–8 PM EDT
WOLLMAN HALL, THE NEW SCHOOL
65 WEST 11TH STREET, 5TH FLOOR, NEW YORK

Join Eyal Weizman, founder and director of Forensic Architecture, for a lecture and conversation with VLC Senior Director and Chief Curator Carin Kuoni to mark the launch of Ungrounding, his groundbreaking investigation into the relationship between violence, territory, and the built environment in Gaza and Palestine. Drawing on decades of forensic research, Weizman traces how architecture, infrastructure, and militarized landscapes have shaped histories of displacement and violence, offering a critical framework for understanding the present.

PRIZE EXHIBITION

ROSANA PAULINO: ¿HISTÓRIA NATURAL?

OCTOBER 9–NOVEMBER 1, 2026
OPEN DAILY 12–6 PM EDT, THURSDAYS LATE UNTIL 8 PM
ARNOLD AND SHEILA ARONSON GALLERIES
SHEILA C. JOHNSON DESIGN CENTER, PARSONS SCHOOL OF DESIGN
THE NEW SCHOOL, 66 FIFTH AVENUE

Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural? features the work of Brazilian artist and Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice recipient Rosana Paulino. Anchored by her landmark artist book of the same name, the exhibition traces Paulino’s critical examinations of the entanglements between science, race, gender, knowledge, and power that played a central role in shaping the national project envisioned by Brazilian elites in the early 19th century. Featuring a range of new, recent, and rarely seen works, the exhibition showcases the poetic and conceptual breadth of her practice and its significance across Brazil, the African diaspora, and other international contexts.
