BOOK LAUNCH: BONE SYMPHONY
BY MICHELLE PHƯƠNG HỒ
WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 11, 6PM

Join us in celebrating the launch of Bone Symphony (Boa Editions, 2026), Michelle Phương Hồ’s debut poetry collection. The evening will feature readings and a conversation with poet Tobi Kassim that centers the book’s attempts to create a language of intimacy within spaces of surveillance. Considering the book’s engagement with the archive as both annotation and silence, the two poets will discuss the uses of illegibility, the materiality of voice, and the relationship between critical fabulation and poetry as a sonic record of the unspeakable.

BOOK LAUNCH: STATES OF EMERGENCY: A SPATIAL HISTORY OF THE FRENCH COLONIAL CONTINUUM BY LÉOPOLD LAMBERT
FRIDAY, NOVEMBER 20, 7PM

Join us in celebrating the launch of States of Emergency: A Spatial History of the French Colonial Continuum (Columbia University Press, 2026) by Léopold Lambert. The book navigates the states of emergencies linking three distinct French colonial space-times: the Algerian Revolution (1954–1962), the Kanak Insurrection (1984–1988), and France’s banlieues uprisings (2005–2020). Tracing the mechanics of this counterrevolutionary legal framework across multiple geographies, the book makes legible a colonial continuum traversed by military actors, agents, immigrants, and revolutionaries working both for and against the shared project of liberation.

Lambert will be joined in conversation by Meriam Soltan and Miriam Ticktin to explore the enduring stakes of this research, now appearing in English for the first time. Together, they will consider how these histories and geographies can be read across contexts, what possibilities for anticolonial solidarity emerge through their study in the United States, and how these histories might inform and strengthen our activism in the face of the political pressures of the present moment.

ACCESSING BLACK UTOPIA
MEMBER WORKSHOP WITH ARIANA FAYE ALLENSWORTH AND BABETTE THOMAS
SATURDAY, NOVEMBER 21, 1–3PM

How have Black communities used utopian imagination as a site of political experimentation, collective organizing, and spatial reclamation?

In this collaborative workshop, Ariana Faye Allensworth and Babette Thomas explore how the concept of utopia has informed practices of thinking, organizing, and acting toward liberatory futures. Drawing on their respective research and creative practices rooted in the landscapes, histories, and cultural life of Black California, they consider how Black communities have drawn on utopian thought to establish horizons of freedom and pursue possibilities beyond the conditions of the present in the West Coast and beyond.

If, as Jayna Brown writes, utopia is a way of dreaming “in place, in situ, in medias res, in layers, in dimensional frequencies,” what might it mean to turn toward utopian thought amid the dystopian conditions of our present? Through shared readings and collective interpretation, we’ll explore historical case studies, including the Black Town Movement and the political and artistic organizations that emerged through the Black Power Movement. Together, we’ll consider the range of ways utopian thought has shaped Black freedom struggles and what these histories might offer our own creative practices and political imaginations.

Format: In person
Cost: Free for Wendy’s Subway members

Wendy’s Subway is a non-profit reading room, writing space, and independent publisher located in Bushwick, Brooklyn. We support emerging artists and writers in making experimental, urgent work and create alternative modes for learning and thinking in community. Wendy’s Subway is dedicated to encouraging creative, critical, and discursive engagement with arts and literature.

Our interdisciplinary programs offer free readings, talks, performances, and reading groups, as well as sliding-scale workshops and courses. Our multiseries publishing initiative includes works in translation, artists' books, poetic texts, and hybrid-genre works by time-based artists. Our non-circulating library holds a collection of over 5,000 titles, with a focus on international independent publishing, art and artists’ books, poetry, theory, and criticism.
