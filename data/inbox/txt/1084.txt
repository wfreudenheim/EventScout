FILM HIGHLIGHT | November 6, 2026

Testament

The word “unforgettable” is perhaps overused, but then there are films that truly never leave you. Released at the height of the Cold War nuclear scare of the early 1980s, Testament is a harrowing, intimate drama starring Jane Alexander, in an Oscar-nominated performance, as a California wife and mother struggling to hold her family together after the unthinkable happens: a nuclear strike on the U.S. Keeping the focus entirely on one household and the tight-knit neighborhood around it, director Lynne Littman fashioned the scariest and subtlest movie about the end of the world in American cinema history. Testament also stars William Devane, Lukas Haas, Roxana Zal, Ross Harris, Leon Ames, Kevin Costner, and Rebecca DeMornay.

We are pleased to present this underseen masterpiece in a 35mm print courtesy of the Lynne Littman Collection at the Academy Film Archive, followed by a conversation with Littman about her remarkable career.

In an essay for the Criterion Collection’s recent release of Testament, MoMI’s Senior Curator of Film writes: “Anyone who has witnessed Testament has surely never forgotten it . . . Littman’s movie, one of American cinema’s most singularly unsparing and unbearably intimate works, transforms the safe space of the home into a battlefield of survival. It’s the end of the world as a lullaby of despair.”

Testament screens this Saturday, November 7, at 12:30 p.m.
