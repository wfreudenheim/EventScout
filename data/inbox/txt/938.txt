FILM SCREENING: RETRIBUTION

October 31 from 6:30—8:15 PM (ET)

Join us for a spooky screening this Halloween! In Kiyoshi Kurosawa’s ghost story, tricks of the mind and tricks of the earth collide. A seasoned detective (Kōji Yakusho) investigating a series of murders roams a waterlogged expanse of ruin and construction in search of clues, but the ghost of a murder victim begins to burrow into his mind and house as an earthquake threatens to slide the city back into sea. (Narrow Margin)

Co-presented with Narrow Margin, a quarterly magazine of film criticism. Their forthcoming issue pairs Kurosawa, contemporary cinema’s greatest chronicler of anomie and social malaise, with one of his most important influences, the perennially underrated Richard Fleischer.

ADRIFT IN THE SOUTH: CHINA’S MIGRANT LITERATURE

October 30 from 6:30—8:00 PM

Join China Books Review for a lively conversation on the rise of China’s migrant worker literature, bringing the voices and lived experiences of the country’s vast internal migrant population into the literary mainstream. The talk will feature Xiao Hai, author of Adrift in the South, a gripping autobiographical account of his 23 years as a migrant laborer, as well as its translator Tony Hao.

On November 3, China Books Review will host a book club to discuss Adrift in the South in an intimate setting. Attendance is free, registration is required. Register here.

AROUND TOWN

October 30: Goodbye, Dragon Inn at Museum of the Moving Image

Filled with expertly timed sight gags, Goodbye, Dragon Inn (2003) is Tsai Ming-liang's rueful backwards glance at the disappearance of the filmgoing culture of his youth—and one of the seminal films of the 21st century. Presented in conjunction with MoMI’s new exhibition Seeing the Cinema, which features the work of four photographers, including Hiroshi Sugimoto, who capture the continuing vibrancy of movie theaters.
