Dear readers, 

In January of 2020, at the beginning of what was already fated to be a strange year, Dushko Petrovich Córdova wrote for n+1 about a new tendency in photojournalism: the blur. Figures like John Bolton, Boris Johnson, and Donald Trump emerged out of blurry backgrounds, or appeared threatened by blurred figures in the foreground. Were the blurs a subtle journalistic protest? A byproduct of camera technology? Or did they represent a symbolic shift, toward a messier, murkier future? “If you put all these pictures together,” Petrovich Córdova writes, “they accumulate to something monstrous, almost obscene.” 

In the intervening years, Petrovich Córdova has continued to chronicle the visual record of our political moment for n+1. In “Big Jill, Little Jimmy, Little Rosalynn, Big Joe,” he analyzed a pandemic-era viral photograph of a meeting between the Bidens and the Carters, distorted by a fish-eye lens: “a surreal—and therefore totally accurate—document of what it has felt like to contemplate our countless impending reunions” after the lockdowns.” For Issue 52, he meditated on the aesthetic signifiers of the second Trump term: Michigan governor Gretchen Whitmer hiding her face behind a stack of DNC-blue file folders in a gilded corner of the redecorated Oval Office, the grays and greens worn by ICE agents as they terrorize communities across the country. In “Orange Era,” written for Issue 54, Petrovich Córdova arrived at the defining hue of the post-9/11 age: orange. Orange is the color of “enduring emergency,” as seen first in graphics depicting the terrorism threat level, then in real-time maps of Covid-19 cases and climate catastrophes. 

Petrovich Córdova’s sharp, very funny decodings of the obscene images that fill our newsstands and newsfeeds will now take the form of a regular column for n+1 titled CAN’T UNSEE. The latest entry, on photographs of Trump with microphones, is available to read on our website. To ensure access to future columns, we hope you’ll subscribe today.

Yours,
The Editors
