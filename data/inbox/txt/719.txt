OUR CENTURY: SPIRALING PATHS THROUGH ARMENIAN CINEMA

October 23—25, see below for film descriptions and showtimes

Traveling, leaving one’s home and country, has long been part of the Armenian cultural imagination. Centuries of displacement, the absence of statehood, the Armenian Genocide and the formation of one of the largest diasporas in the world, as well as difficult economic conditions, have made movement an enduring part of Armenian life. The five films in this program follow characters who are constantly on the move. Their journeys unfold across borders and landscapes, but also through memory, history, identity, and imagination.

Guest curated by Sona Karapoghosyan. All film descriptions courtesy of Sona Karapoghosyan. Thank you to the Armenian General Benevolent Union for their support and sponsorship.

HELLO, IT’S ME

October 23 from 6:30—8:45 PM

The first Armenian film to screen at the Cannes Film Festival in 1966, Hello, It’s Me is a fictionalized biography of physicist Artem Alikhanyan, the founder of an important cosmic-ray research center. Filled with melancholy and the pain of loss, this anti-war film explores technological progress and its consequences for basic human relations, while reflecting on the tensions between scientific ambition, personal sacrifice, and the passage of time.

CALENDAR + LEBBEUS/THADDEUS

October 24 from 12:30—2:15 PM

Oscar-nominated director Atom Egoyan’s Calendar follows a photographer who visits Armenia shortly after the country gains independence from the Soviet Union to photograph twelve Armenian churches for a calendar.

Preceded by Lebbeus/Thaddeus, one of the first cinematic works by Iranian-Armenian theatre and film director Arby Ovanessian. This observational documentary follows a pilgrimage of Armenian Christians to the Monastery of Saint Thaddeus, located in Chaldoran County in northwestern Iran.

LAST STATION

October 24 from 3:15—5:00 PM

Last Station tells the story of Egyptian-Armenian actors Nora Armani and Gérald Papasian, who travel to Los Angeles in search of the American dream. Failing to achieve Hollywood fame, they stage a theatrical performance based on Armenian poetry and begin touring the world. Enriched with poetry, music, and poetic urban landscapes, the film becomes a meditation on shattered dreams, longing, belonging, and the possibility of continuing the journey.

Followed by a Q&A with Nora Armani.

AFTER DREAMING

October 25 from 2:15—4:30 PM

The directorial debut of Christine Haroutounian, After Dreaming is a surreal exploration of a patriarchal society inseparable from war. The film examines toxic masculinity and its fragility, contrasting it with non-stereotypical female representation. Followed by a Q&A with Christine Haroutounian.
