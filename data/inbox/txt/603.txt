GODZILLA MINUS ZERO

TOMORROW AT 12:30PM | PRESENTED IN DOLBY ATMOS

Free Marcel Dzama NYFF64 6x10 poster for the first 50 attendees

An experience that demands the big screen, director Takashi Yamazaki’s follow-up to his triumphant, Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One is a supremely satisfying sequel with even more astounding effects choreography than its predecessor.

JOY OF JOYS

TOMORROW AT 6:00PM | EXTENDED Q&A W. JONATHAN GLAZER, MICA LEVI, AND RICH CLARKE

Free Marcel Dzama NYFF64 6x10 poster for the first 50 attendees

This exuberant yet sobering documentary from Jonathan Glazer (The Zone of Interest)—with the help of London schoolkids equipped with cameras—goes behind the scenes at Abbey Road to capture the recording of a charity album from War Child Records, which aids children in conflict zones.

CAMERON WINTER AT CARNEGIE HALL

FRIDAY AT 11:45AM | 35MM

Free Marcel Dzama NYFF64 6x10 poster for the first 50 attendees

Paul Thomas Anderson turns a revelatory, stripped-down set by Brooklyn’s own Cameron Winter into a smashing cinematic concert, shot on 16mm and 35mm with a perfect mix of intimate images of Winter and of the cavernous space.

TONIGHT AT 6:00PM (AMPH)

FREE TALK: ANDREW HAIGH & IRA SACHS

Andrew Haigh (A Long Winter) and Ira Sachs (The Man I Love) reflect on the legacy of 1990s New Queer Cinema, the expressive functions of naturalism and melodrama, and the challenges of dramatizing untold histories of queer life for a contemporary audience.

FREE EVENT: NYFF TRIVIA NIGHT

The New York Film Festival is proud to welcome back Cinephile for our fifth year of NYFF Trivia Night events during the festival at the EBM Amphitheater. Put your film knowledge to the test and win some fun prizes! NYFF Trivia Nights are sponsored by MUBI.

THURSDAY AT 6:00PM (AMPH)

FREE TALK: MAKING THE CUT

The work of the film editor remains one of the least visible but most essential elements of cinematic storytelling. Ted Fendt (Foreign Travel), Anne de Mare and Lynn True (Once Upon a Time in Harlem), and John Gilroy (Behemoth!) explore the craft of film editing in a panel organized in collaboration with American Cinema Editors.

Article preview: Mariano Llinás on Life of Jorge Luis Borges

“One of the things cinema can do better than anything is depict struggles, violence, battles. You see the battles of Griffith or Eisenstein—nobody had done battles like that before. Alexander Nevsky (1938)—in that film you see someone struggling with the language of cinema as a writer would a text. And because of that, it’s quite powerful. That’s what cinema can do.”

—Mariano Llinás on Life of Jorge Luis Borges by Jordan Cronk

STAFF SPOTLIGHT

BELINDA EDWARDS – THEATER STAFF

What film are you most excited to see at NYFF64 and why? 

Once Upon a Time in Harlem because it’s an intersection of a documentary (which I love), a period piece (which I also love), and I’m here to listen to the wisdom (and sometimes folly) of those who were before us.

What do you love about NYFF? 

The people, the excitement, the buzz, the energy! It’s long days, but the days go by quickly. Honestly, I love working NYFF!
