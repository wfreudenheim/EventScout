OCTOBER 30–NOVEMBER 1
BLANK FORMS AT PHONETICS
Riccardo Sinigaglia, Ellen Arkbro, Judith Hamann

Blank Forms participates in the second edition of Phonetics, a festival and fair organized by Paint It Black bookshop in Turin, Italy, bringing together an international array of like-minded experimental platforms and publishers working across books, sounds, images, texts, and video. Alongside its participation in the fair, Blank Forms presents two programs in collaboration with Villa Lontana.

Friday, October 30, 7pm: Riccardo Sinigaglia

Riccardo Sinigaglia first made his mark in Milan’s experimental underground in the early 80s as a member of Walter Maioli’s Futuro Antico, providing the futurist counterpoint to Maioli’s primordial arsenal of winds and ancient instruments. Since then, Sinigaglia has pursued an idiosyncratic path spanning tape music, synthesis, improvisation, and computer music.

Sunday, November 1, 11am: Ellen Arkbro and Judith Hamann

Ellen Arkbro has developed a singular body of work centering the perceptual effects of sustained, closely voiced harmony. Drawing on her studies in just intonation as well as jazz, blues, and experimental composition, Arkbro works with organ, voice, and myriad acoustic instruments to build slowly unfolding harmonic structures, in which small shifts in interval and resonance take on an almost physical presence. These concepts find a close counterpart in Arkbro’s ongoing collaboration with cellist and composer Judith Hamann, whose practice is characterized by vibration, instability, and an acute sensitivity to the relation between instrument, body, and space. Together they present selected reworkings of jazz standards and popular music, as well as other highlights from their years of collaboration.

NOVEMBER 3–6
BLANK FORMS AT TOKYO JAZZ CLUB
Keiji Haino, Daisuke Fujiwara, Fumi Endo, RQRQ, Kyoji Ohno

Tokyo Jazz Club is a group exhibition debuting in 2026 as part of Art Week Tokyo, an annual citywide event presented in collaboration with Art Basel. As part of the inaugural edition’s wider music program, Blank Forms presents an idiosyncratic constellation of musicians from the Japanese underground, complementing curation from frequent Blank Forms collaborator Cyrus Goberville, Head of Cultural Programming at the Pinault Collection.

Tuesday, November 3, 10pm: Keiji Haino

Deeply informed by the concept of ma—the negative space in between, or “the deepest now”—Keiji Haino makes music that vacillates between excess and tranquility, throwing each into stark relief. Working across blues, rock, jazz, and free improvisation, Haino has produced a prolific body of work, both as a solo artist and in influential ensembles like Fushitsusha and Nazoranai, that has cemented his status as one of the living legends of the Japanese underground.

Wednesday, November 4, 8pm: Daisuke Fujiwara + Fumi Endo

Saxophonist Daisuke Fujiwara studied jazz performance and theory in America in the 1990s and, upon returning to Japan, made a name for himself by combining the far-flung improvisations of free jazz with the spare rhythms of minimal techno. His electroacoustic project quartz-head brought him into close contact with leading Detroit figures like Jeff Mills and Underground Resistance, while his work on saxophone has engaged him with ensembles including Boiling Disorder, rabbitoo, and Orquesta Libre.

Pianist Fumi Endo has cultivated a minimalist approach to her instrument, allowing each note or chord to suspend, wavering, in the air before she advances to new territories. Her recent solo debut, Cold Light in Warm Blue, pays careful attention to silence and resonance, defamiliarizing the piano’s timbres by calling attention to the sounds that emerge in the aftermath of a key being struck.

Wednesday, November 4, 9pm: RQRQ

RQRQ unites folk vocalist Yuka Ijichi with bassist Doronco of Les Rallizes Dénudés and guitarist Mitsuru Tabata of Boredoms and Acid Mothers Temple. The trio interprets American jazz standards in both Japanese and English, inflecting classic Western melodies with the vibrant string textures and surprising rhythms of their previous krautrock and psychedelic projects.

Friday, November 6, 6pm: Kyoji Ohno

Kyoji Ohno began making music with synthesizers in the mid-1970s, while earning a degree in engineering at Seikei University, and has continued innovating on the instrument for decades. His cult-classic album Music in DNA (released under the name Yasuhito Ohno), made using a four-track cassette recorder and Roland Juno-60 polyphonic synthesizer, infused the industrial sounds of the New York avant-garde with a playful, lo-fi innocence. Meanwhile, his long-running series of ambient albums—designed for meditation, concentration, physical fitness, and confidence, among other therapeutic purposes—use 3D audio imaging to create works of intricate sonic architecture that work on both the conscious and unconscious mind.

NOVEMBER 6
OCCAM XIII, OCCAM RIVER XI, OCCAM V
Éliane Radigue, Dafne Vicente-Sandoval, Charles Curtis

Occam Ocean is a series of acoustic compositions made for and with individual instrumentalists by the late French composer Éliane Radigue. Occam XIII, Occam River XI, and Occam V feature two longtime collaborators of Radigue in solo and duo formations: Charles Curtis and Dafne Vicente-Sandoval. The works were recorded at Temple Saint-Théodule, La Tour-de-Peilz, Switzerland, during a 2024 residency at La Becque.

Blank Forms is a nonprofit organization supporting emerging and historically significant artists who produce work across disciplines, often rooted in traditions of experimental and creative music. We aim to establish new frameworks to preserve, nurture, and present these artists’ work and to build platforms for practices underrepresented in art’s commercial, institutional, and historical fields. Blank Forms collaborates with artists on commissions, exhibitions, publications as well as archival and estate projects within contemporary cultural ecosystems and in perpetuity. In presenting and documenting this work, Blank Forms seeks to foster an artistic community founded upon engaged and equitable conversations across continents, media, and generations.
