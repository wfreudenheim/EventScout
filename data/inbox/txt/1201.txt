Traditional Japanese Instruments, Lakota Ritual, Electronics and Indigenous AI Meet on a Ceremonial Stage of White Stones

December 11, 2026

As the culminating program in our TWO SOILS Series, Japan Society presents White Stones, a landmark collaboration from Japanese-American multi-instrumentalist Kaoru Watanabe and acclaimed Oglála Lakȟóta performance/visual artist and composer Kite (Suzanne Kite) that asks what being American is. Watanabe’s exquisite fluency across Japanese drums, bamboo flutes, koto and voice take center stage as he and Kite press together their distinct cultural practices into a vibrant soundscape moving between meditative stillness and driving force.

Both artists perform and physically move through a 3-D graphic score devised by Kite, based on the principles of Noh theater and Lakota ceremonial traditions and built from white stones cast in ritual patterns on the stage. Kite’s sovereign AI program, built from the ground up and taught entirely from Indigenous worldviews and ancestral stories, acts as a responsive element on their interwoven soundscape. Pairing centuries-old techniques with new technology across sound, motion and stagecraft, Watanabe and Kite let the friction between their traditions forge a vision of something new and impossible to achieve without each other's influence.

Coming Up: Under the Radar Returns with Yoroboshi at Japan Society

January 14–16, 2027

New York City’s premier festival of experimental theater is back—and we’re thrilled to join this year’s lineup with the North American premiere of Yoroboshi, a gut-punching work of contemporary puppet theater by notorious Japanese playwright and provocateur Satoko Ichihara. Yoroboshi refashions the centuries-old Japanese legend of a blind boy abandoned by his father into a grotesquely comic tale of desire, illness and violence as embodied by a mannequin, a traffic dummy and a sex doll.
