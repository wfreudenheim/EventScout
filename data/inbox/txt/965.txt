WINNING TICKET | RIRKRIT TIRAVANIJA, (LUCKY NO.2)

This week, as part of a special evening program, the winning ticket was drawn for Rirkrit Tiravanija’s (lucky no.2), 2026. SI is pleased to announce that the holder of ticket 110210 receives the key to the artist’s apartment!

Realized in connection with Regift, the exhibition marking SI’s 40th anniversary at Luma Westbau, Zurich, (lucky no.2) turns a lottery into an artwork. Its 729 tickets each carry a combination of 1, 0, and 2 in the Thai lottery format. The 21 refers to both Tiravanija’s apartment number and his birthday. Characteristic of his practice, the work shifts value away from material possession toward encounter and social relation, while simultaneously interrogating ideas of trust, exclusivity, and access.

SI would like thank everyone who bought a ticket and attended this week’s event at WSA. SI would also like to thank Amy Sillman, John Miller, Catharine Czudej, and Yayoi Shionoiri for their contributions.

If you hold the winning ticket, please contact info@swissinstitute.net.

SI is pleased to present a screening of a documentary by Angelblood on the occassion of Rafał Skoczek's offsite solo exhibition, Kino Drift. Angelblood is a New York based band and art collective formed in 2000 by Rita Ackermann, Jess Holzworth, and Lizzi Bougatsos. Producing various collaborative works, Angelblood is characterized by a raw approach to art and life. In their art, music, and performances they are concerned with dark motifs and ritualized actions.

BOOK LAUNCH | NOLAN OSWALD DENNIS: ECLIPTICS

DEC 12 2026, 2:00PM

Drawing Center

35 Wooster Street

New York, NY 10013

Please join us for the launch of Ecliptics, the first-ever monograph by artist Nolan Oswald Dennis, held in collaboration with the Drawing Center.

Inspired by Dennis’s exploration of a “black consciousness of space,” the publication traverses various realms from fiction, linguistics, and metaphysics to astronomy, mathematics, and technology, as well as Black, Indigenous, and anti-imperial intellectual traditions, to consider the planetary conditions for a decolonized future. Offering an introduction to Dennis’s “para-disciplinary” art practice through an elaboration of the political histories, scientific frameworks, and critical theoretical discourses that inform it, the book features newly commissioned texts, a conversation, and reprinted texts by a global constellation of artists and theorists.

On the occasion of the launch, Dennis will be joined in conversation with writer Zoë Hopkins and one of the book’s editors, SI Associate Curator KJ Abudu. Reviving the participatory spirit of the Black Earth Study Club—a series of public programs held during Dennis’s solo exhibition at SI in 2025—the launch will engage the audience in a collective reading and diagramming exercise.
