Here, Already
Tuesday, November 10, 2026, 7pm

Admission:
General $12
Student $10

Join us at e-flux for a screening of films by Salad Hilowle, Ahmed Umar, Samira Elagoz, and Z Walsh, guest-curated by Power Ekroth. Love takes different forms here, from desire hidden in plain sight to intimacy across continents, and music plays a rather different, but equally important, role in each of the three films. In Salad Hilowle’s Passion of Remembrance, 1990s Swedish pop and television reveal the contradiction of being celebrated for contributions to popular culture while still being treated as outsiders because of xenophobia. In Ahmed Umar’s film Truth Bears No Scandal (الـواضِح مو فـاضِح), queer desire, camouflaged within familiar Sudanese songs, is brought to the fore. In Samira Elagoz and Z Walsh’s You Can’t Get What You Want but You Can Get Me, music carries the excitement and longing of a love story unfolding across continents, with all the obstacles that distance brings. Moving from the public to the coded to the intimate, the works extend their antennae toward the society around them, reflecting on how the personal is political and how taking love and respect as guiding principles in life may be radical in its simplicity. The screening will be followed by a discussion between Ekroth, Umar, Elagoz, and Walsh. Read more here.

And the Dogs Were Silent: A Convening by Sharifa Rhodes-Pitts
Thursday, November 12, 2026, 6pm

Admission
General $12
Student $10

The African Film Institute is pleased to present And the Dogs Were Silent, a program convened by Sharifa Rhodes-Pitts. The program features three short films: Sarah Maldoror's Et les chiens se taisaient (And the Dogs Were Silent) (1978), Stan Lathan's Statues Hardly Ever Smile (1971), and Alain Resnais and Chris Marker's Les statues meurent aussi (Statues Also Die)(1953). Drawing on Aimé Césaire’s theatrical poem on the Haitian Revolution, these works confront colonial collections and museum holdings through acts of cultural reclamation and historical restoration. The screening will be followed by a conversation between Rhodes-Pitts and Christian Nyampeta. Read more here.
