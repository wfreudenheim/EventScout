AUTHOR TALKS & DISCUSSIONS

THE JOANNA JACKSON GOLDMAN MEMORIAL LECTURES | NATIVE NATIONS, BENJAMIN FRANKLIN & THE COMING OF THE AMERICAN REVOLUTION WITH NED BLACKHAWK

Part I: Tuesday, November 17 | 6 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
Part II: Wednesday, November 18 | 6 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
This biennial lecture series at The New York Public Library, established by the estate of the historian Eric F. Goldman in honor of his wife, aims to encourage provocative comment and analysis concerning contemporary issues of deep, long-term significance for American democracy. In Part I, Ned Blackhawk discusses Benjamin Franklin's origins and the influence of his family's relationships with Native communities. In Part II, he expands on Franklin's role in the conflicts over Indian affairs and in the Revolution to explore imperial warfare, settler uprisings, and the crisis of Native American diplomacy. This is a two-part lecture; attendees must register for each part of the lecture separately.

ATTEND ᐳ

MEMOIRS OF MAGIC QUEEN: RESTORING ADELAIDE HERRMANN'S LOST LEGACY

Mon, Nov 2 | 6 PM | Library for the Performing Arts & Online
Adelaide Herrmann once reigned supreme as vaudeville's Queen of Magic, but, after she died suddenly at the age of 78, her memoir was lost for a time. Celebrate a newly annotated and expanded edition of her memoir by Margaret B. Steele with an illustrated talk, magic performances, and a display of rarely seen Herrmann memorabilia, followed by a book signing.

ATTEND ᐳ

BEFORE NEW YORK: ERIC SANDERSON WITH MICHAEL KIMMELMAN

Tues, Nov 10 | 6 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building & Online
In Before New York, Eric W. Sanderson invites us on a breathtaking journey through the evolution of New York City—its natural features, inhabitants, and geography—from long before Henry Hudson's arrival in 1609 up to the present day. Sanderson worked on Before New York: The Natural Geography of the City during his 2019–20 Fellowship at the Library's Dorothy and Lewis B. Cullman Center for Scholars and Writers. He will discuss his book with journalist Michael Kimmelman. In-person tickets sold out; livestream tickets still available.

ATTEND ᐳ

CHANGING TASTES: AMERICAN FOODWAYS AT 250

Tues, Nov 10 | 6:30 PM | Chatham Square Library & Online
Join Top Chef semi-finalist and culinary storyteller Lana Lagomarsini and Jane Ziegelman, author of 97 Orchard: An Edible History of Five Immigrant Families in One New York Tenement, to talk about the history of foodways in the U.S. through the lens of immigrant and Black American culinary traditions. They will discuss 97 Orchard alongside Edna Lewis's beloved Southern cookbook The Taste of Country Cooking. Both titles feature on NYPL's Reading America: 250 Years, 250 Books list.

THE NEXT CENTURY OF BLACK STUDIES CONFERENCE

Mon, Nov 16–Wed, Nov 18 | 10 AM–5:30 PM | Schomburg Center & Online
The Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture has invited a diverse group of university and independent scholars, graduate students, community archivists, librarians, and public historians to consider the next century of Black Studies. Join us to explore the frontiers of Black Studies, elevate neglected narratives, and consider the ethical, legal, and political challenges of the field. Check-in and coffee at 9 AM.

ATTEND ᐳ

BETWEEN THE LINES: BETWEEN THE RIVER & THE RAILROAD TRACKS BY NICOLE R. FLEETWOOD

Tues, Nov 17 | 6:30 PM | Schomburg Center
In her debut memoir, Between the River and the Railroad Tracks, NYPL and MacArthur Fellow Nicole R. Fleetwood weaves a multigenerational family story into a neighborhood's history, illuminating the often-overlooked Black Midwest. Join the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture for a special evening moderated by Hanif Abdurraqib, marking the launch celebration and conversation with Fleetwood, who has conducted research at both the Schomburg and Cullman Centers, about family, place, memory, and history. A reception and book signing follow.

ATTEND ᐳ

SCREENING & DISCUSSION! THE DRUG IN OUR POCKET: SCREENS, YOUTH & MENTAL HEALTH

Thurs, Nov 19 | 5:30 PM | Stephen A. Schwarzman Building
Join us for an evening screening of clips from the new documentary The Drug in Our Pocket followed by a panel conversation moderated by the film's producer, Beth Kojima, on how digital technology is shaping young people's mental health and what families and schools can do together to reclaim their attention, agency, and time. Featured panelists include Dr. Aliza Pressman, Tali Horowitz, Melissa Bernstein, and Annie Sunberg; an audience Q&A will follow. Complimentary light bites and beverages will be provided, and eligible educators will receive 2 CTLE credits.
