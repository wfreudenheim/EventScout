NO TOMORROW
BY WALKER RUTTER-BOWMAN

“What makes the time loop both appealing and terrifying is the tempting thought that our own human mind might be the only real thing in the world, our individual experience the only one like it.”

From Groundhog Day to Solvej Balle’s On the Calculation of Volume, time-loop narratives increasingly speak to the sense of alienation and uncertainty that defines much of the present. The protagonists of these stories are usually stuck and alone, doomed to repeat the same day over and over again without escape. In a new essay, Walker Rutter-Bowman investigates how the temporal glitch that removes these characters from a shared reality can make space for new concepts of time and forms of community.
