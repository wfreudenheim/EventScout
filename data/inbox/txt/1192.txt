FLY, WILD SWANS: BOOK TALK WITH JUNG CHANG AND JIAYANG FAN

November 20 from 6:30—8:00 PM (ET)

China Books Review hosts a special evening with Jung Chang, the internationally acclaimed author of the multimillion-copy bestseller Wild Swans, as she discusses her powerful new memoir, Fly, Wild Swans: My Mother, Myself, and China. Moderated by New Yorker staff writer Jiayang Fan, this event offers a rare opportunity to hear from one of the most influential chroniclers of modern Chinese history and memory.

In Fly, Wild Swans, Chang returns to the themes that made Wild Swans a landmark work, while charting a deeply personal new chapter. The memoir intertwines two lives separated by geography but bound by history: Chang’s own journey after leaving China in 1978 to begin a new life abroad, and the story of her mother, who remained behind as China entered a period of profound transformation.

UP NEXT: DANSAEKHWA: KOREAN ABSTRACT ART AND ITS CULTURAL IMPACT

November 17 from 6:30—7:30 PM

Emerging in the 1970s, Dansaekhwa or monochrome painting was a major abstract art movement in postwar South Korea that pushed the boundaries of painting and color outside the canvas into fields that included drawing, craft, sculpture, installation, and conceptual and experimental art. Celebrate the launch of Dansaekhwa: Korean Abstract Art and Its Cultural Impact with author Dr. Yeon Shim Chung in conversation with Andrew Russeth of Artnet News.

FROM SURVIVAL TO RENEWAL: MORAD TAHBAZ IN CONVERSATION

December 8 from 6:30—8:00 PM
