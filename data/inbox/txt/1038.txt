FILM SERIES | FILMCLUB POPULAIRE

NOV 4 2026, 7:00PM

SI is pleased to present the next iteration in a film screening series held on the occassion of Rafał Skoczek's offsite solo exhibition, Kino Drift. Programmed by Filmclub Populaire, a film collective based in Zurich, the screening will show a recently digitalised documentary, Zur Wohnunsfrage 1972, by Hans and Nina Sturm.

Referencing a 1872 essay by Friedrich Engels, the film examines and describes housing conditions in the cities of Berlin, Munich, and Zurich. It interviews residents of satellite cities and ghettoized areas as well as involved architects, urban planners, housing associations, and real estate entities.

This event will be held at WSA, 161 Water Street, 7th Floor, New York, NY 10038.

Please RSVP to rsvp@swissinstitute.net.

FILM SERIES | MARCHING WITH THE KASAMA

NOV 11 2026, 7:00PM

Please join us for the last screening in a film series held on the occassion of Rafał Skoczek's offsite solo exhibition, Kino Drift.

This screening features the documentary Marching with the Kasama, which offers a glimpse into the Philippine liberation struggle. Through voices of peasants, workers, and guerilla fighters, the film portrays a social movement for land and people's power. The presentation of the footage is not a finished film, but rather an outline and, in itself, part of an ongoing process.

Marching with the Kasama will be presented alongside two other films exploring related topics.

This event will be held at WSA, 161 Water Street, 7th Floor, New York, NY 10038.

Please RSVP to rsvp@swissinstitute.net.

BOOK LAUNCH | NOLAN OSWALD DENNIS: ECLIPTICS

DEC 12 2026, 12:00-2:00PM

Drawing Center

35 Wooster Street

New York, NY 10013

Please join us for the launch of Ecliptics, the first-ever monograph by artist Nolan Oswald Dennis, held in collaboration with the Drawing Center.

Inspired by Dennis’s exploration of a “black consciousness of space,” the publication traverses various realms from fiction, linguistics, and metaphysics to astronomy, mathematics, and technology, as well as Black, Indigenous, and anti-imperial intellectual traditions, to consider the planetary conditions for a decolonized future. Offering an introduction to Dennis’s “para-disciplinary” art practice through an elaboration of the political histories, scientific frameworks, and critical theoretical discourses that inform it, the book features newly commissioned texts, a conversation, and reprinted texts by a global constellation of artists and theorists.

On the occasion of the launch, Dennis will be joined in conversation with writer Zoë Hopkins and one of the book’s editors, SI Associate Curator KJ Abudu. Reviving the participatory spirit of the Black Earth Study Club—a series of public programs held during Dennis’s solo exhibition at SI in 2025—the launch will engage the audience in a collective reading and diagramming exercise.
