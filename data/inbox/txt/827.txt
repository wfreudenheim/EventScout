Thank you to all who joined us at the Vera List Center Forum 2026: Matter of Intelligence! We gathered alongside artists, scholars, curators, technologists, and friends in celebration of Rosana Paulino, recipient of the 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice, along with the Jane Lombard Fellows: ArTree Nepal, Stephanie Dinkins, Jason Edward Lewis, and Jenna Sutela.

As we continue with Matter of Intelligence, our 2025–2027 research theme, we reflect on a common thread that emerged in each of the forum presentations—from a study group and a conversation on "epistemic resistance" to an improvised performance inspired by an act of collective dreaming: intelligence, whether embodied, ancestral, or artificial, is a relational endeavor, one that amounts to resilient, generative, and creative infrastructures. Stay tuned for full documentation on our website in the coming weeks. 

We'd love to hear your thoughts on this year's program through this short survey. By participating in the survey, you will be entered for a chance to win one of five $25 Bookshop.org gift cards.

Join us next for the second Black Feminist Study Group tomorrow, October 17, and for Intelligence, Uncertain on Monday, October 19, which kicks off year two of our Matter of Intelligence Seminar Series. And be sure to visit Rosana Paulino's prize exhibition ¿História Natural? through November 1.

The Black Feminist Study Group is a four-part program shaped by the poetics and practice of Rosana Paulino and assembled within her prize exhibition Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural?. The weekly series is anchored by seminal texts that touch on interconnected struggles for liberation and resistance throughout the African diaspora. Writer and critic Zoë Hopkins joins us for session II of the study group, with a reading and discussion of Dionne Brand's "Nomenclature for the Time Being," hosted by the VLC's Re'al Christian. Visit our website for the full series lineup.

MATTER OF INTELLIGENCE SEMINAR

SEMINAR 7: INTELLIGENCE, UNCERTAIN

MONDAY, OCTOBER 19, 2026
2–3:30 PM EDT 
ONLINE

If intelligence has always been a contested site—attributed, withheld, performed, disputed—misinformation and disinformation reveal the political stakes of that contest in their sharpest form. Presented in partnership with Haus der Kulturen der Welt (HKW), Berlin, this seminar explores uncertainty in the context of contemporary cultural, political, and technological transformations. HKW Fellows Nora Al-Badri and manuel arturo abreu and neuroscientist and writer Suparna Choudhury consider how artistic practice might engage these conditions by holding complexity, sustaining uncertainty without surrendering to it, and refusing false clarity without collapsing into relativism. Hosted by the VLC's Eriola Pira and moderated by HKW's Sara Morais dos Santos Bruss.

Rosana Paulino: ¿História Natural? features the work of Brazilian artist and Jane Lombard Prize for Art and Social Justice recipient Rosana Paulino. Anchored by her landmark artist book of the same name, the exhibition traces Paulino’s critical examinations of the entanglements between science, race, gender, knowledge, and power that played a central role in shaping the national project envisioned by Brazilian elites in the early 19th century. Featuring a range of new, recent, and rarely seen works, the exhibition showcases the poetic and conceptual breadth of her practice and its significance across Brazil, the African diaspora, and other international contexts.
