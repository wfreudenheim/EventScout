GLOBAL MASHUP: Hollywood Meets Bollywood

COMMUNITY DIGEST

In 1877, Edison invented the phonograph, the device that finally allowed us to bring music into our homes. Before that, music was almost exclusively a community affair. People of all ages got together in halls, saloons, theaters, parks, yards, and spaces of all sizes to listen and dance. In 1979, 102 years after the phonograph, Sony introduced the world to the Walkman. This led to individual sound consumption for the first time in history. Sure, people were already listening to music alone in their rooms or cars, but now the casual passerby would not hear the music. Throughout all these leaps in recorded technology, we have never abandoned the live music experience. In many ways, the siloing of sound has made the concert experience so much more of a big deal.

 

I listen to music on my smartphone when I’m on the move, and on my record players when I am at home. These methods only enhance my live music consumption. Sometimes I get music after hearing it live first, and other times I go to a show to enhance my already established appreciation for the music. In both scenarios, live music is still on top. Live music gives us a unique communal experience that can be recorded but never replicated. Whenever I listen to a song I’ve heard performed live, my memory instantly goes to that moment. I remember the people to my right and left. I remember the scents and the way my skin tingled. I remember the musicians and the audience and the venues. There are so many reasons to stay home, but when we venture outside our walls, we can commune with all kinds of art. This autumn and beyond, whether you come to FTH or go somewhere else, get outside and catch a show. Be a part of something magical.  

Palante (Onward) 

—Eliel Lucero
