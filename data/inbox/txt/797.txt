MEET THE 2027 UNDO FELLOWS

----------------------------------------

The fourth iteration welcomes an extraordinary cohort whose work expands the possibilities of documentary cinema.

The UNDO Fellowship supports radical filmmaking practices and new languages of documentary cinema. By pairing filmmakers with writers, the fellowship fosters unexpected encounters for sustained dialogue, inviting each participant to challenge, complicate, and deepen the other's thinking.

BEATRIZ SANTIAGO MUÑOZ & ALMUDENA ESCOBAR LÓPEZ
How might filmmaking become a way of inventing rather than representing aesthetic experiences? Filmmaker Beatriz Santiago Muñoz and writer/scholar Almudena Escobar López’s research begins with a simple proposition: that cinema's political potential may lie less in what it shows than in the forms of relation, collaboration, shared perception, and social relations it makes possible. Their inquiry is grounded in forms of improvisation, sustained attention, and deep listening structural to Santiago Muñoz’s practice.

BEN RUSSELL & JACQUELINE FELDMAN
How can nonfiction take on the revolutionary project of making time—even now—livable for all? Filmmaker Ben Russell and writer Jacqueline Feldman train their attention on documentary film’s capacity, amidst a shattering of possibilities for solidarity and encounter, to restore to lived time its relational, durational potential. Expanding on a realism of revolutionary time seen in Russell’s oeuvre—where time is elastic; where life’s rhythms are, for the audience, a call to presence—their research takes as its object the strength of narrative and anti-narrative in relation with the time-bound subject.

LEE ANNE SCHMITT & TUNG-HUI HU
What can the intimate details of everyday life reveal about the systems that shape our world? Filmmaker Lee Anne Schmitt and poet and digital media scholar Tung-Hui Hu turn to domestic life and its intimate landscapes as indexes of the collapse of larger systems in the present. Drawing on Schmitt’s longstanding focus on personal, family history and everyday life, and the economic, environmental, social pressures that attend them, their research seeks out, in the rhythms of routine, proxies for and grounds of contestation of current intersecting political crises.

SIMON LIU & PHIL COLDIRON
What if the mirror documentary cinema holds up to reality were more like a funhouse? Artist Simon Liu and writer Phil Coldiron approach the documentary image as a site of opacity and distortion, one capable of producing truths irreducible to language. Working through Liu's evolving practice of immersive installation, where mirrors, handmade technologies, and intricate montage refract documentary images into atmospheric environments that resist legibility, their research asks how disorientation might become a means of deeply felt engagement rather than passive spectatorship.

The UNDO Fellowship is generously funded by the Ford Foundation's JustFilms. Each fellow receives a significant fee for their participation, with unrestricted support for the artists and a writing commission for their collaborators. CAMERA MINISTRY: LENS-BASED ENCOUNTERS

----------------------------------------

A workshop exploring lens-based storytelling as a practice of presence, connection and encounter.

Nov 13 — Nov 15

In this three-day intensive workshop, photographer and filmmaker Khalik Allah invites participants into the methods and philosophy behind his singular lens-based practice and what he calls the “Camera Ministry.” Moving between street photography, portraiture, and poetic documentary, we’ll consider image-making as a practice of presence and encounter: what does it mean to truly see another person, and how can the camera become a means for connection rather than simply a tool for observation? Through screenings, discussion, and hands-on exploration, participants will think through how intuition, repetition, proximity, conversation, trust, and vulnerability enter into making an image, alongside the technical knowledge that allows the camera itself to become increasingly unobtrusive.

 

Jamil GS
Visiting Artist

Alongside Khalik, filmmaker, cinematographer, and teacher Shevaun Mizrahi will discuss her transition from cinematographer to director and the ways magical realism and vérité interact in her work, while legendary hip-hop photographer and cultural archivist Jamil GS will reflect on photography as a form of cultural memory. Participants will also have the opportunity to share their own lens-based work with Khalik in a creative feedback session, bringing questions of attention, intimacy, responsibility, and representation back into their own practices.

SHOWGIRLS OF PAKISTAN

----------------------------------------

Enter the universe of three mujra dancers in Pakistan as they dodge state censors and violence to vie for stardom.

Sunday, October 18 — 7:30PM

We’re delighted to come together with our friends at 犄角旮旯 Nooks & Crannies for a special Queer History Month screening of Saad Khan’s Showgirls of Pakistan, presented with Khajistan and DecrimNY. Following three mujra dancers as they navigate the Punjabi theater business, the film draws on intimate vérité, phone calls, social media streams, and home videos to follow women determined to play by their own rules. After the screening, director Saad Khan and art director and co-editor Joey Chriqui will be in conversation with artist Pranav Sood to discuss the film as well as their project Khajistan, the New York-based archive and press preserving censored, banned, and overlooked print and audiovisual culture from the Middle World. The evening will also feature a selection of Khajistan publications and merch, and will raise funds for DecrimNY’s work to decriminalize, decarcerate, and destigmatize the sex trades in New York.
