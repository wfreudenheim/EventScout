MATTER OF INTELLIGENCE SEMINAR

SEMINAR 8: COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE, COLLECTIVE AGENCY

TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 17, 2026, 6:30–8 PM EST 
WOLLMAN HALL, THE NEW SCHOOL
65 WEST 11TH STREET, 5TH FLOOR

The eighth installment of our Matter of Intelligence Seminar Series is convened with and features artist Agnieszka Kurant in conversation with neuroscientist and AI researcher Ida Momennejad. This seminar extends the question of intelligence beyond the individual to the central concern of Kurant’s work: collective intelligence. 

Collective intelligence describes how novel forms emerge from complex systems in nonlinear and unpredictable ways. Drawing on living systems, the evolution of culture and technology, artificial life, simulated societies, and AI agent swarms, the program also explores the processes of networked value creation in the digital economy and the global extraction of labor of the multitude underlying artificial intelligence. Considering intelligence as an emergent property of entire ecosystems, the speakers ask what new forms of agency collective intelligence makes possible, and what its political implications are. The conversation is introduced and moderated by the VLC's Eriola Pira.

EXCITING NEWS AND ANNOUNCEMENTS FROM OUR GLOBAL NETWORK OF ARTISTS, SCHOLARS, ACTIVISTS, AND ORGANIZATIONS WITHIN THE VLC COMMUNITY.

Join current VLC Fellow Joyce Joumaa for the theatrical premiere of her latest film Disappearance is not Death this evening, November 2, at the Museum of Modern Art. Presented alongside a selection of Joumaa's shorts as part of MoMA's long-running series Modern Mondays, the film centers on the military leader and folk hero Youssef Bey Karam, who led a rebellion against Ottoman rule in the 1860s and was later driven into exile. Blending fact and fiction, Joumaa's film unpacks the galvanizing and fraught image of the national hero. 

The fall issue of Art in America features a profile on 2004–2005 VLC Fellow Walid Raad by Emily Watlington, tracing the artist's practice from his earliest interventions as a teenager working against the backdrop of the Lebanese Civil War to his current presentation at the 61st Venice Biennale, where he unpacks the narrative fluidity of historical archives. 

At the Staten Island Museum, 2025–2027 Jane Lombard Fellow Stephanie Dinkins's solo exhibition Anchoring Home opens on November 14. Blending storytelling with emerging technologies, the show centers on an interconnected installation of evolving video, sound, and AI-generated imagery that challenges us to reconsider the relationships between community, technology, and the worlds we are currently building.

Finally, after shifting our clocks back to mark the end of Daylight Saving Time, 2020–2022 VLC Fellow Rasheedah Phillips's new essay “All the Possible Paths” unpacks the limitations of the colonial technologies we use to measure time, urging us instead to consider the "liberatory spacetime" of dreaming. Read the full essay online in United States Artists' new book Artists on Time.
