MALL COUNTRY

BY SHELBY SHAW
NOVEMBER 12, 2026

READ ON OUR SITE

It's projected that the United States may only have 900 malls actively running by 2028, down from around 1,200 in 2023 and 25,000 in 1986. The shopping mall, an iconic staple of the modern U.S., can trace its history back to the end of the 18th century, when the first covered shopping passage was built in Paris. Today, the American mall has largely devolved into a perverted use of time and space: a massive expanse of paved-over land, gargantuan concrete structures with no windows, and an interior architecture that remains consistent and ubiquitous, somehow making every trip to the mall into the same experience. Despite their promise to host all your needs under one roof, American malls have become increasingly unnecessary to visit, and all the more irrelevant now that online shopping has made everyone’s needs (and more) available inside one screen. Who could possibly imagine revitalizing a dead mall today and succeeding?

His name is Leo Karruli. In that all-too-common mistake of placing an online bid you're sure will be outbid by someone else, Karruli became a mall owner by accident when nobody outbid him at $3.15 million, so he and his wife Irene took over the Johnstown Galleria in 2022. A super regional mall of over 800k square feet and four anchor stores in Johnstown, PA, it had been open for only 30 years by the time it had fallen into Karruli's hands in both a state of despair and disrepair. Only 30% of the stores had tenants. The escalators didn't work, or weren't turned on. Almost nobody was going there. In 2024, documentarian Brian Becker went and filmed Karruli's determined attempts to resuscitate The Galleria over the course of the year. The resulting Mall Country isn't a story of how to save the American shopping mall; it's a portrait of one man single-handedly working to make a mall serve its small-business owners and their town.

In some ways, Karruli succeeds (he fills storefronts and boosts general foot traffic), and in others he fails. But he spends every day trying to fix the mall's problems, from the air conditioning to the speaker system, largely on his own. Johnstown once thrived on steel, but after a particularly devastating flood in 1977, business never recovered. From a peak population of more than 67,000 in 1920, the latest census reports a population of 18,411. Under Karruli, small businesses get an opportunity to launch as part of something bigger—The Galleria—with cheap rents and personal support. When tenants can't make rent, Karruli covers it himself. When an Italian restaurant in the food court is about to close, Karruli buys it and works as the cook. He meets regularly with the business owners and responds to their advice and feedback. He is an ideal mayor for The Galleria.

Except that good intentions aren't enough to bring customers back and keep businesses open. As much as we’re treated to archival footage of mallgoers boasting about these new “community” spaces, we are also shown the disdain the malls drew for being privately owned and operated. How can an American mall carry meaning today? Karruli is close to finding out; in the meantime, you can see all of the stores still standing vacant on The Galleria's online map, in case a solution comes to mind.

Mall Country screens this evening, November 12, at SVA Theatre as part of DOC NYC. Director Brian Becker and Leo Karruli will be in attendance for a Q&A.
