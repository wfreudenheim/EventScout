This week at e-flux

Incomputable Earth: Technology and the Anthropocene Hypothesis
Tuesday, October 20, 2026, 7pm

Free admission

Join us for the launch of Incomputable Earth: Technology and the Anthropocene Hypothesis (Bloomsbury Academic, 2026), a new anthology that challenges the dominant narrative that positions technological solutions as the primary response to ecological crisis. The four contributors speaking at the launch approach these problems from different sites. Tega Brain starts from the word "ecosystem," which since Arthur Tansley coined it in 1935 has cast the environment as bounded, knowable, and available for optimization. She asks what that framing has edited out, from scientific forestry to the Grasslands Biome modeling project of 1968 and the ozone loss NASA's satellites missed. Gary Zhexi Zhang takes up finance as the planet's infrastructure of time: how catastrophe gets priced, who insures the insurers when the losses exceed them, and how movements such as DefundDAPL turn dispossession into financial risk. Editor Antonia Majaca turns to the Anthropocene hypothesis itself and to what it would take to reclaim abstraction from computational capture. Bar Laika presents Playback 0040 with Solpara
Wednesday, October 21, 2026, 7pm

Join us at Bar Laika for the fortieth edition of Playback with Solpara. Lebanese-American musician Solpara moves between experimental music, club culture, and interdisciplinary performance. He began making music as a teenager in early-2000s New York, collaborating with Nicolas Jaar before relocating to Montreal, where he co-founded Booma Collective with Valentin Stip and Oren Ratowsky. After returning to New York in 2015, he released his debut solo EP on Jaar’s Other People imprint, followed by records on Quiet Time and Poland’s Brutaz. His debut album, Melancholy Sabotage, arrived in 2024 with Resident Advisor describing it as “undeniably chic post-punk.” Read more here. 

 

Tim Altenhof, Breathing Space: The Architecture of Pneumatic Beings
Thursday, October 22, 2026, 7pm

Free admission

Join us for a conversation between Tim Altenhof, Peter Szendy, and Emily Apter on the occasion of the launch of Altenhof’s book Breathing Space: The Architecture of Pneumatic Beings  (Zone Books, 2026). Breathing Space is a compelling and wide-ranging analysis of pneumatic phenomena in modern culture. Architect and historian Tim Altenhof explores the physiology of breathing and its reciprocal relationship to bodies and buildings, both of which share a common atmosphere. Read more here.
