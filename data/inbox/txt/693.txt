OCTOBER NEWSLETTER: APPLY FOR ENVIRONMENTAL HUMANITIES GRANTS

The study of the environment and the climate crisis is not solely the domain of scientists. How can art, writing, or performance deepen our understanding of nature and humankind's place in it? And how can humanities and social science approaches offer new responses to climate change?

The Environmental Humanities Grant program invites Columbia University students, faculty, and researchers to propose humanities-based projects that explore these possibilities. Co-leads from outside Columbia are welcome. In the previous cycle, 66% of awardees had student leads and 33% had community co-leads.

Grants of up to $5,000 are available. Applications are due December 20. Visit our website to learn more.

MAPPING AI: EUGENICS

October 13 | 5:00 PM - 7:00 PM ET
Fayerweather Hall (Room 513), Columbia University

This workshop asks participants to explore the marginalizing origins and impacts of artificial intelligence. Following a discussion on eugenics in big tech and big data, attendees will consider where such dynamics play out locally. Free and open to the public; registration required.

CLIMATE RISKSCAPES: AN INTERDISCIPLINARY CONFERENCE ON LIVING AND KNOWING UNDER ENVIRONMENTAL PRECARITY

October 22 | 3:00 PM - 6:45 PM
October 23 | 9:00 AM - 3:00 PM
Online and In-person: Fayerweather Hall (Room 513), Columbia University

In the global North, climate change plans are multi-year timelines of gradual changes. But for communities in arid and sub-tropical regions, climate risk is experienced and managed on a shorter timescale. What can this expertise teach us about resilience? Free and open to the public; registration required.

Register Now

SEEING CATTLE LIKE A STATE: NOMADIC PASTORALISM IN THE AGE OF TRACEABILITY

October 26 | 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM
Online and In-person: Fayerweather Hall (Room 513), Columbia University

Governments use livestock identification and traceability systems (LITS) to track and respond to disease and livestock theft. But these systems are not designed for pastoralist communities. Learn about the economic, political and social impact of LITS on pastoralists in Namibia. Free and open to the public; registration required.

Register Now

THE TIN CENTURIES: TECHNOLOGY AND STATECRAFT IN QING CHINA

October 28 | 6:30 PM - 8:00 PM ET
Online and In-person: Gallatin School (Room 527), New York University

How did a mundane metal bind miners and officials to global capitalism and an emerging technocratic state? A book launch and conversation on skills, migration, natural resources, and capitalism in early modern China. Free and open to the public; registration required.
