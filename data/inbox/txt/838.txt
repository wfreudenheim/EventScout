IN PURSUIT OF LIFE, LIBERTY, AND HAPPINESS

EXPLORE THE CONVENING

Join Creative Time and the Brooklyn Museum on Friday, October 23, from 11 AM to 5:30 PM for In Pursuit of Life, Liberty, and Happiness: Reimagining American Democracy, a daylong convening that brings artists, activists, and scholars together to interrogate the promises of American democracy, featuring keynote speaker Chelsea Manning, technologist and data scientist.

With remarks from District 35 Council Member Crystal Hudson, Bertha Vanayshun as MC, and poetry and performances by Nicole Sealey and Keith Yamashita, the dynamic roster of speakers includes American Artist, Mary Enoch Elizabeth Baxter, Tega Brain, Mona Chalabi, Johanna Hedva, Sky Hopinka, Qween Jean, Mariame Kaba, Sam Lavigne, Dean Majd, Paola Ramos, Viva Ruiz, Dr. Yomaira C. Figueroa-Vásquez, and Jackie Wang.

Tickets are $30 general admission and $18 for Brooklyn Museum members.

CONVERSATIONS AND PRESENTATIONS

Our first dialogue of the day, LIFE / Beyond Borders: Mapping Movement and Migration Histories, situates contemporary struggles for migrant justice within longer histories of colonialism, racism, and exclusion. Featuring illustrator and writer Mona Chalabi; visual artist and filmmaker Sky Hopinka; journalist Paola Ramos; and Hunter College Center for Puerto Rican Studies director Dr. Yomaira Figueroa-Vásquez, presenters will draw on art, data, and organizing to envision futures beyond borders.

 

LIBERTY / After Incarceration: Envisioning Abolitionist Futures brings together American Artist; interdisciplinary artist, writer, and cultural worker Mary Enoch Elizabeth Baxter; archivist, educator, and writer Mariame Kaba; and poet and scholar Jackie Wang. From conceptual art to community processes for accountability, this conversation recontextualizes the constitutional promise of freedom through an abolitionist lens. Together, speakers imagine alternatives to the US culture of punishment and caging that instead seek justice and repair.

 

PURSUIT OF HAPPINESS presentations will feature a multidisciplinary lineup of speakers grappling with our fraught democracy while visualizing future possibilities, including artist and engineer Tega Brain with artist and educator Sam Lavigne; artist, musician, and writer Johanna Hedva; artist Dean Majd; artist and founder of Thank God for Abortion Viva Ruiz; and Qween Jean, costume designer and founder of Black Trans Liberation.

Co-presented in partnership with Brooklyn Museum. This convening is part of Drawing America: US@250. A season of art installations, programs, and partnerships, Drawing America honors those who have imagined a more equitable future and fought for the promise of our democracy—and those who continue to do so today.
