Dear readers, 

If you’re reading this newsletter, you’re probably already aware that Bookmatch is back for its seventh year running. But just in case, here’s a refresher on how it works: when you donate any amount to n+1 in the month of November, you’ll be sent a link to the Bookmatch quiz. Answer the questions, and we’ll send you a list of 10 book recommendations chosen just for you by our editors, contributors, and other friends of the magazine.

The goal of Bookmatch (other than to raise ever-more-essential funds for n+1 to keep publishing) is to provide our community with surprising and mind-expanding literary inspiration from real people in age of AI and algorithms. To quote our coeditor, Dayna Tortorici: 

> This year the message of Bookmatch is: Slow down. Read a book. Read ten books! Read ten books recommended by living human beings whose taste and intelligence are hard won and wholly singular, forged in the crucible of their idiosyncratic minds. And take heart in knowing that you have clawed back a little of what the forces of reaction are trying to take from you—your peace, your pleasure, and the unproductive, ruminative hours that are your birthright as a human being.

This week, we’re gathering some of the best writing we’ve published by this year’s Bookmatch recommenders, so that you can spend some time inside those idiosyncratic minds. Read Alexandra Kleeman on the Woodstock Fruit Festival, Rachel Ossip on the woman behind an indelible steakhouse ad, Sarah Thankam Mathews on New York’s young Luddites, and Sarah Resnick on the cryptosphere; read Mina Tavakoli on a ventriloquist conference in Kentucky, Justin Taylor on the ouevre of Katherine Dunn, and read fiction by Tony Tulathimutte—and then, when you’re finished, let them tell you what to read next. 

Yours, 
The Editors
