MoMI NEWS | November 12, 2026

Dear will,

This Friday, we're excited to launch the dual retrospective The Minnellis with a 35mm screening of the Oscar-winning masterpiece Cabaret and celebratory reception; followed through the weekend with screenings of Vincente Minnelli's Some Came Running; The Long, Long Trailer; and The Cobweb—all in 35mm. Also this week, our Kubrick series continues with satirical masterpiece Dr. Strangelove; and we welcome James Gray with a preview of his superb latest film Paper Tiger, the centerpiece of our series James Gray's New York, which also features The French Connection this weekend. Greek film series Always on Sunday returns with Hold Onto Me, Cyprus's first-ever submission for the Best International Feature Oscar.

Last week we were honored to host Diya Vij, Commissioner of the NYC Department of Cultural Affairs, at the Museum for a listening session with teens about how AI and other new technologies are affecting art-making. This Friday, Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment Commissioner Rafael Espinal joins us for the Queens stop of his "Made in New York" Listening Tour hosted by Queens World Film Festival.

In Open Worlds, join us on Friday for the free Marvels of Media Festival Kickoff Mixer, free yoga with The Yoga Room on Sunday, and mark your calendars for MoMI Merch Meet, our first ever artisan market, on Nov. 22! 

MoMI LAB, our co-creation space for technology and culture, presents the workshop Free Yourself from Big Tech, and Phone-Free Fridays continues through December.

Coming soon: two final MoMI After Dark Tours; an episode of The Muppet Show with Liza Minnelli paired with The Muppets Take Manhattan; director Susan Seidelman in person with Desperately Seeking Susan; and more. 

EXHIBITION HIGHLIGHT

Felix the Cat shorts
Closing Thursday, November 12

Courtesy of Rockin Pins, see four restored Felix the Cat cartoons from 1927–28. Watch Felix travel to the bottom of the sea to source a string of pearls (only to be chased out by the angry mother oyster); fashion a straw out of a yard of stolen pasta; attempt to court Juliet (as Romeow) while dueling another suitor and a group of musical animals; and, after a failed outing to the cinema, make his own movie, capturing some unintended mischief.

NOW SHOWING

Cabaret (35mm)
Screening and Opening Reception
Friday, November 13, 7:00 p.m

Bob Fosse rightly won the Best Director Oscar for his shattering musical set in Berlin on the eve of Hitler’s rise to power. Liza Minnelli and Joel Grey also scored Oscars for their unforgettable performances as, respectively, Sally Bowles, a vivacious but damaged American singer selling her soul in a seedy nightclub, and the devilish emcee who presides over it. Winner of eight Academy Awards.

Part of The Minnellis and Song & Dance, free for MoMI Members

The French Connection
Saturday, November 14, 4:00 p.m

“I felt this was a kind of crude poem to the city,” director William Friedkin said of this emotionally complex action masterpiece about an ethically dubious New York City cop, “Popeye” Doyle, who will do anything to take down a ring of foreign heroin smugglers. Winner of the 1971 Academy Award for Best Picture and Best Actor for Gene Hackman.

Part of James Gray's New York and New York Stories, free for MoMI Members

Some Came Running (35mm)
Saturday, November 14, 1:00 p.m.

Vincente Minnelli’s phenomenally directed and acted melodrama about rootlessness in post–World War II America stars Frank Sinatra as a war veteran and former writer who has fallen into alcoholism, and who returns to his Indiana hometown to find nothing is quite as he remembered it. Also starring Dean Martin and a heartbreaking, Oscar-nominated Shirley MacLaine.

Part of The Minnellis

Dr. Strangelove Or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb (35mm)
Saturday, November 14, 6:30 p.m

Dr. Strangelove, co-written by Stanley Kubrick and Terry Southern, strikes just the right manic, irreverent, sexually charged tone, creating a satirical masterpiece about the end of the world. Starring Peter Sellers in three roles, it is one of the seminal counterculture works of the 1960s. 

Part of Kubrick

The Long, Long Trailer (35mm)
Sunday, November 15, 12:30 p.m

Lucy and Desi and Minnelli? Talk about a dream team. In this hilarious mix of romantic comedy, slapstick, and suspense, Ball and Arnaz—at the time the two most popular performers in the nation—play Tacy and Nicky (of course), an engaged couple who purchase a trailer so they can journey the country and play at domesticity before tying the knot. Plus: see Lucy's red hair in Technicolor!

Part of The Minnellis

Hold Onto Me
Sunday, November 15, 3:00 p.m.

It’s summer, and plucky 11-year-old Iris is running wild through the streets with her friend Danae when she discovers that her estranged father, Aris, is back in town for his father’s funeral. Hold Onto Me is the first-ever Cyprus submission for the Best International Feature Oscar.

Part of Always on Sunday: Greek Film Series

The Cobweb (35mm)
Sunday, November 15, 4:00 p.m

Set in a psychiatric hospital, where the staff is likely more unsettled than the patients, this Cinemascope Minnelli melodrama details the fallout after the head doctor’s wife does the unthinkable: changes the color of the library’s drapes! The Cobweb is a gorgeously shot, low-boil soap opera featuring a wildly stacked cast: Lauren Bacall, Richard Widmark, Gloria Grahame, Lillian Gish, Charles Boyer, John Kerr, and Susan Strasberg!

Part of The Minnellis

The Year of Living Dangerously (35mm)
Saturday, November 21, 6:00 p.m.

Suspenseful, romantic, and intelligent, The Year of Living Dangerously predicted Peter Weir’s remarkable run in Hollywood, yet stands on its own as a singular 1980s entertainment that traffics in politically complex themes with nuance and depth. Starring Mel Gibson, Sigourney Weaver, and Linda Hunt in her Oscar-winning role.

Part of The Originals, free for MoMI Members

Gigi (35mm)
Sunday, November 22, 3:30 p.m.

The winner of nine Oscars (a record at the time), including Best Picture, Director, Cinematography, Art Direction, Costume Design, Song, and Score (by My Fair Lady composers Lerner and Loewe), Gigi is a triumph on every level, like a Toulouse-Lautrec painting sprung to giddy, widescreen life.

Part of The Minnellis
