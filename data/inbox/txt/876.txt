This Friday, Anthology celebrates more than 50 years of Troma Entertainment with a retrospective of the proudly low-budget, defiantly tasteless, and astonishingly prolific studio and distributor. This weekend, we’re joined by the People’s Poetry Gathering, founded in 1999 by City Lore and Poets House, with the Bowery Poetry Club joining soon after. Returning to Lower Manhattan for the first time in twenty years, the Gathering will include a film festival here at Anthology, celebrating cinematic explorations of poetry in NYC and beyond. The weekend’s screenings include Chuck Smith’s FUGS FILM!; and Alystyre Julian’s OUTRIDER, followed by a Q&A with Julian and the film’s subject, poet Anne Waldman.

THE TOXIC AVENGER

TROMA-GEDDON
October 23 – November 1
52 years young, and still going strong – in a landscape littered with the remains of countless failed film studios and distributors – the proudly low-budget, defiantly tasteless, and astonishingly prolific Troma Entertainment is a goddamn national treasure! Founded in 1974 by the indefatigable Lloyd Kaufman and Michael Herz, and with a catalogue that currently numbers more than 450 titles, Troma picked up the mantle from the likes of American International Pictures and Roger Corman’s New World Pictures, extending their fast-and-dirty approach to filmmaking into the era of home video and R-rated permissiveness, and creating a space where creativity, ingenuity, collaboration, and sheer lunatic energy were more important than the niceties of good taste. Over the years, Troma has produced and/or distributed hundreds of sublimely over-the-top movies, nurtured the talents of untold numbers of filmmakers, performers, and crew members, and made an indelible mark on American popular culture, thanks to the likes of the TOXIC AVENGER series, CLASS OF NUKE ‘EM HIGH (1986), SGT. KABUKIMAN NYPD (1990), CANNIBAL! THE MUSICAL (1993), and many other lesser-known but deliriously inventive and entertaining films.

Back in the year 2000, Anthology presented a 25th-anniversary tribute to Troma. That series was wholeheartedly endorsed by Jonas Mekas – godfather of experimental cinema and co-founder of Anthology – who recognized in Lloyd Kaufman and his upstart studio a filmmaker who – however populist his sensibility and filmmaking philosophy – was in the tradition of the gleefully transgressive, anti-bourgeois, and defiantly independent spirit of much of 1950s-70s underground cinema. Twenty-six years later, Anthology welcomes Lloyd Kaufman and his team back for a new celebration of their half-century of unrestrained, raucous cinematic shenanigans – a body of work that is, simultaneously and paradoxically, politically incorrect yet politically uncompromising (the Troma filmography is suffused with Kaufman’s deep-seated anti-corporate convictions).

Featuring recent restorations of Troma classics, rare screenings of deep cuts from the company’s back-catalogue, and appearances from cast, crew, and of course Kaufman himself, this series – while it may only scratch the surface of Kaufman and company’s output – contains enough comedy, gore, and cinematic resourcefulness to animate a whole year’s worth of programming. Come dive into the world of Troma – you’ll never be quite the same again.

Special thanks to Lloyd Kaufman, Michael Herz, and Sabrina Mendoza (Troma Entertainment); Bret Berg (American Genre Film Archive); and Jonathan Lees.

PEOPLE’S POETRY GATHERING FILM FESTIVAL
October 24 & 25
The People’s Poetry Gathering, founded as a biennial event in 1999 by City Lore and Poets House, with the Bowery Poetry Club joining soon after, assembles poets from New York City and beyond to pursue discourse, exchange, and cultural preservation through poetry. In 2026, as the U.S. marks the 250th anniversary of its founding, the Gathering returns to Lower Manhattan for the first time in twenty years. It will feature performances and workshops by an array of artists, including indigenous, folk, youth, and literary poets and musicians, as well as collaborations with iconic venues and restaurants, free writing workshops, and a POEMobile initiative that will visit all five boroughs projecting poems onto walls and buildings alongside live readings and music.

As part of the Festival, Anthology hosts a weekend of programs featuring films about poetry, including an encore screening of Alystyre Julian’s portrait of Anne Waldman, OUTRIDER (which we premiered in the spring of 2025); Chuck Smith’s FUGS FILM! (which has not been shown in NYC since its 2025 premiere at DOC NYC); Steven Zeitlin and Amanda Dargan’s IN THE MOMENT: WORLD POETRY DUELS AND IMPROVISATIONS; and Roland Legiardi-Laura’s AZUL (1988), a meditation on the legacy of Nicaraguan modernist poetry, shot on the battlefront with the Sandinista forces. Proceeding some of the films is AFTER BASHŌ, a series of animated poems by Bob Holman and Richard Levine.

These programs have been guest-programmed by Ram Devineni and Bob Holman, and are organized by and co-presented with City Lore and the Bowery Poetry Club. Special thanks to Joanna Kiernan and To Be Heard Foundation Inc.; Bob Holman (Bowery Poetry Club); Alystyre Julian; Richard Levine; Chuck Smith; Anne Waldman; and Steve Zeitlin & Sahar Muradi (City Lore).

Chuck Smith
FUGS FILM!
2025, 84 min, DCP
Formed in late 1964 by Ed Sanders and Tuli Kupferberg, alongside drummer Ken Weaver – anarchic East Village beat poets who could barely play their instruments – The Fugs are credited as the first true underground band. Their lo-fi folk music could be challenging and weird but they had a knack for catchy pop songs, such as “Supergirl” and ‘Slum Goddess”. One of the most crucial figures in countercultural history, Sanders founded the Peace Eye bookstore and the arts journal “F*** You: A Magazine of the Arts”, and, among other late-1960s antics, attempted to levitate the Pentagon and staged a funeral for flower power in San Francisco. Chuck Smith’s vital documentary features eye-popping archival footage and interviews with Sanders, Aline & R. Crumb, Lenny Kaye, Penny Arcade, Jeffrey Lewis, and many others, to tell the story of NYC’s original punks.
Sat, Oct 24 at 6:00. The screening will be followed by a Q&A with Penny Arcade, Steven Taylor and other participants from the film!

OUTRIDER

ALYSTYRE JULIAN & ANNE WALDMAN IN PERSON!
Alystyre Julian
OUTRIDER
2025, 100 min, DCP. With Anne Waldman. Producer: Sarah Riggs; Executive Producer: Martin Scorsese.
Poet/filmmaker Alystyre Julian’s OUTRIDER is a kinetic portal into fast-speaking poet Anne Waldman’s transcendent role as a visionary word-worker, from her “hearthome” in Greenwich Village and The Poetry Project at St. Mark’s Church, to the Jack Kerouac School of Disembodied Poetics at Naropa University in Boulder, Colorado, and outwards to Big Sur, Morocco, and Mexico. OUTRIDER is a portal to her path of imagination, her vow to poetry and activism, and her vortex of artists/collaborators: Patti Smith, Bob Dylan, Thurston Moore, Meredith Monk, Allen Ginsberg, Laurie Anderson, Douglas Dunn, Eileen Myles, Amiri Baraka, James Brandon Lewis, Cecilia Vicuña, Pat Steir, Ha-Yang Kim, Daniel Carter, Eleni Sikelianos, Bob Holman, Janice Lowe, No Land, Akilah Oliver, Lee Ann Brown, Brenda Coultas, and many others. Guided by ancestors of the Beat generation and poetic kinships with radical female musicians, OUTRIDER channels the ancient, bardic tradition – celebrating the thunderous power of poetry.

FILMMAKERS IN PERSON!
Steven Zeitlin & Amanda Dargan
IN THE MOMENT: WORLD POETRY DUELS AND IMPROVISATIONS
2022, 60 min, DCP
IN THE MOMENT features charismatic poets and musicians who are masters of the art of improvisation, dueling with one another and commenting on the meaning of these traditions and of life itself in improvised rhymes. The film introduces viewers to payadores, emboladores, decimistas, band bertsolari poets, as well as freestyle rap. The poets hail from Argentina, Canary Islands, Brazil, Portugal, Italy, Trinidad, the Basque region of Spain, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and the U.S.

Roland Legiardi-Laura
AZUL
1988, 104 min, DCP
AZUL is a documentary about the poetic soul of Nicaragua. Twenty-four poems interweave with interviews to create a narrative of Nicaragua’s poetry and the historical contexts and individuals who formed it. With poems from the creator of Modernismo, Rubén Darío, to Ernesto Cardenal, Carlos Rigby, and Gioconda Belli, to people reciting spontaneously in the street and soldiers workshopping poems on the frontlines, AZUL reveals a nation alive with the creative spirit and a profound belief in poetry as a crucible for change and expression. Originally filmed during the height of the Contra War, the film has been restored digitally.

ROSALYN DREXLER: WHO DOES SHE THINK SHE IS?
October 22
Rosalyn Drexler (1926-2025) was a category-defying creative force. Over the course of her multidecade career, she worked as a painter (creating some of the first examples of Pop art), a novelist, an Obie-award-winning playwright, a film critic, an occasional nightclub singer, and briefly, a professional wrestler.

The documentary WHO DOES SHE THINK SHE IS? (1975) provides a window into Drexler’s extraordinary creative universe. Set against the backdrop of the lost artistic demimonde of 20th-century New York, the film – directed by Patricia Lewis Jaffe and Gaby Rodgers – shows the artist rehearsing her madcap cabaret performances, cooking for her family in her art-strewn Manhattan apartment, and speaking candidly about her art, writing, and life. As Newsweek critic Jack Kroll remarks in the film, “[Drexler’s] essential insight is that people and things are completely insane, and she speaks from inside the whale.”

The documentary will be accompanied by never-before-seen home movies shot by Drexler and her husband Sherman in the 1940-50s, as well as a panel discussion. The screening coincides with a major exhibition of her work from the 1960s at Garth Greenan Gallery at 25 Greene Street; for more info about the exhibition visit the Garth Greenan Gallery website.

TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE

COMING SOON:
• LOST & FOUND: EL GUARDIÁN – U.S. PREMIERE!
October 28
Basilio is hired to watch over a remote beach on Mexico's Baja California coast, a seemingly idyllic stretch of shoreline where little appears to happen. But beneath the tranquil surface lies a community governed by its own codes, where illicit activities coexist with everyday life. Guided by an unwavering moral compass, Basilio is determined to do what he believes is right – only to discover that justice is far more elusive than he imagined. Set against a breathtaking coastal landscape, EL GUARDIÁN, the striking fiction debut by acclaimed documentary filmmaker Nuria Ibáñez (THE NAKED ROOM, A WILD STREAM), is a gripping and quietly unsettling meditation on ethics, belonging, and the fragile boundaries between duty and survival.

• BOOK RELEASE EVENT – TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE
November 1
This evening features a screening of Eduardo Coutinho’s TWENTY YEARS LATER: A MAN MARKED TO DIE (1984), introduced by the internationally acclaimed and Booker-shortlisted writer Itamar Vieira Junior. The evening also celebrates the English publication (by Verso) of Vieira Junior’s new novel, Saving the Fire, which presents the ghosts of a family’s past, lost amid the shadows of Brazil’s history. Vieira Junior masterfully blends the intimate journeys of his characters with faithfully rendered elements of Brazilian life in a world where the scars of colonialism still ache.

• BOOK RELEASE EVENT – RIM BATTAL + MUSTANG
November 2
To celebrate the publication of the English translation of writer Rim Battal’s autobiographical novel I’ll Look Myself in the Eyes, Anthology presents a special screening of MUSTANG (2015), by Turkish filmmaker Deniz Gamze Ergüven, a film that’s closely tied thematically to Battal’s text. In Battal's book, 17-year-old Rim is caught smoking out her window in her home in Marrakesh, Morocco. This minor transgression unleashes the fury of her mother, beginning a nuanced journey into the tangled web of a patriarchal society in which various generations of women seem to complicitly preserve the status quo. Ultimately, it is Rim who breaks the cycle of dysfunction and dares to be her true self. MUSTANG similarly explores the internalized psychological and moral impact of the patriarchy. As in Rim’s novel, we see how far families can go wrong in their perceived mission of protecting the purity of their girls. The screening will be followed by a bilingual reading from the novel by author Rim Battal and her translator Liza Tripp, along with a discussion and audience Q&A.
