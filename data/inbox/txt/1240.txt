THANK YOU FOR COMING TO THE
2026 TRIPLE CANOPY SYMPOSIUM

During a standup-esque talk at last weekend’s Triple Canopy Symposium, Morgan Bassichis asked if we all think like they do—about male bathing rituals, compacts with ghosts, and the erotics of necromancy. The answer was a resounding yes and no. Bassichis’s mix of relatability and outlandishness is an excellent illustration of what we’re after with our annual Symposium, which moves—sometimes seamlessly, sometimes suddenly—between the satisfaction of shared experience and the sense of disorientation that comes from an encounter with unfamiliar and unorthodox ideas. 

This year’s Symposium was heavy on both: Nile Harris enlisted the audience to read a hundred-line poem on a hundred dollar bills, Terre Thaemlitz’s delivered a polemic against spectacle in the form of an ambient piano concert, Jesus Hilario-Reyes and Benjamin Krusling communed with the sounds of emergency, and Sophie Lewis and Kalindi Vora probed the strange, regressive “technomisoginary” of tech oligarchs.
